## 7. Recommended angle and outline

Angle: **traveler-side, three-layer answer (law / platform / host) with a table, dated and linked to primary sources, honest about what is unsettled.** Avoid ESA letters and registries entirely, and say plainly why.

Outline (H2s with 40-60 word direct answers):

1. **Can an Airbnb host charge a pet fee for a service dog?**
   No. Airbnb's Accessibility Policy says hosts can't refuse a service animal, charge pet or animal-hair fees, or apply different house rules. Its pet-fee page says service animals "always stay for free." A host can still claim real damage through AirCover and the Resolution Center, as with any guest. (Source: Airbnb 1869, 3623, SEEN.)
2. **Can a Vrbo owner charge a pet fee for a service dog?**
   Vrbo's policy, as reported in search text, bars a pet fee or higher deposit for a service animal, while damage beyond normal wear can be billed. Verify on Vrbo's page before publishing, since it did not load for us. Individual owners' house rules may be stricter than the platform policy; cite the platform.
3. **What counts as a service animal, and what can a host ask?**
   Under the ADA, a service animal is a dog individually trained to do work or tasks for a person with a disability. A host may ask two questions: is the dog required because of a disability, and what task is it trained to do. No documentation, ID, or demonstration. (ada.gov SEEN.)
4. **Do emotional support animals get the same protection at a rental?**
   Often not. ESAs are not ADA service animals. On Airbnb, hosts may charge a pet fee or decline, except for Homes in California and New York and all stays in Quebec and Ontario. Vrbo's service animal policy does not cover ESAs (REPORTED). Local law may differ.
5. **Why did HUD's 2026 change matter? (short)** Explain the May 2026 rescission and that it does not change Airbnb's policy or state law (REPORTED + SEEN via Duane Morris). Keep to what sources state.
6. **Service animal vs ESA vs pet: decision table** (rows: animal type; columns: ADA, Airbnb, Vrbo, can host charge fee, can host refuse, documentation allowed). Mark Vrbo cells "verify".
7. **What if a host refuses or charges you anyway?** Steps: stay calm, screenshot listing and messages; cite the policy page; message through the platform; contact platform support (Airbnb exemption process exists for hosts with health risks; hosts must request it); report; local disability rights or state civil rights agency; ADA complaint route via ada.gov (verify the route before including). Keep as "options", not guarantees.
8. **Copy-paste template: message to a host before booking.** For service dog handlers: not required to disclose before booking on Airbnb (SEEN), but many choose to. Provide a short optional note, plus a refusal-response script citing the policy.
9. **What to bring so the stay goes smoothly** (checklist): leash/harness, housebroken, under control, off marked off-limits areas, not left alone without approval (Airbnb conduct rules, SEEN).
10. **Registries, certificates and ESA letters: what they are and aren't.** Short and honest; no affiliate links to letter sellers.

FAQ (6):
- Can a host ask for my service dog's paperwork? (No documentation under ADA and Airbnb, SEEN.)
- Can a host charge a cleaning fee for dog hair from a service dog? (No, ada.gov and Airbnb; SEEN.)
- Can a host refuse my service dog because of its breed? (ADA page: entities can't exclude based on breed stereotypes; SEEN. Also Vrbo policy applies regardless of breed restrictions, REPORTED.)
- Does a "no pets" rule apply to my service dog? (Airbnb: no; SEEN.)

## 7. Recommended angle and outline

Outline:

1. **Why do vacation rentals ban certain dog breeds?**
   Usually because the owner's property insurance or the rental manager's policy limits coverage for certain breeds, not because of your dog's behavior. Some owners also follow HOA rules or weight limits. Insurer practices vary by company and state, and some states limit breed-based underwriting. (adoptapet, Best Friends, SEEN; label as advocacy where relevant.)
2. **Does Airbnb or Vrbo ban pit bulls?**
   Airbnb's pet page covers pet count and fees, not breeds (SEEN); breed rules appear to be set by each host in listing rules (REPORTED, third-party). Always read the House Rules and message the host. Check Vrbo's own policy before claiming anything about it.
3. **How do I find vacation rentals that allow pit bulls?**
   Steps: use pet filters, then read the house rules for breed or weight language; search for "no breed restrictions" in descriptions; use the My Pit Bull is Family database as leads (it doesn't say if vacation rentals are included, so confirm by phone); prefer owner-managed listings and small local managers over large insurer-bound chains; book only after getting written confirmation.
4. **What should I ask the host before I book?** Checklist table: breed policy in writing; weight limit; number of dogs; pet fee and deposit; fenced yard; crate or alone-time rules; whether insurance is the reason; whether an exception is possible with proof.
5. **How do I write a polite request?** Template message (copy-paste) plus a short "proof pack": vet records, vaccination, spay/neuter, Canine Good Citizen or training certificates, a reference from a previous host or landlord, photos, a pet resume, willingness to pay a pet deposit, and honesty about breed (don't hide it).
6. **Table: why they say no, what it means, what to say.** Rows: insurance exclusion; HOA/building rule; weight limit; past damage; owner preference.
7. **What if my dog is a service animal?** One paragraph: ADA says no breed exclusion for service animals (SEEN). Link to Candidate 3's article if built. No coaching.
8. **What if the host says no?** Move on politely, ask for a referral to a pet-friendly sister property, check the host's insurance reason, use filters, consider a pet-friendly hotel (note one pet-travel blog names a hotel, undated: NOT CONFIRMED, don't include without verification).
9. **Why this keeps changing**: Brief on model act and state insurance laws, only with sources fetched.
