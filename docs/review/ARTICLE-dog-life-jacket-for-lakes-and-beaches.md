## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. **The Whole Dog Journal ranking on the first pick card claims more than our catalog records, and it is the first product a buyer sees.**
   Where: the EzyDog DFD pick card ("Whole Dog Journal, in a 2021 review of two test dogs, named it its all-around pick and says it earned top honors for buoyancy, comfort-minded features and affordability") and the eyebrow "Whole Dog Journal's all-around pick".
   What is wrong: the `ezydog-dfd-life-jacket` catalog record says only that Whole Dog Journal (July 2021, two test dogs, observational, buoyancy not measured) "gave the DFD its top paws" and that GearJunkie names it best for big dogs. "Top honors for buoyancy ... and affordability" is not in the record, and "affordability" is a price-quality claim the site must not make (no price claims). The card also leads with another outlet's ranking instead of the problem the jacket solves, which is inconsistent with the other two cards (job-first eyebrows) and puts the weakest-evidenced claim first.
   Fix: edits 1 and 2: reword the card to what the catalog supports, and change the eyebrow to the job (a bigger dog, or a rocky shoreline).

2. **Buying advice is thin where the buyer needs it: who needs a jacket at all, which dogs struggle, and who should skip each pick.**
   Where: the whole "Jackets to start with" section and the FAQ; the research record lists "do dogs need life jackets" and AKC's "which dogs struggle in water" as covered by competitors, yet the article never says it.
   What is wrong: a buyer arrives asking "does my dog need one and which jacket", but the guide never answers the first part (no sentence on dogs that tire, short-legged or deep-chested dogs, or the Coast Guard video's own point), and the three picks have no "skip it if" line. The article shows that the Float Coat says "choose the smaller size" between sizes and the Dawson recommends sizing up at the top of a range, then does not tell the buyer what to do when their dog sits between two ranges for a given pick. The Dawson card says "ask about return terms" but the catalog holds no return terms for it, while the DFD has 30-day returns; that asymmetry is not stated.
   Fix: edits 3 to 6.

3. **The opening assumes the reader already owns a jacket, the pool statistic pushes the answer down, and the buying help arrives at about 40% of the page.**
   Where: "You are on a lake dock or a rental's beach path with a dog who is already pulling toward the water, and a jacket you bought online is in the bag." and the next paragraph ("This guide is for open water and beaches. For a rental's pool or hot tub... in the 38 manager pages we read...").
   What is wrong: a reader who has not bought yet (the buyer the pin and title attract) reads two paragraphs about a jacket "in the bag" and a seven-of-38 pool statistic before the five bullets; on mobile the first bullet starts after about 1,000 px of header, banner, TOC and these two paragraphs. The picks section sits at about 6,550 px of 15,500, after the fit test and features table. That is right for owners, wrong for buyers.
   Fix: edits 7 and 8: make the opening cover both readers in one sentence, and move the pool pointer after the bullets.

4. **"Buying checklist", "What to buy" bullet and "What to buy first" repeat each other, and the Springland bottle dilutes the jacket recommendation.**
   Where: bullet "**What to buy:** a jacket sized by chest girth..." then the six-item "Buying checklist", then "What to buy first"; and the "Water hazards a jacket does not solve" paragraph that introduces the Springland 20oz Flip card.
   What is wrong: the same features list (handle, leash ring, bright trim, adjustable straps, measure by girth) appears three times within 2,000 px and again in "Features worth checking". The bottle card is reasonable as water-safety advice but its eyebrow "Fresh water on the dock" and the three-bullet-size lead make it the fourth card on a page about jackets; on a phone it sits between hazard bullets and the CDC line, and breaks the argument that a jacket does not fix hazards.
   Fix: edits 9 and 10.

5. **SEO: the page misses obvious long-tail intent and a plain size/fit decision for the common case.**
   Where: head and FAQ. The title tag "Dog Life Jacket Fit Test and Checklist" is accurate; the FAQ answers certification, measuring, fit check, supervision and beach access, but not "do dogs need life jackets", "what size life jacket does my dog need" as a size table, or "life jacket for a big dog / small dog".
   What is wrong: "do dogs need life jackets" is in `relatedKeywords` yet there is no heading or FAQ for it; "dog life jacket size chart" is in the keyword list but the article gives only one maker's outer girth range and tells the reader to open the other pages. Two of the three picks cannot be compared from the page.

## What works well
- Honest framing nobody else has: "no federal requirement or standard" quoted from the Coast Guard video (dated 2013), and a plain explanation that "bluesign approved" covers materials, not flotation, with the "unknowns" table spelling out what no source covers. The disagreement between Ruffwear ("choose the smaller size") and Outward Hound is the dek and the hook, and it is accurate against the catalog.
- The fit test is concrete and sourced: girth at the widest part of the rib cage, two fingers under the straps, tub then shallow water, signs of poor fit, and the lift check is labeled "our suggestion, not from a source".
- The Dawson card does not overstate: "a flotation aid for a conscious dog in calm waters" and "never to leave the dog unattended" match the catalog, and the Float Coat card says "no flotation rating stated". Hero and pin show a small dog in a teal jacket on a dock, which matches the topic and the pin title.
