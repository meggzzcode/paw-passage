## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. **Where:** H1 "Traveling With a Puppy to a Vacation Rental: Age Rules, Accidents and Deposits" (also the Pinterest pin title, which is printed on public/pins/puppy-at-a-vacation-rental.jpg). **What is wrong:** the title promises "Deposits", but the body has no deposit evidence at all. "Deposit" appears only inside the host message ("whether a deposit or card hold applies") and the checklist line "Damage, cleaning and deposit terms read". The only money facts are per-night pet fees and one non-refundable fee up to $1,000 (Resort Realty), which is a fee, not a deposit. A reader who came for deposits leaves without an answer, and the pin image repeats the promise. **Fix:** either (a) retitle to "Traveling With a Puppy to a Vacation Rental: Age Rules, Accidents and Fees" and make the same change in the pin CSV row and pin image, or (b) add a short, sourced "Fees, deposits and card holds" paragraph under the accidents section using only wording from the five cited pages (quote what, if anything, each page says about a deposit or hold, and say plainly where a page is silent). Option (a) is safer unless the pages really carry deposit wording; do not invent any.

2. **Where:** "## Buying checklist" (the second section, right after "The short answer"). **What is wrong:** the reader has not yet learned whether the puppy will be accepted, but is handed a shopping checklist (pads, crate, chew toy, "Check the current price") before the age rules, the pet-count question, the vaccination note and the accident-cost section. The article's own angle is "treat the puppy as a hidden rule to find before booking", and this section contradicts that order. It also repeats at "What to buy first" and again in the "puppy checklist", so the same gear is listed three times. **Fix:** move "Buying checklist" to just before the first pick card (immediately above "Accidents and the damage-and-cleaning risk" is where the first product appears), or delete it and keep only "What to buy first". In "The short answer", keep the closing line "Once the rental says yes, the gear is short: ..." as the only gear mention.

3. **Where:** pick card "Best washable | Petful names it 'Best washable' among puppy pads ..." (pet-parents-pawtect-pads). **What is wrong:** the "why we picked it" leans on a third-party ranking (petful.com) that is not in the article's Sources block (13 listed, none is Petful), so a doubting reader cannot see where the claim is documented and it reads like a ranking we cannot support. The pad is also never named in the prose; the first time the reader meets it is the card, with no sizing help (the catalog lists four sizes, Extra Small 18 x 24 in to Large 41 x 41 in, 2 per pack) and no "who should skip it" (a puppy that shreds or chews fabric, or a rental with no laundry). **Fix:** rewrite the card text to the maker facts only: "The maker lists machine-washable pads in four sizes with a waterproof backing and a floor-gripping backing, two per pack." Add the Petful link to the Sources block as a titled source only if you keep any mention of it. Add a prose sentence before the card naming the product and the skip rule (see edit 5).

4. **Where:** "## Crates and alone time" and the card "midwest-icrate-dog-crate". **What is wrong:** the article advises buying a crate "sized for the adult dog" with a divider, but never tells a buyer how to pick the size, never says the crate must fit through the rental's door or sit in the sleeping room, and gives no cheaper or no-crate alternative for people who are not crate-training. A buyer with a toy-breed puppy (the hero is a small terrier-type puppy) is told to buy for the adult dog without a way to tell which of the seven sizes (18 to 48 in) that is. Also the Petmate play yard card sits mid-argument in the accidents section, before the reader has any reason to want a pen. **Fix:** add one sentence to the crate section: "The maker's sizes run from 18 to 48 in; match the size to your dog's expected adult length and check it against your breed's adult size with your vet or breeder, then use the divider while the puppy is small." Move the Petmate play yard paragraph and card to just after the crate card (a new short sub-heading "If the yard is not fenced") so the accident section ends on the pad card and the two "containment" products sit together.

5. **Where:** "## What to buy first" and the page tail ("Keep reading" / "What next?"). **What is wrong:** the close is vague ("the crate and divider ... washable pads ... then one stuffable chew toy"). It does not name the picks or link them, and a buyer cannot click straight to the three cards. It also never mentions the play yard even though it is a pick card, and it does not point to the one adjacent guide a puppy owner most needs (how many dogs / pet fees are linked; the Airbnb angle is missing, see SEO). **Fix:** rewrite the first paragraph so each item names its product with a link to the same URL the card uses (MidWest iCrate, Pawtect Pads, KONG Classic) and add the play yard as "optional, only if the yard is unfenced and the host agrees". See edit 6.

## What works well
- The five verbatim manager quotes (Tahoe Getaways 18 months, A Blue Ridge Cabin Rental 18 months, Emmons Realty 1 year, Beach Realty and Resort Realty no puppies) are re-read, cited with footnotes, and the "5 of 38, convenience sample" framing is honest and not oversold. This is the differentiator no competitor page had.
- The host message and "What we could not confirm" table are practical: the reader leaves with a script to send and a clear statement that vaccine timing is for the vet.
- The researched-not-tested stance is clear without repetition, and the play yard and crate paragraphs state their gaps ("no units and no folded size", "reviewers report latch complaints, which are customer opinions"). The affiliate disclosure box sits above the first section and every Amazon button is rel="sponsored nofollow".
