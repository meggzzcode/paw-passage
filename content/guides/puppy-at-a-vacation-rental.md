## The short answer

Some rentals allow puppies and some do not, and the rule is usually one sentence on the manager's pet page. Two managers ask for 18 months, so a "pet friendly" listing can still exclude a 14-week-old dog. A fee will not change an age rule, so settle it before anything else.

Our sample is 38 managers' public pet pages, a convenience sample read 2026-11-18, not a count of the market. These five state a rule:

- [Tahoe Getaways](https://tahoegetaways.com/policies): "We do not allow dogs under the age of 18 months."
- A Blue Ridge Cabin Rental: "Each pet must be at least 18 months old and be fully house-trained." [[2]](https://ablueridgecabinrental.com/cabin-policies/)
- [Emmons Realty](https://www.emmonsrealty.com/pages/rental-policies): "Dogs must be at least 1 year old."
- Beach Realty & Construction (Outer Banks): "Puppies and certain breeds ... are not permitted in Beach Realty properties." [[4]](https://www.beachrealtync.com/node/707)
- [Resort Realty](https://resortrealty.com/outer-banks-pet-friendly) (Outer Banks): "no puppies are permitted."

Ask three things first: how old must the dog be, does a puppy count as a pet and a dog under the limit and the fee, and who pays if there is an accident. The [will my dog be accepted tool](/tools/will-my-dog-be-accepted) helps you line up those questions, and a message you can paste is [further down](#what-to-send-the-host).

## Age rules, in the managers' own words

The wording differs, and so does what it covers. Two managers use 18 months, Emmons Realty uses 1 year, and the two Outer Banks managers say puppies are not allowed without giving an age. Tahoe Getaways pairs its age line with "well-traveled, adult dogs," and Beach Realty describes its general rule as "two adult, house-trained dogs." [[1]](https://tahoegetaways.com/policies) [[4]](https://www.beachrealtync.com/node/707)

"House-trained" also appears as its own requirement at A Blue Ridge Cabin Rental, Beach Realty and Resort Realty ("housebroken"), which a young puppy may not meet even where no age is given. The other 33 managers did not state a minimum age on the pages we read. That is not a promise that they accept puppies; the page was silent, so you still need the answer in writing.

## Does a puppy count as a pet and as a dog?

Limits and fees are usually written per pet or per dog, and the five pages count animals, not ages. Tahoe Getaways says guests "may bring up to two well-traveled, adult dogs" with "$35/night pet fee per dog." A Blue Ridge Cabin Rental allows "a maximum of two (2) pets" and charges "a non-refundable pet fee of $150 per pet." [[1]](https://tahoegetaways.com/policies) [[2]](https://ablueridgecabinrental.com/cabin-policies/) None of the five quoted a lower rate or a separate limit for puppies.

Treat a puppy as a full dog for counting until the manager says otherwise. Ask whether the puppy uses one of the pet slots, whether the fee is charged at the full rate, and whether a second dog changes either answer. The mechanics are in [how pet fees work](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work) and [how many dogs you can bring](/guides/how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental). On Airbnb or another platform, message the host and ask the age question before you book; the [pet-friendly rental checklist](/guides/pet-friendly-vacation-rental-checklist) lists what else to confirm.

## Age and vaccination: what the official pages say

This guide gives no vaccine schedule. What the public-health pages we read say is short:

- The CDC's pet travel page says to make sure your pet's vaccinations are up to date and to "get a health certificate from your veterinarian dated within 10 days of your departure." [[7]](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/travel/index.html)
- The CDC Yellow Book says travelers should "contact their veterinarian to discuss travel safety and plans for their animals" and notes that travel conditions "may contribute to medical complications" in certain animals, naming very young animals among them. [[6]](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/family-travel/traveling-with-pets-and-service-animals.html)

Which shots your puppy has had, and whether it is ready for a long car ride, is a question for your vet. Bring the vaccination record in case the manager asks for it.

## What to send the host

Send this before you book, and keep the reply:

"Hello, we are considering [property] for [dates]. We would bring one puppy, about [age] months old. Is a dog of that age allowed, and does the puppy count as a dog for your pet limit and pet fee? Please confirm the total pet charge for our dates, whether it covers any damage or cleaning, whether a deposit or card hold applies, and whether the puppy may be left alone or must be crated. Thank you."

If the answer is no, you have lost an email, not a booking. If it is yes, you have a written answer to quote at checkout.

## Accidents and the damage-and-cleaning risk

A puppy that has not finished house training will have accidents in a new place. The ASPCA puts it plainly: "House training is not an exact science," and there is "no sure-fire formula or timetable" that works for every dog. [[8]](https://www.aspca.org/news/house-training-your-dog-or-puppy)

What decides whether an accident costs money is the manager's wording, not the dog:

- A Blue Ridge Cabin Rental says the tenant is responsible for "any damage caused by the pets," including floors, walls and furniture, and that "additional fees will be assessed" if pets necessitate deeper cleaning or repair. [[2]](https://ablueridgecabinrental.com/cabin-policies/)
- Tahoe Getaways warns that its "Damage Waiver does NOT cover accidental damage caused by your dog." [[1]](https://tahoegetaways.com/policies)
- Resort Realty's page says the pet fee is non-refundable, up to $1,000, and varies by property, but does not say whether it covers damage. [[5]](https://resortrealty.com/outer-banks-pet-friendly)

So a pet fee is not damage cover. None of the five pages described a separate puppy deposit or card hold, so ask about that in your message. For how charges after checkout are raised and disputed, read [Airbnb pet damage charge after checkout](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout).

To lower the odds, use the ASPCA's approach: limit the puppy to "one or two rooms where you are able to see her," take her out often, and if there is a mess, "just clean it up and soldier on," because "a dog will not learn anything by being scolded for a past accident." [[8]](https://www.aspca.org/news/house-training-your-dog-or-puppy) A bathroom or tiled kitchen is easier to wipe than a rug, and you can close a door or use a baby gate.

## Crates and alone time

A rental is not the place to start crate training. The ASPCA says the crate should be big enough for the dog "to stand up, turn around and lie down," and that young puppies "should not be crated for more than an hour or two" at a time, except at night. [[9]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/introducing-your-dog-crate)

That limit is the real constraint on your day. A restaurant dinner, a boat tour or a long hike may not fit around a puppy that needs a break every hour or two. If you need time away, plan for a sitter or a dog-friendly activity; [can you leave a dog alone in a vacation rental](/guides/can-you-leave-a-dog-alone-in-a-vacation-rental) covers the managers whose rules say crated or never alone.

If your puppy already sleeps in a crate at home, bring one. The ASPCA suggests buying a size that fits the adult dog and using a divider while the puppy is small, because a crate that is too large lets a dog use one end as a toilet. [[9]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/introducing-your-dog-crate) The MidWest iCrate comes in sizes 18, 22, 24, 30, 36, 42 and 48 in, includes a divider panel and a removable leak-proof plastic pan, has a slide-bolt latch, and folds flat for the car. [[11]](https://www.midwesthomes4pets.com/product/dogs/crates-carriers-kennels/icrate-dog-crate/) Match the size to your dog's expected adult length (your vet or breeder can give the range for your breed) and keep the divider in until the puppy grows into the space. The page gives no wire gauge, weights or folded dimensions, and some reviewers report latch and edge complaints, which are customer opinions, not specs, so test the latch before the trip. Skip it if you are not crate-training; use a gated room instead.

## Chewing

Chewing is normal: the ASPCA says "it's normal for puppies and dogs to chew on objects as they explore the world," and that teething typically ends by six months of age. [[10]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/destructive-chewing) In a rental the problem is that the chair leg and the remote belong to someone else.

The ASPCA's tips fit a short stay: give "plenty of his own toys and inedible chew bones," supervise and redirect to an approved chew, and when you cannot watch, use a confinement area with the things he should not chew removed. [[10]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/destructive-chewing) Before you let the puppy loose, scan at knee height for cords, shoes, throw pillows and plants. The [packing list tool](/tools/packing-list) has a place for the chews and blanket your dog knows.

A stuffable rubber toy is the usual approved chew. The KONG Classic comes in six size bands by dog weight, from XS (up to 5 lb) to XXL (85+ lb); the maker says to size up if between sizes, that the softer Puppy KONG is for puppies under 9 months, and that no toy is indestructible, with supervised use only. [[12]](https://www.kongcompany.com/kong-classic/) Stuff it while you are in the room, so the crate gets a good association (that use is our idea, not the maker's). Skip it for a dog that tears rubber apart.

## A first-night plan

1. Before unpacking, walk the puppy around the yard or the approved spot so the first toilet trip happens outside.
2. Choose the one or two rooms you will use and close the rest. Photograph the floors and furniture first, so you have a record of the condition at arrival.
3. Set up the crate, bed and water in the room where you will sleep, away from a front door or window that will set off barking.
4. Take the puppy out right before bed and again at the first sign of waking, and keep the routine from home.
5. Keep towels, manager-approved cleaner and waste bags within reach. Report an accident to the host promptly if the manager's policy asks for it.
