  - q: Is a lick mat safe to leave with my dog while I go out?
    a: Not by any source we read. The AKC says there is "no such thing as a 'leave-it-and-forget-it' dog toy" and that lick mats are best used with supervision, and KONG says to supervise a dog with any non-edible toy. Many rentals also restrict leaving a dog alone, so check the house rules first.

    a: Do not force it. Take the toy away, go back to the routine your dog knows and offer a walk or a quiet sniff outside. If the stress is more than a settling-in day, talk to your vet.

Bring one or two quiet, contained things: a lick mat, a sniffing game with treats rolled in a towel, or a stuffed rubber toy. Use them on a towel while you're in the room, and freeze runny fillings so they don't drip. Pick the size that fits your dog, and skip the toy entirely if your dog breaks off and swallows pieces. A toy helps a dog settle; it doesn't fix a dog that struggles when left alone.

## What to try first

The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/canine-diy-enrichment) groups enrichment into food puzzles, scent games and indoor play, and says "sniffing can be stimulating and calming." The mess ratings are our judgment, not test results, and your floor changes them: a towel on carpet and a tile kitchen are different problems.

| Activity | Mess (our judgment) | Noise | Stay in the room? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scent game with a few treats rolled in a towel | Low | Quiet | Yes |
| Settling on a mat after a walk | Lowest | Quiet | Nothing edible, so supervision is lighter |
| Short training session | Low | Quiet | Yes |
| Lick mat with a spread filling | Low to medium | Quiet | Yes |
| Snuffle or sniff mat with kibble | Medium (crumbs) | Quiet | Yes |
| Stuffed rubber toy, frozen | Medium (drips as it thaws) | Quiet, though it can skid on tile | Yes |
| Rigid treat-dispensing feeder | Medium (kibble scatters) | May be loud on hard floors | Yes |
| Chew bones and hard chews | High (grease, fragments) | Varies | Yes; not recommended in a rental |
| Raw or greasy fillings | High | Quiet | Skip in a rental |

The first three rows need no product: roll a few treats in a towel, do five minutes of cues your dog already knows, settle on the mat from home, or take a slow, sniffy walk where your dog sets the pace. For many dogs that's enough for a rental evening.

## What to pack

- The toy your dog already knows
- A mat or towel
- A small bottle brush (not the kitchen's)
- Dry food portions you already packed
- A sealable bag for used toys

If you plan to freeze a filled toy, ask the host whether the freezer has room. ## Rules that keep it safe

- **Stay in the room.** The [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/lick-mats-for-dogs/) puts it plainly: "There's no such thing as a 'leave-it-and-forget-it' dog toy." [KONG](https://www.kongcompany.com/faq/) says to supervise a dog with any non-edible toy. We found no source that supports leaving a dog alone with a particular toy in a rental, and many rentals restrict it; see [leaving a dog alone in a vacation rental](/guides/can-you-leave-a-dog-alone-in-a-vacation-rental).
- **Take it away if the dog tries to swallow it** (ASPCA). The [American College of Veterinary Surgeons](https://www.acvs.org/small-animal/gastrointestinal-foreign-bodies/) lists vomiting, loss of appetite, abdominal pain and diarrhea as signs of a gut blockage. If your dog swallows part of a toy or shows those signs, call a vet or an emergency clinic.
- **Check for xylitol.** The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/updated-safety-warning-xylitol-how-protect-your-pets) says xylitol can cause a sudden drop in blood sugar in dogs, with vomiting, weakness and sometimes seizures, and very large doses can cause liver failure. It names sugar-free peanut butter among the foods that may contain it. If your dog eats it, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) or, if your dog shows symptoms, go to a vet immediately with the packaging.
- **Count the filling as part of the day's food.** The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/destructive-chewing) suggests putting part of the daily food in a food toy; the AKC notes peanut butter can carry syrups and oils that add up.
- **Ask your vet** about allergies, a medical diet or a weight plan before you fill a toy.
- **Freeze runny fillings.** The AKC suggests freezing low-sodium broth on a mat, and KONG says freezing a stuffed KONG for a few hours makes it last longer.

## Which toy fits your dog?

Maker instructions are the best guide, but they're the makers' own claims, not independent tests. KONG says to size its Classic by weight and to size up if between sizes.

| Your dog | Likely fit | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gentle licker | Lick mat; towel scent game | Mat slipping on tile |
| Average chewer | Stuffed rubber toy sized by weight (KONG says the red Classic is for average chewers) | Cracks, missing pieces |
| Power chewer, or a dog that tears or swallows pieces | Ask the maker; consider skipping product enrichment (KONG says a power chewer may quickly damage a soft rubber toy) | Pieces coming off |

Hard chews like antlers and nylon bones are linked to tooth fractures ([Veterinary Practice News](https://www.veterinarypracticenews.com/durable-treats-dental-risks/)); skip them in a rental.

## Keep the rental clean

- Use a hard-floor spot, like a kitchen or entry, for anything with food, with a towel or mat under it. Keep food toys off beds, sofas and rugs.
- Photograph the floors and furniture at arrival, as in our [damage-charge guide](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout), and wipe drips as you go, not at checkout.
- Wash toys right after use with soap and warm water and your own brush (AKC, KONG). Dishwasher only if the maker says so (KONG says its rubber toys are top-rack safe; natural rubber mats are hand-wash) and the host agrees.
- Throw out worn toys: cracks, punctures or missing chunks (AKC, KONG). In a rental, bag it and take it with you.
- At checkout, wash and dry every toy, check under furniture for crumbs and hidden pieces, and tell the host promptly about anything stained or broken.

## Skip the toy if your dog

- bites off and swallows pieces, or has swallowed toys before;
- eats a lick mat rather than licking it;
- guards food or items, or reacts when you take them away: the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/destructive-chewing) says to ask a Certified Applied Animal Behaviorist or board-certified veterinary behaviorist;
- is a puppy that's still teething (see our [puppy at a rental guide](/guides/puppy-at-a-vacation-rental));
- has a diet limit you haven't cleared with your vet.

A toy is not a treatment for anxiety. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety) says a stuffed toy only works for mild cases and that highly anxious dogs usually won't eat when their people are away. If your dog pants, trembles, won't eat or can't settle, drop the product, stay with your dog and keep the routine from home. For ongoing distress or a dog that reacts to sounds, see our [reactive dog guide](/guides/vacation-rental-with-a-reactive-dog) and talk to your vet.
