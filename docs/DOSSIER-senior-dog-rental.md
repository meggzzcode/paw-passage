### Top fears
Dog slipping or falling on unfamiliar floors; stairs that cannot be avoided; heat in a parked or stopped car; the dog being "dazed" or disoriented afterward; being stuck far from a vet; accidents and being charged; having to refund-lose a booking (item 1).

### Outcomes (what actually happened)
Only a few threads record outcomes: item 3 (trip rescheduled to a cooler day), item 2 (pet cabin booked, long sniff walks worked), item 5 (dog tired but fine). Items 1 and 6 end without an outcome. No example found of a host refusing or charging because of a senior dog's accident.

### Misconceptions or contradictions in what people say
- Many replies say a short wait in a parked car is fine if not hot (item 4, item 3). ASPCA's 2017 page says never leave a pet in a parked car (SEEN). Present both; the ASPCA rule is the safer position for an older dog who overheats easily.
- "Just carry the dog up" (item 1) is a reply from one person, not a vet recommendation. Dog Aging Project says ramps and stairs, and Cornell lists stair difficulty as an OA sign; carrying a large dog is a separate safety question for the person. NOT CONFIRMED by any vet source I read.

### Scripts or documents that worked
Nothing in the sample records a vet letter, "pet resume" or insurance proof working for a senior dog. REPORTED/SEEN items that exist: messaging hosts beforehand (item 2), a phone log of appetite/water/bathroom/mood (item 8), carrying copies of records (ASPCA, VCA below).

### Surprising stories
- Item 1: the problem was created at booking (no elevator, six flights, no refund) and the only reply options were a kennel, carrying, or paying extra for another unit.
- Item 6: the owner's fear was not the trip itself but the dog being "dazed" for days after.
- Item 8/9: the best-documented owner tactic is not gear but pacing: shorter days, shade, a stroller or a travel log.

## 3. Hard facts from primary documents

### Mobility, floors, ramps, beds (vet or vet-linked sources, SEEN)
- Dog Aging Project, "Mobility Tips and Tricks for Senior Dogs," 18 Mar 2021, author Kellyn McNulty, DVM (https://dogagingproject.org/mobility-tips-and-tricks-for-senior-dogs). SEEN.
  - Limit access to slippery hard floors "limit your older dog's access to these areas of the house."
  - Rugs or mats where the dog lies down or sleeps and high-traffic areas; "Be present when your dog first uses these rugs or mats."
  - Short nails help paw-pad traction: "this is essential for ensuring a dog can properly use their own paw pads for traction." Overgrown nails: tip-only trims "every 1-2 week" for months, then every three to four weeks.
  - Toe grips: "Be careful using these products if your dog is a chewer." Booties/socks with rubber soles can help; DIY options described.
  - Ramps need "very good gripping material"; train with positive reinforcement. Team "cannot ethically offer medical advice"; talk to your vet.
- Banfield, "Senior-proof your home," updated 8 Jul 2026, medically reviewed by two DVMs (https://www.banfield.com/Senior-hub/care-for-older-pets/senior-proof-your-home). SEEN.
  - "non-slip surfaces like rugs or yoga mats"; orthopedic bed with low profile, washable cover, non-slip bottom; ramps need "the right incline" and traction; elevated or tilted bowls; for stair trouble, move belongings to ground level.
- Banfield osteoarthritis page, updated 8 Jul 2026 (https://www.banfield.com/Senior-hub/common-health-issues-in-senior-pets/osteoarthritis-in-dogs-cats). SEEN. Signs: stiffness, trouble rising, hesitation on stairs, difficulty getting onto or off elevated surfaces. Warning: "don't dismiss it as just old age." VET if signs appear.
- Cornell Riney Canine Health Center, "Osteoarthritis," updated 9 Dec 2025 (https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/osteoarthritis). SEEN. Signs include "Difficulty navigating stairs or jumping on couches." Home: "non-slip rugs and ramps to get on beds, couches or cars." Low-impact activity like leash walks or swimming. Long-term pain medication needs "routine monitoring with blood work." The page names drug classes; the article should not. VET decides.
- AKC "Making home accessible for senior dog," updated 31 Mar 2026, AKC Staff, no reviewer named (SEEN, via SERP-3 and re-read). Grip aids: boots, socks, nail grips; non-slip rugs or yoga mats; stair treads "deep steps and an anti-skid surface" with rounded edges and a weight rating; gate off stairs; ramp "gentle slope and non-slip surface"; lightweight ramps suit travel; orthopedic beds of polyurethane or memory foam. Affiliate context.
- AKC "Dog stairs and ramps," updated 2 Mar 2026 (https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/general-health/dog-stairs-and-ramps/). SEEN. Match height to "sofa, bed or trunk surface"; check weight limit; non-slip surface; fold-away adjustable-angle portable ramp for car use. It gives no measurements for slope or width. Unnamed reviewer. Slope figures like "10-18 degrees" appear only in a blog summary (REPORTED); do not use.

### Cognitive decline (SEEN)
- Dog Aging Project, "Understanding Behavioral Changes in Senior Dogs," 25 Nov 2020, Gray Barnett, DVM. Signs listed under disorientation, interactions, sleep-wake, housesoiling, activity, anxiety, learning/memory. Keeps "a consistent daily routine, since older dogs adjust to change less easily." Pain, dental disease, sensory loss and other illness "can all cause behavior changes," so see a vet first. States AAHA recommends wellness exams twice yearly for seniors (second-hand to AAHA).
- AKC "Recognizing behavioral changes in senior dogs," updated 14 May 2018. Quote: it is "imperative to get a full and accurate evaluation by your veterinarian." Signs include night pacing and getting stuck in corners.
- ASPCA "Behavior problems in older dogs," undated (footer 2026). Signs (confusion, changes in responsiveness, activity, sleep-wake, house training); "Be sure to report all changes you see to your dog's veterinarian"; adjust for more frequent bathroom trips; crating can raise anxiety if not used to it. Lists a drug by name; do not repeat.
- Travel-specific cognitive claims (new place disorients, 4-6 weeks pre-trip exam, dim night light) come only from a templated blog via search summary (pawventures.pages.dev, REPORTED). Do not state as fact. The general principle (routine matters, changes may be harder) is supported by the Dog Aging Project quote above.
- AAHA 2023 Senior Care Guidelines: pages returned 403. REPORTED from search text only: seniors defined as "the last 25% of estimated lifespan"; screening questionnaires for cognitive dysfunction. NOT CONFIRMED on the page.

### Travel records, ID, vet at destination (SEEN unless noted)
- ASPCA "Traveling With Your Pets: Dos and Don'ts," 9 Aug 2017 (https://www.aspca.org/news/traveling-your-pets-dos-and-donts). Harness seatbelt or crate; "Never leave your pet in a parked car"; ID on collar plus microchip; "take a copy of his medical records" if history is complicated; check for an interstate health certificate; save "a local veterinary emergency hospital at your destination" and the Animal Poison Control number (888-426-4435) in your phone; check for dropped pills left by previous guests. No rest-stop guidance.
- VCA "Traveling with your pet," last updated 18 Oct 2023, Dagner and Buzhardt, DVMs. Carry medical records; pack medication and food "with extra in case your return is delayed"; collar with ID tag even if microchipped; a dog who cannot manage stairs may need a ground-floor room; talk to your vet about anxiety medication.
- AVMA travel page: fetch returned empty. REPORTED from a search summary only: a Certificate of Veterinary Inspection is generally issued within 30 days for ground travel and 10 days for air; some airlines stricter. NOT CONFIRMED. Interstate requirements vary; ask the destination state or your vet.

### Airbnb accessibility features (SEEN, but written for human guests)
- Airbnb help article 3138 lists filters including "Step-free access," "Step-free bedroom access," "Bedroom entrance wider than 32 inches," and an experience filter "Mainly flat or leveled ground." If a listing lacks information, "contact the host" (paraphrase). Undated.
- Airbnb host guide to photographing accessibility features (published 22 Sep 2020, updated 2 Dec 2024). Step-free entrance means no steps, stairs or curbs over 2 inches (5 cm) along the route; photos every 10 feet with the camera tilted toward the ground, open entrance from 8 feet; thresholds no higher than 2 inches with close-up and 5-foot photos from inside and outside. Doorways at least 32 inches wide with a tape measure shown. No flooring or bedroom photo standard.
- REPORTED (search text): Airbnb agents check a photo for each accessibility feature a host ticks before it shows. Not read on a page.
- Caveat: these filters are built for human guests. Whether they describe a dog's access is the writer's inference. Step-free access to the entrance is relevant to a dog; "mainly flat ground" is only an experience filter.

### Contradictions to flag
- Parked car: ASPCA (never) vs forum replies (short and cool is fine).
- Pre-trip timing: blogs say 4-6 weeks or within 10 days with no source; no vet authority I could read gave a number. Leave out.
- Stairs: Dog Aging Project and Banfield recommend ramps and stairs for furniture; none says a ramp works for a full indoor flight. PetPlan (UK, search summary, REPORTED) says ramps are rarely practical for a full flight and recommends gates.

## 7. COMPILED FINDINGS FOR THE WRITER

### Ten most useful, non-obvious insights
1. The one story with a clear failure (item 1) came from booking a 3rd-floor, six-flight walk-up before checking; the cure is asking before booking, not after.
2. Airbnb has a published photo standard for accessibility features (entrance route, thresholds at or under 2 inches, doorways at least 32 inches). A reader can ask a host for equivalent photos. The standard is for people, so say so.
3. Brindley's fee explicitly does not cover "unreported accidents": for a dog with accidents, report promptly.
4. Rental rules like "no furniture unless covered" and "not unattended" can clash with an old dog needing a ramp to a bed or having anxiety alone; ask in writing.
5. Weight and count limits (60 lb at Brindley; 2 dogs at five of six managers) matter for older large dogs; Resort Realty allows "adult" dogs only, which seniors satisfy.
6. Every vet source recommends the same four portable fixes: non-slip rugs or yoga mats, short nails, grip aids, and ramps with traction. Dog Aging Project adds: be present the first time a rug is used.
7. Ask whether stairs are carpeted or bare wood, and whether a gate can be placed (AKC suggests gating stairs).
8. Pacing matters more than gear in owner stories: ground-floor booking, shade, shorter days, a symptom log.
9. A "go, ask your vet, or stay home" decision is the real question (items 1, 3, 6). Forum advice splits; do not promise an answer.
10. The parked-car conflict (ASPCA vs forum advice) is a place we can be clear without medical advice: prefer arrangements that avoid leaving the dog alone.

### Five things that will make this better than anything ranking
1. A host-message script plus a photo request list (entrance route, stairs, bedroom path, flooring, yard slope), tied to Airbnb's own photo standard.
2. A "rental fit scorecard" with questions and why each matters, citing Cornell, Banfield, Dog Aging Project.
3. A table of real manager pet rules (section 4) with the senior-specific implications.
4. A "when to ask the vet" box built from Cornell OA signs and Dog Aging Project/AKC/ASPCA cognitive signs; no dosing.
5. A packing and records checklist citing ASPCA, VCA (and AVMA once confirmed).

### Solid enough to state
- Non-slip rugs or yoga mats, short nails, grip aids, and ramps/stairs with traction help older dogs on slippery floors (DAP 2021, Banfield 2026, Cornell 2025, AKC 2026).
- Orthopedic bed features: supportive foam, low profile, non-slip base, washable cover (Banfield, AKC).
- Stair difficulty and trouble getting on furniture are listed osteoarthritis signs; do not dismiss as "just old age" (Banfield, Cornell).
- Behavior changes should be reported to a vet before assuming cognitive decline (DAP, AKC, ASPCA).
- Carry records and medications, wear ID plus microchip, save the destination emergency hospital (ASPCA, VCA).
- Real manager rules in section 4, dated to the day read.

### Hedge
- Stops every 1 to 2 hours: forum and blog only.
- Ramp slope angles: no authoritative number.
- Whether Airbnb step-free filters mean "easy for a dog": inference.
- Heat and cold thresholds for seniors: I found none from a vet source; cite none.
- Parked car for brief stops: ASPCA says never; say it is the safer line.
- AVMA CVI windows (30/10 days): REPORTED only.

### Leave out
- Any drug, supplement or dose names; sedation advice.
- Fee averages and "typical weight limit" statistics (no sources).
- Pre-trip exam timing (4-6 weeks, 10 days), age cutoffs, "X hours max in the car".
- Claims about hosts' legal duties or deposits on Airbnb.
- Claims about Reddit.
