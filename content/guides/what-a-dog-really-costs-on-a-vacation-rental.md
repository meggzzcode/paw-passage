How much does a dog add to a vacation rental bill? In the 38 managers whose pages we read (a convenience sample, not a survey), nightly pet fees run from $20 to $50 and flat or per-pet fees from $50 to $400, with one manager saying "up to $1,000", and some charge $0. Cleaning, deposits, service fees and taxes come on top, so the total for your dates is the only number that counts.

You found two rentals, one lists a $0 pet fee and the other asks for $40 a night, and the cheaper-looking one is not obviously cheaper. The best way to choose is to compare what you will actually pay for the same dates and the same dog.

This guide is about the total, and what to do with it. How each fee type works is in our [pet fees guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work), so we skip those tables here.

## What does a dog add to a vacation rental bill?

Add up four things, and keep them in separate piles:

- **Definite cost:** the nightly rate, the cleaning fee, the pet charge, the platform service fee if there is one, and taxes.
- **Refundable cash:** a deposit that comes back unless there is a claim. You need it up front, but it is not a cost.
- **Contingent charges:** damage, extra cleaning, or a penalty for an unapproved dog. You do not budget these, but you do avoid them.
- **Everything outside the rental bill:** fuel, gear, boarding. We give no figures for these because we have no official source for them; list your own.

Then compare quotes on the first pile only, and check the second pile against your card limit. The method below shows the arithmetic.

## What the pet fee does and does not cover

On Airbnb, the pet fee is meant for expected cleaning after a pet stay, and it cannot be more than the nightly base rate. For unexpected damage, Airbnb's page points to its AirCover protection. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623) That fee is a separate line from the cleaning fee: Airbnb's pricing page says a pet charge "may be separate from cleaning fees." [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/125)

Managers write it the same way. Tahoe Vacation Rentals says its pet fee "does not include damage or additional cleaning caused by the pet." [[9]](https://www.tahoevacationrentals.com/reservation-information/) So the pet fee is a charge for being allowed in, not insurance, and it can sit beside a cleaning fee, a deposit or a waiver.

## The pet fee line, in our dataset

We read the public pet pages of 38 vacation rental managers (a convenience sample, not a survey, read 2026-11-18). Twenty-five state a fee: 22 charge an amount and three say $0 (Sliding Rock Cabins, Twiddy & Company and Hapey Cabin Rentals). The other 13 give no amount. The tables are on the [pet policy dataset page](/data/pet-policies).

Among the 22 that charge, nightly amounts run from $20 to $50 and flat or per-pet amounts from $50 to $400 (the $400 is one manager's rate for stays of 14 or more days), with one manager saying "up to $1,000". A few named examples, each re-read on its live page on 2026-11-18:

## The rest of the bill

### Cleaning fee

Airbnb says some hosts charge a cleaning fee to cover cleaning after your stay. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/125) Its host page says the cleaning fee is shown to guests separately unless a total-price display is turned on. [[3]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2385) Vrbo's fee help lists pets and cleaning as separate standard fee types. [[5]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-manage-my-fees)

A manager can charge both. Cabin Rentals of Georgia lists "$50/pet/night" plus a "$75 pet cleaning fee", which the page does not label per night, so we read it as charged once. [[7]](https://cabin-rentals-of-georgia.com/policies) A rental with a low pet line and a high cleaning fee can cost the same as one with the reverse, so compare the sum.

### Deposits

A refundable deposit is money you hand over and may get back. Vrbo's payment page describes a "damage deposit amount set by" the host that is not part of the host's payout and is returned to the guest unless the host claims damage. [[6]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-read-my-payment-details) Cabin Rentals of Georgia lists "a refundable $250 pet deposit," returned at departure if there is no pet damage or excessive pet hair. [[7]](https://cabin-rentals-of-georgia.com/policies)

Count a deposit as a cash need, and ask who holds it and when it is released. Some managers use a nonrefundable waiver instead: Tahoe Vacation Rentals says its waiver covers up to $1,000 of accidental damage "in lieu of a security deposit." [[9]](https://www.tahoevacationrentals.com/reservation-information/) That one is a cost, not a deposit, even though it replaces one.

### Service fees

Airbnb says it may add a guest service fee depending on the listing, and where a host uses the single fee structure no guest service fee is charged. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/125) Vrbo says its displayed price includes mandatory fees and service fees. [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Price-Display) Its payment page describes the guest service fee as paid directly to Vrbo when booking. [[6]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-read-my-payment-details)

### Taxes

Airbnb says local taxes depend on where the listing is and may not be in the shown price until checkout. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/125) Vrbo says its displayed price does not include taxes, which are added at checkout. [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Price-Display) A manager's own page can add tax to its fees: Tahoe Vacation Rentals quotes "$75 plus tax per pet", so the pet line is more than $75 there. [[9]](https://www.tahoevacationrentals.com/reservation-information/)

On both platforms, use the price breakdown before you pay. Airbnb points to the Price breakdown option on checkout and the receipt after booking. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/125) Vrbo says travelers see a full breakdown including taxes before confirming. [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Price-Display)

### Optional extras

Vrbo's pricing page says optional fees, such as recreation fees, are not in the displayed price, and hosts can describe them in the property description. [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Price-Display) Read the description for anything that is not in the total.

## Three quotes, side by side (hypothetical)

These numbers are invented, round, and illustrative. They are not prices from any listing. One dog, five nights, same dates, taxes left out of both so the comparison is like for like.

| Line | Rental A | Rental B | Rental C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nightly rate x 5 | $200 x 5 = $1,000 | $225 x 5 = $1,125 | $210 x 5 = $1,050 |
| Cleaning fee | $100 | $125 | $100 |
| Pet charge | $40 per night = $200 | $0 | $150 per stay |
| **Definite cost** | **$1,300** | **$1,250** | **$1,300** |
| Refundable deposit | none | none | $250 |
| **Cash needed up front** | **$1,300** | **$1,250** | **$1,550** |

Rental B, the "no pet fee" one, is cheapest. Rental A and C tie on cost, but C asks for $250 more up front, which matters if the deposit is a card hold or a collected payment. Now add a second dog, assuming A charges per pet and C's flat fee covers both dogs: A rises to $1,500 and C stays at $1,300, while B stays at $1,250. The ranking can flip when the dog count changes, which is why you enter your real count.

The same method works for the real fees above. For example, at the Cabin Rentals of Georgia terms, one dog for five nights would be $50 x 5 = $250 plus the $75 cleaning fee, so $325 of definite pet cost and a further $250 refundable deposit, if the $50 is per pet per night as the page states and the $75 is charged once. [[7]](https://cabin-rentals-of-georgia.com/policies) That is before the nightly rate and taxes, and before checking the page's other conditions (a weight limit, a crate requirement and manager approval of each dog).

## When the pet fee is not the story

A $0 pet fee is not a promise of a cheaper trip. Twiddy lists ten homes with no pet fee or deposit, nine of them in the words "no additional fee or deposit" [[13]](https://blog.twiddy.com/bring-your-pets-on-vacation/), but the nightly rate and cleaning fee still apply, and only your dates will show the total. Compare on the total, then on the rules: a rental that is $50 cheaper and caps dogs below yours is not an option (see [which managers state a weight limit](/guides/vacation-rentals-with-no-dog-weight-limit)). Our [no pet fee rentals guide](/guides/vacation-rentals-with-no-pet-fee) goes through what the $0 managers say, and the [dog count guide](/guides/how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental) covers limits.

## Charges you avoid by asking first

Two bills are not part of the plan, and both are avoidable.

- **Damage and extra cleaning after checkout.** A pet fee does not stand in for a damage claim. If a charge appears afterwards, [what to do about an Airbnb pet damage charge after checkout](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout) walks through asking for an itemized explanation.
- **A dog the booking did not approve.** Cabin Rentals of Georgia says all pet fees are doubled if an unapproved dog is found. [[7]](https://cabin-rentals-of-georgia.com/policies) Tahoe Vacation Rentals lists a $300 nonrefundable per-pet charge and immediate departure for a pet in a non-pet-friendly home. [[9]](https://www.tahoevacationrentals.com/reservation-information/)

None of it changes what a host or platform may charge, and none replaces photos at arrival and checkout. We hold no price data, so check current prices on each listing. If you pack only one thing, match it to your dog: the mat if your entry is tile or wood and walks are muddy, the towel if your dog comes in wet, the bed if the host limits furniture. ## How to compare two quotes

1. Fix the inputs: same dates, same number of guests, same number of dogs.
2. For each rental, open the price breakdown (Airbnb) or the full breakdown before confirming (Vrbo) and write down nightly rate, cleaning, pet charge, service fee and taxes.
3. Add them into one definite-cost number.
4. Write the refundable deposit or hold on a separate line, and check what your card limit can carry.
5. Ask the host the one question the page leaves open, using the message in our [pet fees guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work).
6. Only then look at the rules: dog count, weight, breed, and whether your dog is accepted at all. The [will-my-dog-be-accepted tool](/tools/will-my-dog-be-accepted) helps with that.

If a listing allows several dogs, the [dog count guide](/guides/how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental) covers how limits and per-pet fees interact.
