- Old dek: Veterinary sources and pet retailers give different cold thresholds, and the maker pages give no temperature ratings. Here is how to decide whether your dog needs a coat or boots, size them, and keep a rental clean.
- New dek: Boots matter on salted or icy paths; a fleece matters for small, thin-coated, old or sick dogs on dry walks. Here is how to size both.
- Old first paragraph: Does your dog need a dog coat or boots at a winter cabin? **Short answer: boots matter when the path is salted or icy; a coat or fleece matters for small, thin-coated, old or sick dogs on dry, cold walks.** A thick double-coated dog that mostly needs paw care can skip the coat, and any dog on clear, dry paths can skip the boots.

A thick double-coated dog on clear, dry paths can skip both, and neither the sources nor the maker pages we read give a safe temperature for your dog, so you judge by the dog.

The dog hiking gear that matters is a fixed leash of six feet or less for every trail, then a handle harness, boots or a clip-on light only if your trail calls for them.
