You have found two cabins you like. One is on Airbnb, one is on Vrbo, both say pets are welcome, and the nightly prices are close. Before you pick, it helps to know what each platform actually says about dogs, and what only the host can tell you.

This guide sticks to what the platforms' own help pages say and marks what we could not confirm. General information only; rules vary by place and host.

## The short answer

- We found no platform-wide rule that makes Airbnb or Vrbo better for dogs. Pet terms are set listing by listing, so compare the two specific places.
- Airbnb says hosts can charge a pet fee per booking, per night, per pet or per pet per night, shown in the nightly rate and in the checkout total. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623)
- Vrbo says displayed prices include mandatory fees, and its fee help lists pets among standard fee categories. [[3]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Price-Display) [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-manage-my-fees) We could not confirm that every listing's displayed total includes the pet charge.
- Service animals are not pets on either platform's help pages, and the ADA says fees for pets must be waived for service animals. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623) [[5]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-is-HomeAway-s-service-animal-policy) [[6]](https://www.ada.gov/resources/service-animals-2010-requirements/)
- The fair test is the complete total for the same dates, guests and dogs, confirmed by the host in writing. Run the numbers below.

## How do you find pet-friendly places on each platform?

On Airbnb, use Filters, then Booking options, then Allows pets. You can also add the number of pets when you add guests, and the listing's pet policy is in its House rules section. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/86)

On Vrbo, we did not find a help-center page we could read that explains the pet filter step by step, so we will not describe its exact wording. What we did read is Vrbo's house rules page, which tells hosts to say whether pets can come, how many, what type, and any size restriction, and notes that a pets filter is among its most popular. [[7]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-House-Rules)

Either way, treat a filter as a starting point. Airbnb itself says to tell the host about your pets even when pets are allowed, and notes that a host may set limits such as no pets on beds or sofas. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/86)

## How are pet fees structured and shown?

Airbnb: hosts choose per booking, per night, per pet or per pet per night. A single flat fee can cover more than one pet, and Airbnb's page says it cannot be more than the nightly base rate. Guests say how many pets they bring (0 to 5), and the fee shows in the nightly rate and in the checkout total. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623)

Vrbo: pets, cleaning and extra guests are standard fee categories, and hosts are told to enter charges in the fee settings rather than only in listing text. [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-manage-my-fees) Displayed prices include mandatory fees and service fees, while taxes are added at checkout. [[3]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Price-Display) The pages do not say how a given listing's pet charge is configured or displayed, so a displayed price is not proof of a complete pet total.

One example from our team, labelled as one listing: in November 2026, on one Blue Ridge, Georgia Airbnb cabin for December 11 to 13 with two adults, adding one dog raised the request-to-book total from $519.11 to $576.61. That was $50 more on the nightly line plus $7.50 tax, and the breakdown showed no separate pet-fee line. We did not run the same test on Vrbo, so we cannot say how a Vrbo listing would look.

For more on the four fee units and deposits, see our [pet fees guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work).

## How many dogs can you bring, and what if you add one later?

Direct answer: both platforms let hosts set pet terms, so the allowed number is a listing question. Airbnb asks you to enter a pet count and says hosts can review it. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623) Vrbo tells hosts to state how many pets are allowed. [[7]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-House-Rules)

If you decide to add a dog after booking, we did not find a help page we could read that explains how either platform changes the price. Ask the host to confirm the change and the new total in the message thread before you rely on it. Vrbo's fee help does say a host can send a payment request to add a charge to an existing reservation, which describes a mechanism rather than proof that the charge is valid. [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-manage-my-fees) Two or more dogs raise their own questions; see our [two-dog guide](/guides/traveling-with-two-dogs-accommodation-guide) and [large-dog guide](/guides/vacation-rentals-that-welcome-large-dogs).

## What about service animals?

Direct answer: service animals are not pets. Airbnb says service animals always stay for free, and Vrbo says its policy applies regardless of a property's normal pet policy because service animals are not classified as pets. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623) [[5]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-is-HomeAway-s-service-animal-policy)

The Department of Justice's ADA page says a business that requires a deposit or fee from patrons with pets must waive it for service animals. [[6]](https://www.ada.gov/resources/service-animals-2010-requirements/) Airbnb adds that damage by a service animal can still be claimed through its process. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623) We did not read platform text on emotional support animals, so check the current policy and do not describe a pet as a service animal to avoid a fee.

## How do you compare the real total for two listings?

Direct answer: put the same dates, the same guests and the same number of dogs into both listings, open the full price breakdown on each, and ask each host to confirm the pet charge in writing. Only then compare. Cheap nightly rates can hide a larger total.

1. Enter identical dates, adults, children and dog count.
2. Open the final breakdown and note nightly rate, cleaning, service fees, pet charge and taxes.
3. Check for any deposit, hold or damage product separate from the fee.
4. Read the house rules for size, breed, count, furniture and being left alone.
5. Compare cancellation terms for each booking.

Then run the numbers below, or use the [pet cost tool](/tools/pet-cost-check) on its own, to line up both totals and draft your host message. Our [rental checklist](/guides/pet-friendly-vacation-rental-checklist) covers what to look for in the listing itself.

## Which should you choose?

We cannot verify a general winner, so use these as questions rather than verdicts.

- Does the listing state a pet count, size or breed limit that fits your dog? If not, ask before booking.
- Does the host confirm the complete pet charge for your dates in writing? A host who does gives you a firmer number.
- Does the total with taxes and the dog beat the other listing, not just the nightly rate?
- Is the cancellation policy acceptable if your plans change?
- Do you have a service animal? Then the pet-fee rules above apply to you differently on both platforms.

If you are on the fence about a place that says no pets, see our guide on [bringing a dog to a no-pets rental](/guides/bringing-a-dog-to-a-no-pets-rental).

## What to send the host before you book

“We are traveling with [number] dog(s) for [number] nights, [dates]. Please confirm the complete pet-related charge for this reservation, whether it is per booking, per night, per pet or per pet per night, and whether it is already in the displayed total. Please also tell us the maximum number of pets, any size or breed limits, and any separate cleaning charge, deposit or hold.”

Save the reply, and keep the listing's house rules and the price breakdown with it.
