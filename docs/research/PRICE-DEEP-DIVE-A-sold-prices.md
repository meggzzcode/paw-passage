# PRICE DEEP DIVE A: What starter / pre-revenue content sites actually sell for

Researched 2026-11-24. Subject: pawsandpassage.com (1 week old, 30 articles ~77k words, 38-row dataset,
32-page directory, gear catalog, 3 tools, no traffic history, no earnings).

HEADLINE: Only 3 verifiable-ish final sold prices for genuinely pre-revenue sites exist in what I could reach
(all $1,500-$1,700, all 2-4 year old domains, 56-106 articles). I did NOT find 20 distinct data points.
Roughly 14 rows below, but most are anecdotes, estimates or not-pre-revenue. Do not treat this as a dataset.

## 2. Distribution (sold, <1 yr old, no/minimal revenue)

There is NO verified sample of sites under one year old. The best evidence is:
- Verified-ish (broker case studies, 3 points, but domains 2-4 yrs old, 56-106 articles): $1,500 / $1,600 / $1,700.
- Anecdotal starter-site sales (rows 7, 9, 10): $70-$600, central ~$250-$350.
- Promotional claims (row 12): $2,000-$3,500. Discount heavily.

Working distribution, low / median / high, my judgment not a statistic:
- Low: about $100-$300 (bare starter, few articles, no backlinks).
- Median: about $400-$800 for a one-week-old site with decent content (interpolated; no direct sample).
- High: about $1,500-$1,700 (documented ceiling for pre-revenue content sites even with 56-106 articles and
  aged domains). Above ~$2,000 only claims by promoters.

Because rows 1-3 involve 2-4 year old domains with some backlinks and still sold at $1,500-$1,700 (one a pet
site with 106 articles at $1,500), a one-week-old domain with 30 articles should be expected at or below that.

## 3. How article count and niche moved price

- No monotonic relationship in the only structured data: 106 pet articles (105k words) sold for LESS ($1,500)
  than 69 parenting articles ($1,600) and 56 home articles ($1,700). Niche/domain age/backlinks and buyer
  haggling mattered more than volume. Pet was priced ~50% below the broker's own valuation.
- Flippa's own blog (seen via search snippet only) suggests content value ~10c/word if of freelancer-agency
  quality, which would put 77k words at ~$7,700, but the realized comps are ~1.4-3c/word (rows 1-3:
  $1,600/39k=4.1c, $1,500/106k=1.4c, $1,700/53k=3.2c). Cost-of-creation valuation is a ceiling, not a price.
- Gaurav Tiwari's guide (estimate, no sales): under $500 = essentially domain+hosting; $500-$1,500 most common
  band for a genuine content site; $1,500-$3,000 needs visibly better build; above $3,000 needs traffic,
  revenue, or independently valuable asset. Same guide: 15-25 articles, 3-6 months old, $500-$1,500;
  25+ articles/working store, 6-12 months, $1,500-$3,000. These are estimates, not sold data.
- Original dataset, tools and directory: no evidence in any source that these lift price for a pre-revenue site.
  Buyers in the anecdotes cared about domain name and content, little about design.

## 4. Share of starter listings that never sell

- Only figure found: "57% of all Starter Sites offered on Flippa get sold in post-auction negotiations"
  (FirstSiteGuide 2023 roundup, attributed to Flippa; wording ambiguous; could not verify). Another old blog:
  58% of all Flippa listings sold in a 3-month window (undated, platform-wide). Flippa itself noted a sharp
  drop in sell-through after 2021.
- Gaurav Tiwari states marketplaces do not publish pre-revenue sell-through, so any rate is unverified.
- Treat roughly 40-60% of starter listings failing to sell as a plausible but unverified guess.

## 5. Time to sell

- Flippa 2023 median close: 15 days for deals under $50k (snippet from Flippa data; all sizes, not starter only).
- Seller anecdote (row 9): usually within 3 months; ranges "a day to weeks or longer".
- Starter listing term on Flippa is up to 60 days for the Entry listing, so unsold listings expire.

## 6. Fees and net proceeds

Flippa (sources conflict, verify live before listing):
- Starter/template listing fee: $15 per one snippet; $29 flat Entry (60 days) per the guide; $49 for other
  listings per another source. Listing fee not refunded if unsold.
- Success fee: 10% under $50k (7.5% $50-100k; 5% above). A snippet says 15% for a broker-assisted sale.
- Payment handling about 1% (FlippaPay) to 1.2% (Escrow.com).
- Asking price cap for starter/non-revenue assets: $9,999. "Starter Sites" filter = sites 6 months old or younger.
- Net (guide's calculation, Entry listing): $500 sale nets $416 (17% lost); $1,000 nets $861; $2,000 nets
  $1,751; $3,000 nets $2,641 (12% lost).
Empire Flippers: requires about 12 months of revenue history and rejects most submissions; Investors Club
says sites need $500/mo and 12 months. Not an option for pre-revenue. Motion Invest buys vetted sites only;
no pre-revenue path found. Website Flip appears to handle non-revenue sites (the three case studies) but I
found no fee schedule; thewebsiteflip pages other than the guide were not read.

## 8. Skeptical read

- The three Website Flip prices are the only structured sold numbers, and they are the broker's own marketing,
  undated, with no asking prices. Still, they are internally consistent with the Flippa BIN anecdotes at the low end.
- Most "you can sell zero-revenue sites for $2,000-$5,000" claims come from people selling courses or flipping
  guides. No verifiable listing backs them.
- Seller-reported prices skew high (survivorship) and old (2011-2015 comments).
- Asking-price listings on Flippa for starters commonly sit far above realized prices; I found no asking-vs-sold
  pairs, so I cannot quantify the gap.

## 9. Best estimate for pawsandpassage.com

- Realistic net-sold range if it sells: about $400-$1,500 gross; $330-$1,300 net after Flippa fees.
- Most likely single outcome: ~$600-$1,000.
- Upside above $2,000 is unsupported by evidence unless real traffic/revenue accrues first (the 2-yr-old sites
  with 70 posts and 80k views/mo sold for $22k, showing traffic, not content, drives price).
- Probability it does not sell at all in a 60-day starter listing: unknown, guess 40-60%.
- Confidence: low-to-moderate (about 40%) on the range; the ceiling ~$1,700 is better supported than the floor.
