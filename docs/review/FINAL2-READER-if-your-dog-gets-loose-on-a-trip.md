# FINAL2 reader review: if-your-dog-gets-loose-on-a-trip

Viewed at 390 and 1280 px. Role: panicked phone visitor.

## What works
- "Do this first" is the first heading and a numbered list: (1) search where last seen, (2) call your microchip registry, (3) call animal control, vets, shelters and tell the host. Bold lead phrases make the three actions readable in a glance.
- No product card or product link appears before "Who to contact" and none in "The next hours and days". Products come only under "Before your next trip" and after.
- Attribution is specific (AKC Reunite, ASPCA); the tracker section is candid that a location is "a lead, not a rescue".
- No horizontal scroll; internal links returned 200.

## Observable defects
1. At 390 px the first screen shows the title, dek, affiliate disclosure box and collapsed "On this page" before the list; items 1 and 2 are visible but item 3 is cut off at the fold ("Call local animal control, vets and..."). Ten seconds is achievable, but only barely; the disclosure sits above the urgent content.
2. Item 3 packs four calls (animal control, vets, shelters, host) into one step and has no number to dial; item 2 says AKC Reunite "runs a 24/7 helpline" but the page shows no phone number or "tap to call" on the first screen.
3. Ordering tension: step 1 says search, step 2 says call; the next paragraph says "Send someone else to make the calls" only "if there is anyone with you", so a solo visitor is left without which of the two to do first.
4. Repeated answers: contacts are listed in "Do this first" and again in "Who to contact" (registry, animal control/vets/shelters); the tag and microchip advice is restated in several places.
5. Title says "Dog Got Loose on Vacation: The First Hour and Prevention" while the lower half is prevention and four tracker/ID cards. The urgent page is half a shopping guide. The line "Most of this takes ten minutes and no purchase" is followed immediately by a product card.
6. WhoseID card: a niche brand with "we could not read the brand's own page, so that is the listing's word"; the "no subscription" claim is listing-only.
7. Four tracker/light cards (Tractive, Fi, Petcube, Ruffwear Beacon) after a section that says a tracker "does not stop an escape" and sort tag and chip first; Petcube is carried with "one reviewer called it the most inaccurate", a card with a stated negative reason.
8. Nothing for the very first minutes inside the dwelling beyond "check small places"; no "do not chase / do not call loudly toward a frightened dog" guidance is given (absence, not an error).
