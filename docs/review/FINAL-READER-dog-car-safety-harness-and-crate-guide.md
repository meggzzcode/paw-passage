# Reader review: dog-car-safety-harness-and-crate-guide
Method: Playwright Chromium at 390 and 1280 px, screenshots, text and link audit. Page height 15,430 px at 390 (about 2,600 words): the longest of the three.

## Role 1: first-time search visitor
- First screen (390): title "Crash-Tested Dog Harness or Crate: How to Check One", strong subhead ("'Crash tested' is not regulated. Look up the exact model and size in the Center for Pet Safety directory, match your dog's weight, and skip extension tethers."), hero (dog in a pink harness in a car: on-topic), disclosure. The core advice is visible in the subhead: the best first screen of the three. The "Quick picks" are one scroll down.
- Search intent "best dog car harness" is only partly served: the guide names one harness (a 2014 test), and says "Over 90 lb: no harness here is tested that high". Honest, but a visitor may leave without a pick.

## Role 2: mobile traveler planning a trip
- Quick picks list is scannable and weight-based. But the Lucky Duck pick is awkward: "The card opens the Intermediate; for 70 to 110 lb you need the Large, a separate listing you look up yourself." and later "we have not verified a link for the Large ... search for 'Lucky Duck Lucky Kennel Large'". The traveler is asked to do the product matching the page promised to do. Similarly the Clickit Sport: "The button opens a listing for one size (Medium) when we checked on 2026-11-18" and "We could not find a Sleepypod product page for this model". A reader may reasonably decline to click.
- Repeated answers: "skip extension tethers" appears in the subhead, buying criteria ("A connection you can trust"), the Install section ("add no extension tether") and the pre-leave checklist ("No extension tethers or zipline-style add-ons"). "Never leave your dog alone in a parked vehicle" appears only in checklist, ok. The weight-match rule is stated in subhead, lead, Quick picks, criterion 2, "Small-dog picks" and the checklist. "Models, sizes and certifications change, so confirm the exact model and size with the maker and the CPS directory" plus "Nothing here replaces your veterinarian's advice" are boilerplate right after Quick picks.
- Table "Harness or crate?" is 3 columns by 3 rows; readable at 390 (columns narrow, cells wrap to 4 lines) with no horizontal overflow. It is a reasonable use because it compares two options on shared factors, but its rows are thin ("Quick on and off; the dog is a seat passenger"), mostly restating the prose before it.
- Product cards: five (harness, crate, carrier, booster, plus the picks are introduced by paragraphs). Each is after its reasoning, and "Skip it if" lines are present for harness and crate. The cards read as caveat-heavy: "Why we picked it: ... The button opens one size (Medium); choose by rib cage." This is accurate, but "Why we picked it" is being used for stock disclaimers. The Lucky Duck card is headed "Kennel" with the chip "Button opens Intermediate" and "CPS lists Large (one dog)" which confuses the title line. The Sleepypod Atom card does not say how to secure it in a vehicle ("Its product page does not say how to secure it in a vehicle"), and still is promoted for a car carrier; a safety-relevant weak point.
- "Buying checklist: seven criteria" has seven long bulleted items (about 450 words) with citations "(Center for Pet Safety, Center for Pet Safety)" doubled, which looks like a template glitch.
- Closing "Before you leave" checklist of 9 items repeats earlier content; "What to buy first" is short and useful but sits at the very end rather than near Quick picks.

## Role 3: skeptical reader
- This guide is the most rigorous: CPS evidence, "The test dog is a dummy", dated tests, admission that the studies are old. A strong trust signal is that it tells the reader its own harness listing is from 2014 and that no Sleepypod page was found.
- Trust cost: the page recommends a product whose page "we could not find" and whose Amazon link opens a single size; the Kurgo Heather booster is recommended with the maker disclaiming crash-test; the Atom has no securing method. A sceptic may see the commercial links as loosely supported, even though each carries a warning.
- Citations: "(Center for Pet Safety, Center for Pet Safety)" appears several times (criteria 2 and 4, Crate pick); mixed parenthetical and inline style. "CDC and ASPCA" claims are cited only as "(ASPCA)" or "(CDC, ASPCA)", acceptable.
- Disclosure: one top notice with Amazon sentence; no per-card labels; 13 Amazon links all rel sponsored. Good.
- Factual/safety flags: "Up to 90 lb" harness whose test is July 2014 and for a "Small to X-Large" range, while criterion 3 says "Some makers size by rib cage". Telling readers to feed "3 to 4 hours before leaving" is attributed. "A seat hammock is not a restraint." is an uncited statement in "What to buy first". Intro says the CDC and ASPCA "both advise the back seat" with only an ASPCA link: partial.
- Perceived length: the longest and densest; roughly 10 screens at 390 with a long checklist and a collapsed 16-source list.

## Observable defects
1. Picks that the reader must finish themselves: Large Lucky Kennel ("a separate listing you look up yourself"), Clickit Sport button opens "one size (Medium)", Sleepypod page "could not find".
2. Doubled citations "(Center for Pet Safety, Center for Pet Safety)".
3. "Skip extension tethers" and "match weight" stated 4-6 times; boilerplate "Models, sizes and certifications change..." right after Quick picks.
4. Atom carrier promoted without a securing method; Heather booster is not a restraint (acknowledged), so two of five cards are weak safety picks.
5. Harness/crate table repeats prose; Lucky Duck card title "Kennel" is confusing.
6. Length and density: seven-criterion list plus nine-item checklist.

## Verdict: READY WITH MINOR FIXES
Fixes: de-duplicate the CPS double citations; trim the repeated tether/weight reminders and the boilerplate paragraph under Quick picks; rename the Lucky Duck card heading to the model name; reconsider showing the Atom and Heather as cards given the stated gaps; shorten the "Before you leave" checklist; consider moving "What to buy first" up. If the unverified Large link or Clickit Sport size issue can be fixed with a correct link, do that first.
