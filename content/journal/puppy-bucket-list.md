A puppy's first year goes quickly, and it is full of firsts worth remembering. Many bucket lists online are for grown dogs, or they read like a checklist you must finish. This one is smaller. Think of it as a short list of gentle firsts, offered as invitations your puppy is always free to decline.

## The short answer

Choose a few experiences, keep each one short and positive, and let your puppy set the pace. Before you go anywhere with other dogs or shared ground, ask your veterinarian which places are right for your puppy's vaccination stage.

## Seven first-year ideas

**1. The first sniff of grass.** Sit on a clean patch of your own yard with your puppy and let them explore. No agenda.

**2. The first quiet car ride.** Use a secure carrier or harness, drive a few minutes and stop somewhere calm. Treat the trip as a success if your puppy stays comfortable.

**3. The first gentle new surface.** Offer a towel, a cardboard path or a rug. Let your puppy approach on their own.

**4. The first friendly visitor.** Invite one calm, dog-savvy person, let your puppy approach and keep it short.

**5. The first small puzzle.** Hide a few bits of puppy kibble in a muffin tin or box. Supervise closely, and remove anything they try to swallow.

**6. The first photograph.** A quiet portrait at your puppy's eye level, no flash, no fuss.

**7. The first rainy-day adventure.** Staying in counts. A blanket fort and a sniff trail are plenty.

## Practical considerations

Vaccination timing matters. The AVSAB position statement describes the first three months as the key period for socialization and says exposure should be as safe as possible and should not cause excessive fear or withdrawal. Your veterinarian can tell you which environments are safe now. Avoid busy dog areas until they say it is time, and keep food to what your puppy normally eats, away from foods on the ASPCA list.

## Your puppy, your pace

Participation is voluntary. If a puppy freezes, hides or turns away, increase distance and end on something easy. Puppies tire quickly, so short outings and plenty of naps are normal. Ask your veterinarian before starting running, jumping or long outings with a growing dog. If you have more than one dog at home, introduce new games separately.
