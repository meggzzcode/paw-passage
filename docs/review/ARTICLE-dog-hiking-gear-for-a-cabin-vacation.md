## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. **Pick-card and prose claims about the Web Master harness are not in the catalog record, so the card cannot be checked from our own data.**
   Where: the Web Master pick card ("The maker lists three leash attachment points, a neck buckle and a light loop for The Beacon, sized by girth from XXS to L/XL"), the sentence "The maker says that between sizes you should choose the smaller size" (and the FAQ "For both, the maker says to size down if you are between sizes"), and "the Web Master harness lists reflective trim" in "Seeing the dog at dusk".
   What is wrong: the `ruffwear-web-master-harness` catalog record lists sizes, handle wording, material, "Three leash attachment points ... neck buckle" and the "no car or crash-test claim" line, but nothing on a light loop, reflective trim or a between-sizes rule (the between-sizes rule is recorded only for the Grip Trex boots). The body also says "so check that your harness or collar has a loop; the Web Master harness above lists one" and uses that to tie the Beacon to the harness. If the maker page does not say it, this is an unsupported compatibility claim a skeptical buyer could check and fail.
   Fix: re-read the maker page and add the three facts (light loop, reflective trim, between-sizes rule) to the catalog `keySpecs` with the access date, then keep the text; if any is not on the page, delete it from the card, the "Seeing the dog at dusk" paragraph and the FAQ (edits 1 to 3). This is a PRODUCT task for the data owner before the guide can pass Trust.

2. **No decision shortcut: the reader who just wants "what do I buy for my trail" must read about 9 minutes to assemble the answer.**
   Where: the top bullet list ("Rules first.", "Leash.", "Harness.", "Boots.", "Light.") and the close "What to buy first".
   What is wrong: the bullets say what the sources say but never say what to buy; the sibling guides lead with a "What to buy" bullet. "What to buy first" tells the reader to measure and compare charts and to "buy only what the trails you chose call for", which is honest but leaves the harness, boots, leash and light in four separate sections with no side-by-side. The one table is the unknowns table at the bottom. There is no "if your trail is X, gear is Y" shortcut, and the research-complete claim that the guide helps people choose is not delivered in one screen.
   Fix: add a four-row "Match the gear to the trail" table after the bullet list (edit 4) using only statements already in the article, and add a "What to buy:" bullet (edit 5).

3. **"What to buy first" skips two of the four products and links the other two to the maker, not the pick card.**
   Where: "What to buy first" ("Compare them with the maker charts for the [Web Master harness] and [Grip Trex boots]...").
   What is wrong: the leash and the Beacon, both pick cards, are absent from the close; the two named products link to ruffwear.com (non-affiliate) while every earlier mention of the Web Master and Knot-a-Leash in the prose links to the Amazon button. Mixed destinations make the close look like it points away from the recommendation, and a reader who has decided has no single place to click. It also never says which of the four to buy first.
   Fix: edit 6 rewrites the close; link the names to the same targets used in the pick cards, and name an order (harness only for steep or scrambling trails, boots only after the paw check, leash first because every trail needs one).

4. **Title tag is too long and the meta description opens with an awkward clause.**
   Where: head: "Dog Hiking Gear for a Cabin Vacation: What to Pack | Paws & Passage" (67 chars) and the description "When a dog needs boots, harness or collar on steep trails, leash length, dusk visibility, heat and park trail rules, with maker size charts."
   What is wrong: Google typically truncates around 60 characters, so "What to Pack | Paws & Passage" will be cut and the page loses its hook; the description is a run-on that does not state the benefit. "Dog hiking gear" is a competitive head term; the cabin angle is the differentiator and should lead.
   Fix: edits 7 and 8.

5. **Missing "who should skip it" and a no-Ruffwear path for the light and leash; an unlinked product mention.**
   Where: the Beacon card and paragraph ("It clips to Ruffwear collars and apparel light loops"), the Knot-a-Leash card ("It is not a hands-free or bungee leash"), and the Quencher bowl paragraph (a product named and linked to Amazon in prose with no pick card, only in this paragraph).
   What is wrong: all four picks are Ruffwear; the guide says the Beacon fits only Ruffwear loops (from the catalog) but does not tell a non-Ruffwear owner what to do, and none of the cards says who should skip the product. The Quencher bowl is described with specs but has no card and no "details and specs" link, so it is a recommendation in prose only; its spec text ("a 4,000 mm waterproof rating on the lining") matches the catalog, but it breaks the pattern the other products follow.
   Fix: edits 9 to 11 add the skip lines and the Quencher card.

## What works well
- Rules come before gear: the "Check the trail rules before you buy gear" section cites NPS, the Gila National Forest page and WTA with the right scope warnings ("That is one forest's page", "That is a Washington example, not a national rule"), so the guide does not oversell any rule.
- Sizing is the buying advice: the harness chart figures (XXS 13-17 in ... L/XL 32-42 in), the boots instruction quoted from the maker ("Measure across widest part of paw when bearing weight. Measure ALL paws. If between sizes, size down.") and the front/rear difference all match the catalog, and the "Do you need boots" section honestly says no source gives a cutoff.
- Labelling is honest: "Our own reasoning, labeled as ours", "our judgment, not a sourced rule" and the unknowns table separate sources from opinion without inventing tests, and each product states what its page does not say (no strength rating on the leash, no heat or snow limit on the boots).

## Buyer walkthrough
1. Lands on the dek ("Most trail trips need less gear than the lists say") and the five bullets; learns to check rules first, but no "what to buy" line.
2. Scrolls to "Harness or collar", reads shared advice from PetMD and Orvis and our reasoning, meets the Web Master card at about 25% down with measurement advice just after it.
3. Boots section is the strongest decision aid ("a dog on soft forest paths for an hour probably does not need them"); the Grip Trex card follows.
4. Leash and Beacon cards come at 40% and 50%; the bowl is only a prose link at about 60%.
5. At "What to buy first" the reader is told to measure and compare charts and then to run the checklist; friction: two cards missing from the close, no single decision table, and the two links go to the maker.
