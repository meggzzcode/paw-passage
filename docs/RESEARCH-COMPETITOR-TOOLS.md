# Competitor Tools Audit: Dog-Friendly Travel

Prepared 2026-11-17 for Paws & Passage (static site, verified/dated/sourced data, no user-generated content, no backend; launch regions North Georgia mountains and coastal California).

## Method and honesty notes

- Labels: **SEEN** = I fetched the competitor's own page this session and read it. **REPORTED** = third-party page, review, forum, or a search-tool summary of a page I did not open myself. **NOT CONFIRMED** = I looked and could not verify.
- Many competitor sites blocked the fetch tool (403/429/503): BringFido, Rover, Vrbo, PetTravel.com, Pet Friendly Rentals, AllTrails support, Hipcamp support, Frommer's. For those, nearly everything is REPORTED or NOT CONFIRMED. I did not sign in or install any app, so in-app features are not SEEN.
- Only five competitor pages were fully SEEN: GoPetFriendly, DogFriendly.com, TripsWithPets, AKC hotel-chain guide, Chewy "How To Travel With a Dog".
- App-store and Trustpilot evidence came through search summaries, not the review pages. Complaint counts below are indicative, not measured. Several search hits were low-quality SEO pages; I discounted them and say so.
- Quotes are kept short. Links are inline.

## 1. Competitor-by-competitor audit

### BringFido
- **What / model:** Largest dedicated dog-travel directory plus hotel and rental booking, with a mobile app. Business model is not stated on the pages I could read. REPORTED: promo copy shows pay-now hotel booking discounts ([BringFido listing page](https://www.bringfido.co.uk/lodging/1076194)), and a trade article says it markets to vacation-rental owners ([RentalScaleUp](https://www.rentalscaleup.com/bringfido-com-niche-vacation-rental-websites/)). Commission model: NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Free tools:** REPORTED (App Store listing via search): list view, map view, search of pet-friendly places worldwide ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/bringfido/id682820712)). Whether size/fee filters or paid tiers exist: conflicting, NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Data sourcing:** REPORTED: user reviews plus company-verified policies; the company says it keeps policies updated. Homepage itself not SEEN (403).
- **Freshness:** Per-listing dates not confirmed.
- **Complaints (REPORTED):** Trustpilot summaries are dominated by booking errors and refund disputes, not stale policy ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bringfido.com?page=9)). BBB: not accredited, 18 complaints in 3 years ([BBB](https://prod.bbb.org/us/sc/greer/profile/dog-boarding/bringfidocom-0673-90005189/complaints)). One summary says the Android app sometimes shows places that are not pet-friendly.
- **Does NOT do (as far as I could tell):** park/beach/trail rule detail; any dated source citation per rule; downloadable data. NOT CONFIRMED either way.

### GoPetFriendly
- **What / model:** Pet travel site founded around 2009. **SEEN:** the founder's farewell post (dated Feb 13, 2025) says the directory is shutting down while the blog stays ([post](https://www.gopetfriendly.com/blog/its-been-a-great-ride/)). Reasons given: pet travel has become mainstream and trip planning is easier.
- **Free tools:** Directory was free; now closed. Blog guides remain (national parks, beaches, mountain getaways). SEEN: no trip planner or map mentioned on the page.
- **Data:** SEEN: lodging prices are averages of provider-stated rates, no guarantee. Listing sourcing not described.
- **Freshness:** SEEN: comments dated through Aug 2025; footer (c) 2026.
- **Does NOT do:** Directory no longer exists. Notably, its own wind-down is evidence that a plain "is it dog-friendly" directory is hard to sustain.

### DogFriendly.com
- **What / model:** Directory since 1997 covering lodging, beaches, parks, trails, dining, RV parks, interstate guides ([site](https://www.dogfriendly.com/)). Revenue model: SEEN: not stated (no ads or affiliate links visible in the fetched content).
- **Free tools:** SEEN: browsable guides, a clickable US state map, "Top Dog-Friendly" lists, articles. No calculator, no filters, no booking, no account tools visible.
- **Data:** SEEN: no sourcing method. It warns policies "change frequently" and asks users to report places that no longer allow dogs, i.e. user-flagged correction.
- **Freshness:** SEEN: only "since 1997" and (c) 1997-2026. No per-listing or per-article dates.
- **Complaints:** none found specific to it (NOT CONFIRMED).
- **Does NOT do:** show when a listing was verified, state the rule source, or show fees.

### TripsWithPets (TWP)
- **What / model:** Hotel-chain pet policy pages, rentals/resorts listings, "RoutePaws" route trip planner, PAWS rescue-support program, blog ([site](https://www.tripswithpets.com/)). Revenue model not stated (SEEN: it nudges users to book direct with hotels and mentions a rescue giving program).
- **Free tools (SEEN):** chain policy pages, RoutePaws route search for pet-friendly stays. Airline policy search: NOT CONFIRMED (page truncated).
- **Data / freshness:** SEEN: no sourcing description, no dates or last-updated stamps on the page.
- **Does NOT do:** show verification dates; park/beach/trail rules.

### Rover
- **What / model:** Two-sided marketplace for sitters/walkers/boarders. Revenue is service fees: REPORTED sitters pay about 15-25% per booking, owners about 11% on newer accounts, varying by country ([Rover help](https://support-ca.rover.com/hc/en-ca/articles/360037446911), [community Q&A](https://www.rover.com/community/question/51846/what-are-rover-fees-for-owners)).
- **Free tools:** NOT CONFIRMED (homepage blocked, 403). No dog-travel rule tools found.
- **Relevance:** A care marketplace (leave the dog at home), not a destination-rules resource.

### Wag
- **What / model:** Dog walking/sitting app. REPORTED: fee structure described inconsistently across blogs; current fees NOT CONFIRMED from an official page ([Budget Diet](https://www.thebudgetdiet.com/is-wag-worth-it)).
- **Reviews:** REPORTED: Trustpilot has thousands of reviews with recurring complaints about support, app bugs, and walker pay ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/wagwalking.com?page=3)). Many are from the caregiver side.
- **Travel tools:** none found. Note: a Wag Walking lifestyle page lists campground info (e.g. [Vogel State Park](https://wagwalking.com/lifestyle/campgrounds/vogel-state-park)) with pet fee and leash fields; REPORTED, I did not check its dating or accuracy.

### Vrbo (pet filter and pet fee display)
- **Filter / fees:** REPORTED: a "Pets allowed" filter exists but does not screen by weight or breed; guests must read house rules ([PawVentures guide](https://pawventures.pages.dev/blog/dog-friendly-vacation-rental-booking-guide-2026/); low-authority blog). REPORTED by one host: US checkout stopped showing the pet fee in the price breakdown ([Hospitable community](https://community.hospitable.com/industry-conversations-33/vrbo-pet-fees-1391)). Single anecdote; Vrbo's own help pages not verified.
- **Does NOT do (REPORTED):** consistent fee-by-weight or breed display up front.

### Airbnb (pet filter and pet fee display)
- **Filter / fees:** REPORTED: pet fees are folded into the nightly rate and not shown as a separate line ([Motley Fool](https://fool.com/the-ascent/personal-finance/articles/3-fees-to-know-about-before-booking-an-airbnb/), [HostTools help](https://help.hosttools.com/en/articles/6644888-why-isn-t-my-pet-fee-showing-on-airbnb)). Host forum threads describe the fee not displaying or not saving ([community thread](https://community.withairbnb.com/t5/Community-Cafe/Pet-fee-not-calculating-to-booking-price/m-p/1533580)). Mostly undated host posts; Airbnb's own current behavior NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Does NOT do (REPORTED):** show the pet fee as its own line; filter by dog size or count.

### Hipcamp
- **What / model:** Marketplace for private-land camping; hosts set pet fees. REPORTED (search excerpts of Hipcamp help and listing pages): "Pets allowed" filter, ability to add a dog as a guest, per-listing fees such as $15-$25 per dog, host-set limits on number, size or breed ([Hipcamp support](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/48103100619668-Bringing-Your-Dog-to-a-Hipcamp-Tips-for-Hipcampers)). Direct fetch blocked.
- **Public land:** Hipcamp has Q&A pages on public sites (e.g. [Half Moon Bay](https://www.hipcamp.com/en-US/question/are-dogs-allowed-at-half-moon-bay-kqw3sgrk)). Sourcing and dating NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Dog-specific complaints:** none found (NOT CONFIRMED).
- **Does NOT do:** standardized fee/rule fields across hosts (REPORTED: "up to the Host").

### AllTrails (dog filters)
- **What / model:** Trail app; freemium (Plus/Pro paid). REPORTED: dogs-permitted is a trail attribute users can suggest edits to; AllTrails claims land-manager partnerships plus community reports ([AllTrails support](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018930672-How-to-update-or-change-information-about-a-trail), blocked from direct fetch; summary via search).
- **Complaints (REPORTED):** general crowd-sourced accuracy criticism ([Buckrail](https://buckrail.com/know-before-you-go-users-report-alltrails-inaccuracies-in-gtnp/), [Columbian](https://columbian.com/news/2021/jun/06/trail-apps-offer-a-bounty-of-sometimes-false-info)). I found no report specific to the dog filter, so wrong-dog-rule frequency is NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Does NOT do (REPORTED/inferred):** cite the specific regulation or its date on the trail page; I could not confirm either way.

### Dog-friendly cabin rental sites (regional operators)
- **Examples (REPORTED, via search):** Cabin Rentals of Georgia lists under-35 lb, crate-trained dogs only, $50/pet/night plus cleaning fee and deposit ([policy page](https://cabin-rentals-of-georgia.com/policies)); other Blue Ridge listings range from no pet fee to $150/dog, or $30/night/pet; some Airbnb listings advertise "No Pet Fee" in the title.
- **Pattern:** Policies are per-operator, on separate pages, in different formats (flat fee, per night, deposit, weight caps, breed limits). No cross-operator comparison exists in the results.
- **Does NOT do:** normalize to a total cost for a given stay with N dogs.

### Pet Friendly Rentals
- **What / model:** NOT CONFIRMED. The site returned 503 and search found nothing specific to it. Cannot characterize tools, model, or data.

### PetTravel.com
- **What / model:** International pet-transport and airline-policy information; homepage blocked (403). REPORTED: covers 160+ airlines and import requirements for 220+ countries; says it tries to match current airline rules ([airline rules page](https://Pettravel.com/airline_rules.cfm)). Revenue model: NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Does NOT do:** domestic road-trip lodging or park/beach rules; no independent accuracy audit found.

### Chewy travel guides
- **SEEN:** "How To Travel With a Dog" is editorial, updated Jul 1, 2025, vet-reviewed, no interactive tools; links to a downloadable packing-list image; heavy product cards with Add-to-Cart ([guide](https://www.chewy.com/education/dog/general/pet-travel-101)). Business model is retail: content drives product sales.
- **Does NOT do:** location-specific rules or fees.

### AKC travel content
- **SEEN:** Hotel-chain guide shows "Updated: Jul 31, 2026"; policies in prose, not a table; many "vary by property" entries; tells readers to confirm with the hotel ([AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/travel/a-guide-to-pet-friendly-hotel-chains-in-the-united-states/)). REPORTED: a separate AKC complete-travel guide covers hotels, fees budgeting and vaccine records ([AKC guide](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/travel/complete-guide-to-traveling-with-your-dog/)).
- **Does NOT do:** structured/comparable data, a lookup tool, or any local-region depth.

### The Dyrt (campground dog-rule filter)
- **What / model:** Campground discovery app/site, freemium. REPORTED: "Pets Allowed" filter ([tutorial](https://thedyrt.com/magazine/camping-skills/tutorial-filters-saved-campground-lists/)); its 2024 dog-friendly rankings used AI over user reviews and tips ([press](https://thedyrt.com/press/the-9-most-dog-friendly-campgrounds-in-the-u-s-according-to-the-dyrt/)). A sample location page had generic, boilerplate dog claims (REPORTED by search summary; weak evidence).
- **Accuracy audit:** none found. NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Does NOT do (REPORTED):** show rule-level detail (leash length, breed limits, fee) from an official source.

### Apps/tools that check dog rules for parks, beaches, trails
- **PawMap (Australia):** REPORTED to show council rules from official council sites and disclaim local enforcement ([PawMap](https://pawmap-api.onrender.com/)); not a US tool, low-authority host.
- **Dog Park Finder Plus, Paw Parks, DogPack, Sniffspot, DogMap:** REPORTED via roundup ([TechWalla](https://www.techwalla.com/12000015/these-4-apps-will-help-you-sniff-out-the-best-dog-parks)); mostly dog parks and user-contributed data; Sniffspot is private rentable spaces, not public rules.
- **Orijen Trails:** REPORTED web tool for dog-friendly trails in 100+ state and national parks; I could not open the Frommer's piece or the tool to confirm sourcing.
- **Official sources exist but are fragmented:** California State Parks publishes a park-by-park dog table and the default rule (dogs generally barred from trails and beaches unless posted) ([parks.ca.gov/dogs](https://www.parks.ca.gov/dogs)); Georgia State Parks states a 6-ft leash and no pets in buildings or swim areas ([gastateparks.org](https://gastateparks.org/ParkRules)). Cabin fees for Georgia parks came only from third parties and disagree (about $45 vs $50): NOT CONFIRMED from the official booking page.

### Cross-cutting evidence on stale "pet-friendly" flags
- REPORTED: advocacy site argues stale pet-friendly flags and hidden fees are a systemic problem ([Pet Friendly Is Broken](https://petfriendlyisbroken.com/hotels/the-false-filter); fetch blocked, search-excerpt only, and it may be a competitor or advocate with an angle). UGA vet school's own hotel list says fee info may be out of date ([UGA PDF](https://vet.uga.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Pet-Friendly-Hotels.pdf)). NerdWallet says pet fees are often buried ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/travel/learn/your-guide-to-hotel-travel-with-pets)).

## 3. Eight biggest gaps (with evidence)

1. **No total dog-cost view for a rental stay.** Airbnb reportedly folds the pet fee into the nightly rate and Vrbo reportedly hid it at checkout for at least one US host. Regional cabin fees vary from none to $150/dog or $30-$50/night. No calculator found. (REPORTED: Motley Fool, Hospitable, cabin pages above.)
2. **Weight/breed/count limits are not filterable.** Vrbo/Airbnb filters reportedly ignore weight and breed; cabin operators set caps like under-35 lb. (REPORTED.)
3. **No visible "verified on [date] from [source]" on rules.** DogFriendly and TWP show no per-item dates (SEEN); AKC and Chewy date whole articles only (SEEN). Directories push verification onto the reader ("policies change frequently," SEEN on DogFriendly).
4. **Park/beach/trail dog rules are fragmented.** Official info is split across agency pages (California table, Georgia rules page) and conflicts with third-party copies (Georgia cabin fee $45 vs $50). Mainstream trail and campground apps rely on crowd edits or reviews (REPORTED).
5. **California coast default surprise.** State parks generally bar dogs from trails and beaches unless posted (REPORTED via parks.ca.gov summary), yet roundup content often implies broad dog-friendliness. No consumer tool found that answers "which specific California state beach allows my dog, on-leash or not, with the park's own source." NOT CONFIRMED that none exists.
6. **No comparable structured data on hotel chains.** AKC and TWP use prose and "varies by property"; no table of fee/limit/count by chain with date. (SEEN.)
7. **Directories are shrinking or unmaintained.** GoPetFriendly closed its directory (SEEN, 2025). DogFriendly shows only a copyright range (SEEN). Reduces the supply of maintained, local, verified data.
8. **Regional depth (North Georgia mountains, coastal California) is missing.** National directories and AI-ranked lists dominate; I found no site combining cabin fee rules, state-park dog rules, and local trailhead/beach rules for these two regions. (Absence of evidence: NOT CONFIRMED, but my searches surfaced none.)

## 4. Which gaps a small static site could credibly fill

Realistic with verified, dated, sourced, no-UGC data and client-side JavaScript:

| Gap | Fit | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Total dog-cost view | Strong | Client-side calculator: nights x dogs x fee structure (flat/per-night/per-dog, deposit, cleaning). Inputs from operator policy pages, each cited and dated. |
| 3. Verified-date + source on every rule | Strong | Every record carries source URL, verification date, and next re-check date. This is the trust differentiator. |
| 4/5. Park, beach, trail rule lookup for the two launch regions | Strong | Small curated dataset (GA state parks, selected North Georgia trailheads and USFS areas, CA coastal state beaches and parks). Quote or paraphrase agency rule, link the agency page. |
| 6. Hotel-chain table with fees/limits | Moderate | Possible for a short list of chains, but chain policies vary by property so the table must be framed as "chain default, verify property." Maintenance burden is real. |
| 2. Weight/breed filter | Moderate | Works for operators the site itself covers; cannot replace Airbnb/Vrbo search at scale. |
| Printable/downloadable checklists and rule sheets | Strong | Static PDFs or CSV; no competitor offers downloadable verified data. |
| 7. Fill the abandoned-directory space | Weak | Do not try to replace a national directory; stay curated and regional. |
| 8. Regional depth | Strong | This is the launch strategy itself. |

Risks: re-verification cost grows with record count; rules and fees change (even the official Georgia pages could not be opened to confirm cabin fees, so verify at the source before publishing); keep a visible "report a change" mailto rather than a backend. Do not claim completeness.

## 5. Where large players are strong; do not compete

- **Booking and inventory at scale:** Airbnb, Vrbo, Hipcamp, BringFido (booking). Cannot match inventory, payments, or availability.
- **Pet-care marketplaces:** Rover and Wag (two-sided trust, insurance, scheduling).
- **Trail navigation and GPS content:** AllTrails (maps, offline tracks, reviews, land-manager partnerships claimed) and The Dyrt (campground reviews, photos).
- **Community reviews and photos:** BringFido, Hipcamp, The Dyrt, AllTrails. A no-UGC static site should not try to imitate this.
- **Product retail and mass editorial:** Chewy, AKC (brand authority, vet review, large SEO footprint).
- **International pet import and airline policy:** PetTravel.com and government sources; high-stakes, fast-changing, and best left to official authorities (link out).
- **National coverage:** BringFido and DogFriendly's breadth.
