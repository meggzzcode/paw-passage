Older dogs are often at their best company: relaxed, known and full of favorites. Many have not lost their sense of fun, only their need to prove anything. The best things to do with a senior dog are the ones that suit the dog they are today, and there are plenty of them.

## The short answer

Think gentle, nose-led and unhurried: slow sniff walks, scent games, simple tricks and comfortable time outdoors. Cornell's senior enrichment guidance suggests easy routes, cool times of day and walks treated as relaxed sniffathons.

## Six ideas for a golden-years day

**1. A sniffathon.** Choose an easy path at a cool time of day and let your dog set the pace. Bring water and snacks.

**2. A scent game.** Hide a few treats in cups or boxes and let your dog find them. Scent games ask for no running or jumping, and many dogs nap happily afterward.

**3. A food puzzle.** Scatter-feed in a large cardboard box or use a simple puzzle. It is a gentle way to keep the mind busy.

**4. A new old trick.** The idea that an old dog cannot learn is a myth. Choose a low-effort trick like a nose touch and keep it short.

**5. A rolling game.** Roll a ball along the floor instead of throwing it. Noisy or light-up toys can help dogs with less vision or hearing.

**6. A birthday or gotcha day.** Invite a favorite person, make a treat dog-safe and take a quiet photo. Celebrating more than once a year is allowed.

## Practical considerations

Check with your veterinarian at the annual wellness visit about activities that suit your dog. Reassess often, because dogs who want to please you may push past their limits. If your dog goes around a low obstacle instead of over it, that may be a sign they are no longer comfortable jumping. Keep swims short, avoid currents and use a long line. Provide non-slip floors and a soft place to rest.

## Your dog, your pace

Some seniors still love an adventure; others like a warm patch of sun. Both are good days. Watch for signals like lagging, panting or stopping, and shorten the plan. With more than one dog, choose activities that suit the oldest, and give each dog their own space for food games.
