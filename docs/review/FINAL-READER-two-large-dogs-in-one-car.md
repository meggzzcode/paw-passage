# Independent reader review: two-large-dogs-in-one-car

Method: Playwright Chromium at 390 and 1280 px against http://localhost:4179/guides/two-large-dogs-in-one-car. Viewed first-screen and full-page screenshots (390 px page about 13,600 px tall; no horizontal overflow; no tables). Internal links all return 200; external 403s (cdc.gov) are bot blocking. No user testing; one reviewer's reading. Scenario applied: someone with an SUV and two large dogs.

## First screen
Title "Two Large Dogs in a Car: Crates, Harnesses and Barriers"; dek "Your cargo floor width and rear belt positions decide between two strapped crates and a harness per dog. Measure those before you shop." That is a direct statement of the problem and the first action, and it fits the scenario exactly. Photo of two collies in an SUV cargo area is on-topic. Affiliate notice is below the photo and buttons, cut at the fold at 390 px.

## Scenario test: can the SUV owner identify what to measure and the layout questions?
Mostly yes, with gaps.
Present: "What to measure before buying anything" lists cargo floor width and depth, rear belt positions, each dog's weight and girth, anchor points, and barrier opening. "Start with the heavier dog's weight" and the under-90 / over-90 split give a layout decision. "How your vehicle changes the answer" gives an SUV entry ("the most choice. Two crates fit only if the floor is wide enough").
Missing for this reader:
- No exterior crate dimensions. The guide gives only interior widths ("20.5 inch interior width", "at least 44 inches of width before the walls"), so the reader cannot compare to a cargo floor. "At least 44 inches ... before the walls" is hard to interpret.
- No wheel-well width (the actual limiter in most SUV cargo areas), no cargo height versus crate height with the hatch closing, no mention of seats folding flat or a sloping load floor, no third-row case, no stated dog height (needed to size crate height).
- It never says whether two Large Lucky Kennels plausibly fit a typical SUV, so the headline crate recommendation can't be sanity-checked from the page.
- The measure list is section 5 of 8, after about 1,000 words and after "Common setups". The dek promises it up front; the actionable list is buried.

## Role 1: first-time search visitor
Strong opening paragraph ("Two large dogs in one car is a layout problem before it is a shopping problem"), though the "X before Y" construction is a mild stock pattern. The decision bullets (Under 90 lb / Over 90 lb / Either way) are useful. "Nobody has independently crash-tested two dogs side by side" is an absolute claim the guide cannot prove; "we found no test" would be accurate.

## Role 2: mobile traveler planning a trip
Scannable bold-lead paragraphs and vehicle bullets work on a phone. Repetition: "a barrier is a divider, not a restraint" is stated in the decision bullets, in "Harnesses plus a barrier" ("does not hold either one in place in a stop"), as the line "Barriers, as dividers only." before the cards, and in the final checklist ("Barrier added only as a divider"). "Never share [a crate]" appears in setups, checklist and FAQ. The final checklist repeats the "What to measure" list ("Cargo floor, belt positions, girth and anchor ratings measured"). The heatstroke/state-law section is correct but tangential to layout and adds length. End blocks overlap: "Related reading: our car-safety gear category, the ramp guide and the multi-dog rental guide" and then "Keep reading" lists the harness guide and multi-dog guide again; the ramp guide link is off-topic here.
Math sentence: "Two of them come to under 216 pounds in total, in more than 41 inches of interior width" is correct (2x(70+38), 2x20.5), but it is presented in a sentence full of spec numbers and an inline Amazon link ("Lucky Kennel"); it takes two reads.

## Role 3: skeptical reader (trust, product clicks)
Strengths: heavy, honest sourcing with inline publisher links (CPS, ASPCA, CDC, Vetstreet, Cornell, AAA all linked); "skip it" conditions on every product; Ruffwear and Kurgo claims are labelled as the makers'; cards are grouped in one "Restraint and barrier examples" block after the instructions, so they do not interrupt them.
Problems:
- The lead crate card points the wrong way for this audience. The guide says "Two large dogs will often need the Large, but the button below opens the under-70 lb Intermediate; choose the Large on the maker's page." and the card chips read "Button opens Intermediate" / "CPS lists Large (one dog)". A reader with two large dogs clicks through to the product the page says is the wrong size.
- The "harness per dog under 90 lb is a real option" lead rests on one CPS listing from July 2014, with the guide admitting "Sleepypod now shows a Clickit Sport Plus we did not match to it, so confirm the exact product first." Recommending a product it cannot match to its test is a weak commercial recommendation; the 75-90 lb gap (where many large dogs sit) is covered only by that card.
- "Why we picked it" is mostly a spec echo again ("Tension-fit metal frame with rubberized caps and a storage pouch"); PetSafe has "No crash testing mentioned" yet is carded.
- Affiliate disclosure: one top notice with the Amazon sentence, no per-card labels. Correct. Inline body links to Amazon ("Lucky Kennel", "Rover Hammock", "Backseat Barrier", "Tru-Fit") sit in factual sentences, so a reader may not realize which in-sentence links are commercial.
- Safety: advice is conservative and mostly well sourced. Unsourced assertions: "Sedan: crates rarely fit the trunk safely"; "A cab with no rear seat cannot take two large dogs and the driver". "Kurgo... we did not verify its fit for two dogs" is honest.
- Six cards for a 2,100-word page (7 min) is a lot, but each is tied to a setup.

## Verdict: READY WITH MINOR FIXES (items 1-3 are the ones I would not ship without)
1. Add exterior crate dimensions (or say interior width is not the fit number) and give a measure-and-compare step for the SUV owner: wheel-well width, cargo height versus crate height with hatch closed, seats-folded load floor.
2. Fix the Lucky Kennel card so the button opens the Large (or the example is the Large), since the guide's own text says two large dogs need it.
3. Reconcile "under 90 lb: harness is a real option" with the thin evidence (one 2014 listing the guide cannot match to a current product); soften or add a second option.
4. Move "What to measure before buying anything" up, directly after the layout decision.
5. Collapse the repeated "barrier is a divider" and "never share a crate" statements; remove the duplicate final checklist or the duplicate "Related reading" block (and the ramp link).
6. Replace "Nobody has independently crash-tested two dogs side by side" with a statement about what the guide found.
