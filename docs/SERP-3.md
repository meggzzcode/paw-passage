Title idea: How Many Dogs Can You Bring to an Airbnb or Vrbo? (And What to Do With 3+)

Outline:

If the listing says nothing about a number, ask before booking and get the answer in writing.
2. **How does Airbnb handle pet count?** Walk through the 0-5 declaration, what the host sees, how pet fees apply (per booking, night, pet, or pet per night; cap at nightly base rate). Cite 3623.

4. **Is there a weight limit for dogs in vacation rentals?** Platforms do not set one; hosts do. Typical numbers (e.g. 50 lb) are REPORTED from third parties, so present as "commonly seen", not data. Include Best Friends 44.6 lb context carefully labeled.
5. **What if I have 3 or more dogs?** Steps: filter, read rules, message, get a count confirmation in writing, declare the true number at booking, plan for fee, consider whole-house listings with yard. Warn: arriving with an undeclared extra dog can lose the booking and fee protections (frame as risk, cite host forum thread as an example, not law).
6. **Do extra dogs cost more?** Table of fee structures with Airbnb's official options; say amounts vary and show formula example. No invented averages.
7. **Do city dog limits apply to a rental?** Honest: many cities cap dogs per household at 2-4 (cite animallaw.info after fetching); how they apply to guests is NOT CONFIRMED; ask host or city.
8. **What to message the host** (template below).

Copy-paste template:

Are [N] dogs OK? Is there a weight limit per dog? What is the pet fee for [N] dogs, and is it included in the total at checkout? Please confirm here so we both have it in writing. Thank you."

Title idea: Renting a Vacation Home With a Senior Dog: Stairs, Floors, and What to Ask the Host

Outline:
1. **Can you take a senior dog on a vacation rental trip?** Direct answer (about 50 words): Often yes, if your vet agrees the dog is fit for the trip. Age alone is not the deciding factor; health, mobility, and how the dog handles car rides are. Ask your vet before booking, especially for heart, kidney, joint, or cognitive conditions.
2. **Should you go at all? (decision aid)** Table: signs it is probably fine / ask your vet first / consider a sitter. Cite forum themes as examples of the dilemma, not evidence.
3. **What should you ask the host about stairs and floors?** Step-free entrance, bedroom/bathroom level, flooring type, stair material, yard slope, fence. Direct answer (about 45 words).
4. **How do you handle slippery floors in a rental you can't change?** Rubber-backed rugs/yoga mats on routes, booties/toe grips, keep nails trimmed, gate stairs, ramp for furniture. Cite Dog Aging Project and Banfield. Note: ask host before moving rugs/furniture.
5. **How far should the rental be from a vet?** No official distance standard found. NOT CONFIRMED. Provide "find the nearest 24-hour emergency vet and save the number before you go" (ASPCA-style advice, REPORTED), a map-check method.
6. **What medications and supplies do you pack?** Refill meds before leaving, keep in original packaging, bring records and a written list from your vet (blogs suggest; Dogtrekker SEEN suggests refilling about a week ahead, unsourced, so say "before you go").
7. **How do you keep a senior dog comfortable in the rental?** Familiar bed, routine, temperature, quiet room, accident kit; mention accident/cleaning-fee policy and tell the host.
8. **How long should the trip be?** Shorter or fewer-night stays suit many seniors per blogs (REPORTED/SEEN opinion), not a rule.

Decision aid: a "go / ask your vet / stay home" table plus a "rental fit" scorecard (stairs, floors, ground-floor bedroom, fenced flat yard, vet within X minutes set by user's comfort).
