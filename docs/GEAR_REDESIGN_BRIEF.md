# Gear redesign brief (owner directive, 2026-11-24): a 10/10 photo-free gear shopping experience

Source of truth for the Gear redesign workers. Build and test, do not just recommend. Goal: a premium starter-site flip (about $1,500): the Gear section must feel like a polished editorial gear shop that real visitors want to browse and click through, with no fabricated ratings, reviews, prices, testing, stock or popularity.

## Hard constraints
- NO product photography: no Amazon/manufacturer images, no unrelated stock photos, no AI images of real products, no empty image placeholders or "Image unavailable" boxes. The design must feel intentional without photos: typography (Fraunces for headlines, DM Sans for UI), editorial composition, tonal colour-block panels, restrained original vector shapes, Lucide line icons, subtle geometric backgrounds, spec chips, purpose labels, clear shopping journeys, restrained cards, subtle interaction feedback.
- Reuse existing colour tokens in src/styles.css (ivory, dark green, sage, sand, terracotta, light sky); no second palette, no giant gradients, no glassmorphism, no heavy animation, no new heavy dependencies. Rounded cards, tonal surfaces, fine borders, consistent button sizes, reduced-motion support.
- Preserve: affiliate link precedence in src/content/gear-links.ts (approved affiliate > manufacturer > plain retailer > no button), rel attributes, `trackProductClick()` events, partner identification, approved-status logic, non-affiliate fallbacks, buyer ID replacement logic, SEO (canonical /gear, category URLs, legacy 301 redirects, noindex rules, breadcrumbs structured data, server-rendered content, valid status codes), accessibility, route behaviour. Server-rendered content must not depend on hydration; no SPA.
- Owner's latest decisions (override anything older in code): product cards show the buy button FIRST then "Details and specs"; there is NO per-card "Affiliate link · We may earn a commission" label (a clear affiliate notice sits at the top of pages that carry affiliate links; Amazon's exact sentence "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." stays wherever Amazon links appear); no "Check the current price" lines; no per-card repeats of "sizes change" (once per page). Do not show prices or stock. Do not call products best seller / top rated / customer favorite / most popular / expert tested / award-winning. Labels like "Good for small dogs", "Useful for cabin cleanup" must be supported by the product record. CTA wording must match the destination ("See it at Amazon", "See at <Maker>", "Visit manufacturer"; never "Buy now" for an info page). No button when no valid link: show an information-only state.
- Remove the research-paper feel: reduce "This is not a store", "No prices, no ratings", repeated "we have not tested", repeated manufacturer-spec explanations, repeated "check before you buy", methodology links after every section. Keep ONE concise research explanation per page with a methodology link; keep material disclosures by commercial actions; keep safety-critical distinctions beside the recommendation (not hidden in collapsed panels); put long evidence in accessible detail panels.

## /gear homepage (src/routes/gear.index.tsx)
A. Hero: eyebrow "DOG TRAVEL ESSENTIALS"; headline "Good gear. Better getaways."; supporting sentence "Thoughtful essentials for easier road trips, cozy cabin stays, and adventures with your dog."; primary CTA "Find gear for your trip", secondary "Browse all categories". Desktop: copy left, intentionally designed photo-free composition right (3 to 5 abstract vector/icon cards for car, bowl, bag, cabin, trail; not resembling specific products). Mobile: stacked, concise, first shopping navigation visible in the first viewport where practical.
B. "Where are you headed?" ("Start with the kind of trip you're packing for."): five trip cards from the existing `shopByTrip` data in src/content/gear-hub.ts (road trip, cabin stay, beach and lake, winter, hiking). Card: large icon, small label, short trip name, one sentence about the gear, navigation affordance. Desktop 3 + 2 (or balanced grid), tablet 2 columns, mobile 1 or 2. A trip selection MUST lead to a real curated collection of actual products from the existing catalog (e.g. a server-rendered trip-filtered section on /gear via a search parameter or hash that works without JS and with browser history; no new standalone indexable SEO pages; no empty collections; show fewer good products rather than padding).
C. "A few good places to start" (3 to 6 actual products): deterministic, supported by product records, active, working link; variety across road trip, cabin, comfort, safety; reuse `guidePickFor()` where useful; "best for" labels only from product data.
D. "Find exactly what you need": all 11 categories from src/content/gear-categories.ts (do not replace the taxonomy), cards with consistent icon, name, concise benefit descriptor, dynamic active product count, affordance; 3 columns desktop, 2 tablet, 1 to 2 mobile; no research badges per tile.
E. "Every dog travels differently": shopByDog groups (large, senior, escape artists, multi-dog) as compact cards leading to relevant products or existing dog-type guides; no medical suitability claims.
F. "Plan the trip, not just the packing": the best buying/packing guides plus the packing-list tool as a clear CTA ("Make a packing list").

## Product card (src/components/site/GearHubCards.tsx and ProductSpecCard.tsx; product purpose first, buying decision second, research on demand)
A use-case label (e.g. SMALL DOG CAR TRAVEL, CABIN COMFORT); B photo-free graphic panel (one relevant line icon, category tone, simple geometric motif, optionally 1 to 2 spec chips; extend `SPEC_CARD_STYLE`/icon mapping; no giant icon floating in empty space); C product name prominent, brand small, no repeated brand; D one or two sentences on the problem it solves (product.summary and guide pick data; no empty sales language); E at most three decision-driving facts from `cardSpecs` or verified records; F one concise decision-relevant caution where appropriate (e.g. check the vehicle securing instructions for restraints); G one primary CTA (buy button first), secondary "Details and specs"/related guide only; H no per-card affiliate label (see owner decisions); I native accessible `<details>` "Details, fit & sources": full specs, maker claims, independent-testing info where supported, extra watch-outs, source links, read date, related guides, alternative retailers. Safety or suitability qualifications that could mislead if hidden stay visible outside the collapsed panel. Subtle hover (border, background shift, arrow), visible focus, `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Category page (src/routes/gear.$category.tsx)
Compact hero (breadcrumb, H1, two-sentence shopper intro, contextual icon); products immediately (grid or readable split cards; single column on mobile); "How to choose" after the products (short preview linking to detail; safety-critical categories such as restraints keep essential distinctions visible early); secondary-category products as compact related rows, not repeated oversized cards; related guides without a wall of cards; one concise "How we pick" with a methodology link.

## Curation
Typed, validated central configuration (extend src/content/gear-hub.ts); no duplicate inventory or database; validation that featured/collection product IDs exist and are active; deterministic and maintainable.

## Integration
Link products to relevant guides and guides to their gear category; connect the packing-list tool to Gear without overloading it; reuse components rather than parallel product cards; update `GearPicks` and the in-article pick cards only where needed to stay visually consistent and keep owner decisions above.

## Responsive and accessibility
Test 320, 375, 390, 430, 768, 1024, 1440 px: no horizontal overflow, clipped chips, tiny CTAs, text collisions, excessive card heights. Semantic headings/lists, accessible card and button labels, keyboard navigation, visible focus, sufficient contrast, accessible expandable sections, decorative icons aria-hidden, no colour-only meaning, no hover-only content. No heavy image or animation libraries; initial content server-rendered.

## QA
Test all 11 categories, every trip and dog shortcut, featured selection, primary and fallback links, affiliate notice and Amazon sentence, analytics handlers, details expand/collapse, related-guide links, mobile navigation, empty/unpublished category handling, legacy redirects, catalog validation, server-rendered content. Add focused tests (category counts, active products only, deterministic curation, valid featured references, no invalid affiliate links, accessible card structure, disclosure placement, link precedence, no broken navigation). Run tsc, eslint, vitest, production build; investigate failures instead of dismissing them. Visual QA with Playwright screenshots at mobile, tablet and desktop widths, reviewing the actual output. Five shopper journeys to simulate: weekend cabin traveler, six-hour road trip, senior-dog owner needing a ramp, comparison shopper, skeptical first-time visitor.
