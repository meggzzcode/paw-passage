### vacation-rental-secure-fenced-yard (2,041 words, published)

**Verdict: TRIM.** The advice is right and the checklist, table and evidence pack are exactly the "easy" format; the length comes from a dog-profile section, a decode-the-listing section, two Ellijay cabin examples and three product write-ups that restate what is already said.

**First 150 words.** Opens: "A 'fenced yard' photo shows a lawn, not where the fence ends, how wide the gap under the gate is, or who else can walk through." Good hook. Then "The short answer" is a single sentence about "partially fenced" and invisible fences, which is not the answer to the reader's real question (how do I check?). The actual answer (ask for a sketch, tape-measure photos, a gate video) does not appear until "Request a small evidence pack" about 1,100 words down, and only in the dek and FAQ before that.

**Know / Bring / Care.** Know: present but spread over six sections. Bring: buried (pen and leash picks at the 60 percent mark). Care: present (arrival walk-around, "one person on the dog, another on the doors").

**Encyclopedia smells.**
- "Start with your dog, not a universal fence height": opens with two AKC citations, then a five-bullet jump/climb/dig/squeeze/rush table that the "weakest section" section repeats (Height, Climbers, Diggers).
- "Decode the listing before you contact the host": the gated-deck point is made twice ("A gated deck is not a fenced yard" appears in the first paragraph and again at the start of the Sliding Rock paragraph), plus a glossary of listing words ("natural boundary", "dog run", "secure").
- Two named cabin operators (Sliding Rock, Sacred Waters) to prove a point already made in one line.
- The Airbnb paragraph ("lists a fenced-in yard or private patio among the pet features guests may want to know about") is a source summary with no decision attached.
- "Special cases" repeats the VCA invisible-fence summary that the short answer and FAQ already state.
- Three product paragraphs carry maker-spec detail (eight panels, anchors, 16.3-inch door, 6-month warranty).

**Voice.** Mostly a careful manual: "Compare listings side by side in your own notes, not with a numerical 'safety score.'" Friendly lines exist ("one person on the dog, another on the doors") but are rare. Problem line: "Pause if a gap is concealed by mulch or loose stones."

**Edit plan (target about 1,300 words).**
1. Replace "The short answer" with the proposed opening below and fold the one-line "partially fenced means leash-only" rule into it.
2. Merge "Start with your dog" into "Check the boundary": delete the AKC sentence, keep the five bullets as one tight "what your dog does, what to ask" list, and delete the duplicated Height/Climbers/Diggers bullets (keep the "weakest section, not the average" line).
3. "Decode the listing": cut to the four-line version (fenced property is not a yard, gated deck is not a fenced yard, enclosed patio, secure is an opinion). Delete both cabin-operator paragraphs and the Airbnb host-guidance paragraph; keep the single line "ask even when the filter says pets allowed."
4. Move "Request a small evidence pack" (checklist plus decision table) to straight after the opening. It is the screenshot-able core.
5. Keep "Give gates their own inspection" but cut to the gap, the latch video, and the cleaner/driveway three bullets.
6. Pens: collapse the two paragraphs into one sentence each ("MidWest pen for a dog that stays put, sizes 24 to 48 in; Carlson metal for small or medium, 28 in, low for a jumper") with the `{{pick}}` lines unchanged, and drop anchor/warranty details. Keep "ask the host first" and "none of these holds a jumper, climber or digger."
7. "Special cases": keep one line each for shared yards, invisible fences, pools.
8. Cut "Next step" to two links.

**Proposed opening (about 70 words).** "A photo of a lawn tells you nothing about where the fence ends or how wide the gap under the gate is. Before you pay, ask the host for a sketch of the whole enclosed area, tape-measure photos of the lowest fence section and widest gap, and a short video of each gate closing. 'Partially fenced' means leash-only, and an invisible fence is not a fence. Then walk the boundary yourself before the dog goes off leash."

**Do not cut.** "Never install stakes... without permission"; pool and hot tub separation; "an invisible or electric fence is not a physical enclosure"; leash first and perimeter walk on arrival; "a repair or refund is not automatic" backup plan; CPSC line scoped as child guidance; the "can't leave the dog alone" next-step link.

## Pattern summary

- **Cite-everything voice.** The most common smell across the set is "[Source] says X" attached to common-sense advice (reactive dog, senior dog, Christmas hazards, NYE, large dogs). The fix is the same everywhere: state the instruction in the friend voice, keep one link where the claim could be disputed, and drop the paraphrase of the source. Sources lists and frontmatter stay untouched.
- **Method and caveat notes ahead of advice.** "Specs below come from each maker's page, read on...", "convenience sample, not a survey", "we could not confirm", "a small sample, so views, not rules", "this is not legal advice". Most can be a link or a single trailing line; the caveats that protect a decision (ADA scope, tether laws unresearched, Twiddy's ten homes) stay once.
- **Worked examples with made-up numbers.** The fees guide (two tables plus break-evens) and the no-fee guide ($920 vs $880) both argue the same point twice. One example per point is enough.
- **Source walk-throughs of named managers.** The no-fee guide, large-dog guide, senior-dog guide and Christmas booking section tour individual managers where a decision rule or a table row would do. Keep the dataset numbers that decide something (35 to 65 lb weight limits, 3 / 22 / 13 fees, $20 to $50 nightly) and drop the per-page prose.
- **The best content is late.** Evidence pack (fenced yard), first-day plan (reactive dog), copy-and-send message (fees, no-fee) and "plan the evening" patterns were all found at the 55 to 90 percent mark in guides where the reader needs them first. Moving them up costs nothing.
- **Same rule restated in dek, opening, short answer, section, checklist and FAQ.** Seen in fenced-yard, reactive-dog, fees and no-fee guides.
- **Product density.** Eleven `{{pick}}` entries in the two-dogs guide, five in the loose-dog guide, four-plus in the checklist, reactive-dog and Christmas guides. Picks are properly labelled with who should skip; the issue is the surrounding maker-spec paragraphs (stabilizer counts, 146.5 inch gate width, folded size caveats), which the pick cards already carry.
- **What is working.** Numbered "do this first" openings (senior dog, loose dog), checkboxes (checklist, fenced yard), copy-and-send messages (every guide), do/skip tables and "skip it if" language on products. These are the house style the others should move toward.
- **Voice.** No invented personal experience was found in any of the 12 guides. The remaining voice problem is the lecture register from stacked attributions, not fake friendliness.
