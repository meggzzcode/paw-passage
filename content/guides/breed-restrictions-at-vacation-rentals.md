You found a cabin with a "pets welcome" badge, paid attention to the pet fee, and then, on the manager's separate pet-policy page, found a list of banned breeds. Or there is no breed list at all, just a weight limit your dog is a few pounds over. This guide is for catching that before you pay.

The short version: hosts and managers set their own pet rules, the weight cap is more common than the breed list, and a rule you only find after booking is a rule you can walk away from only if you asked in writing first. Below are 14 real pet policies, what the platforms say, and a message to send.

## The short answer

- Hosts and managers set their own pet rules. Airbnb's host pages talk about how many pets and what types, and say nothing about breed.
- The managers who explain a breed list point to insurance.
- In our 14 policies, weight caps (35 to 60 lb) were more common than named breeds. A cap can keep out a big dog of any breed or mix without naming one.
- "Pet friendly" does not mean your dog passes. The breed or weight line often sits on a separate policy page or in the house rules.
- Get the answer in writing and screenshot the listing's pet wording when you book.

## Can a vacation rental refuse my dog because of its breed?

In the policies we read, yes. Nothing we read gave a traveler a right to bring a dog the host did not approve, apart from service animals (covered below).

Airbnb's ground rules say guests must not bring pets into a listing with a "no pets" rule or "more pets than are approved." [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2894) Its hosting page lets hosts set a limit of 1 to 5 pets and says the pet fee is built into the nightly price; breed is not mentioned. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463) Vacasa says limits are "clearly stated at the top of each listing" and that only dogs authorized in the reservation are allowed. [[3]](https://www.vacasa.com/pet-friendly-vacation-rentals)

**Vrbo.** Vrbo's house-rules help page tells hosts to say whether guests can bring pets, "how many, what type of pet, and any size restriction." Breed is not on that list. [[4]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-House-Rules) That matches what we saw in manager policies: breed and weight rules live in the host's own text, not in a platform setting. One host on a host-side forum said the pet field in Vrbo's house rules is capped at 50 characters and was cut off (2024), which is one report, but a good reason to screenshot what you saw. [[5]](https://www.ownerrez.com/forums/general-help/issue-with-vrbo-house-rules-for-pets)

So "Airbnb bans certain breeds" is not what the pages say. The rule you hit is usually the host's or the manager's.

## Why do vacation rentals restrict breeds?

The managers who explain their lists usually cite insurance:

- Costanoa: "Due to our property insurance policy breed restrictions must apply." [[6]](https://www.costanoa.com/stay/pet-policies)
- Beach Realty: "certain breeds considered dangerous by insurance companies are not permitted." [[7]](https://www.beachrealtync.com/node/707)

Insurers are not all alike. Proper, a specialist insurer, says its policy "does not have exclusions for types of animals or dog breeds." That is marketing copy, not policy wording, but it suggests a host with a different insurer is not necessarily forced into a ban. [[8]](https://www.proper.insure/blog/pros-and-cons-of-allowing-pets-at-your-rental/) Best Friends, an advocacy group, says insurers can act on a pet's appearance and that a 2022 NCOIL model act bars action "based solely on the specific breed." [[9]](https://bestfriends.org/node/679665) Whether that act reaches vacation-rental host policies is listed in the unknowns table below.

A rule is about a category, not your individual dog, and nothing here says any breed is safe or dangerous.

## Which breeds are on the lists, and what about weight?

When a manager publishes a breed list it can be long: Elk Springs names 12 breeds. But the wall is often a number. In the 14-row table below, 6 policies have a weight cap of 35 to 60 lb and 2 name breeds. Cabin Rentals of Georgia says only "certain breeds" without naming them, and Cabins of the Smoky Mountains lists no breed but caps combined dog weight at 60 lb.

**Our inference:** a cap in the 35 to 60 lb range can exclude many large dogs, shepherds, retrievers, hounds and mixes alike, without any breed being named. So when you read "no breed restrictions," read the weight line too. In our wider 38-manager dataset (a convenience sample, read 2026-11-18; see the [pet-policy data page](/data/pet-policies)), only 5 mentioned breed limits and only 2 named breeds. Both samples are small, so use them as a reason to read your own listing, not as odds.

**Two dogs: watch for combined limits.** Row 8 caps dogs at "60 lb total." One host posted a scheduled guest message setting "the maximum combined pet mass" at 36 kg (80 lb) and a maximum of 2 pets (2021). That is one host's own limit, not an Airbnb rule, but it shows a combined cap can hide in a guest message. [[10]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/airs-new-pet-policy/50459?page=4) With two dogs, add the weights and check whether the cap is per dog or combined. Our [how-many-dogs guide](/guides/how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental) covers counts.

## What happens if I bring a dog nobody approved?

It can cost you. Policies in the table above allow extra charges, doubled fees or removal, and a host may try to end the stay at arrival. The full picture, with amounts and what hosts and platforms say, is in our guide to [bringing a dog to a no-pets rental](/guides/bringing-a-dog-to-a-no-pets-rental). If a charge shows up after checkout, see the [damage-charge guide](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout).

## What if my dog is a mix and nobody can tell the breed?

Costanoa's list covers crosses of the breeds it names, which is the one clear answer we found on mixes. For everyone else, put the question to the host: how do you treat mixes, and who decides? Describe your own dog accurately on the booking, because the reservation depends on the dog you describe.

## What about service animals?

Service animals are not pets. Airbnb's ground rules say service animals "are not considered pets," and the hosting page says a host cannot charge a pet fee for one. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2894) [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463) Where the ADA applies, DOJ says a fee or deposit required of pet owners must be waived for a service animal. [[19]](https://www.ada.gov/resources/service-animals-2010-requirements/) This is general information, not legal advice, and it is not a loophole: do not describe a pet as a service animal to get around a rule.

## What should I send the host?

Advice pages suggest vet letters, training certificates, a Canine Good Citizen certificate or a DNA test. We read no host or traveler account of any of them winning an exception at a vacation rental, so treat them as things you can offer, not things known to work.

Copy, paste, and fill in the brackets:

> Hi [Host], we are interested in [listing] for [dates]. We would like to bring [name], a [weight] lb [breed or mix], [age], who is spayed/neutered, current on vaccines, house-trained and crate-trained. Could you tell me whether your insurance or house rules restrict breed or weight, the total pet fee, whether the yard is fenced, and whether other dogs live at or visit the property? Does your rule cover mixes, and would you consider any documents for an exception? I can send vaccination records and a short reference. We would like your reply in writing before we book, and the pet rule to appear in the listing or house rules.

No reply? Ask once more through the booking platform and hold off on paying. If the answer is no, ask whether a sister property takes your dog. The [will-my-dog-be-accepted tool](/tools/will-my-dog-be-accepted) can help you line up your dog's details before you write.

## How do I find places that say yes?

- Use the platform's pet filter, then read the house rules and the manager's pet page for breed and weight lines. Twiddy, for example, has no manager-wide breed rule but lets individual homes set limits on "number, size, or type." [[16]](https://www.twiddy.com/outer-banks/guests/pets/)
- The National Dog Inclusive Rental Database lists properties with no breed or weight limits and calls its entries "leads" to confirm; it does not say whether vacation rentals are included. [[20]](https://www.mypitbullisfamily.org/housing/)
- Our [large-dog rental guide](/guides/vacation-rentals-that-welcome-large-dogs) and [Airbnb versus Vrbo guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work) cover size and platform questions.

## Before you book

- [ ] Read the listing's house rules and the manager's separate pet policy for breed names, weight and pet-count lines.
- [ ] Find out whether insurance or house rules cause the restriction, and how mixes are treated.
- [ ] Get the full pet charge and deposit, using the [pet cost check](/tools/pet-cost-check).
- [ ] Send the message above and keep the reply in writing.
- [ ] Look up the destination's local animal rules.
- [ ] Save the listing, the reply and the confirmation, then add the rest to the [trip checklist](/tools/checklist) and our [rental checklist](/guides/pet-friendly-vacation-rental-checklist).
