If your dog greets every day at full volume, you already know that another lap of the block is rarely the whole answer. Lively dogs often have plenty to give, and they are usually happiest when the day mixes movement with something for the brain to chew on.

## The short answer

For a high-energy dog, balance. Pair some movement with scent games, simple puzzles and short training sessions, and finish with rest. More running is not automatically better.

## Six ideas

**1. A flirt-pole session.** A toy tied to a rope on a pole taps the chase instinct. ASPCA's guide shows a simple homemade version. Keep sessions short, use a non-slip surface and let your dog catch the toy often.

**2. A search game.** Hide treats and toys around a room or yard and let your dog hunt. Make it harder as they get better.

**3. A tug-and-pause game.** Play tug with clear rules: begin, pause, trade the toy for a treat and begin again. This practices self-control inside the fun.

**4. Backyard obstacle fun.** Use low items like a cushion, a broom handle on the ground and a tunnel of boxes. Keep heights low and reward every try.

**5. A work-for-it meal.** Spread a meal in a muffin tin, a snuffle mat or a box puzzle so eating becomes a job.

**6. A sniff-and-go walk.** Alternate brisk stretches with a "free sniff" section at the end. Slowing down is part of the game.

## Practical considerations

Check heat before any active game and bring water. Ask your veterinarian when a young dog is ready for running, jumping or long outings, since growing joints need care. Avoid slippery floors and hard turns, and watch for panting that does not ease, limping or lagging. A sudden change in energy or behavior is worth a veterinary call.

## Your dog, your pace

Not every energetic dog wants the same thing. A scent-lover may prefer a search to a chase, and a retriever may want a job. Rest is part of the plan. With more than one dog, make sure each can take part comfortably, and keep toys and food games apart so nobody guards. Whichever ideas you try, let your dog stop when they want to.
