# Independent reader review: dog-friendly-cabin-packing-list

Method: Playwright Chromium at 390 and 1280 px against http://localhost:4179; first-screen and full-page screenshots viewed (page is 10,747 px tall at 390, 7,814 px at 1280); text and all links extracted; external links fetched; FAQ and Sources opened. No prior reviews read. No user testing was done; these are my observations as a reader.

## Scenario: ten minutes to pack for a weekend

- First screen at 390: title, subtitle ("A phone-friendly checklist for a two- or three-night stay..."), hero photo, Print/Copy/Pinterest buttons, and the top of the affiliate notice. No checklist yet.
- The "Essential packing checklist" heading sits at y=1334 px at 390 (about 1.6 screens down), behind the photo, the disclosure, a collapsed "On this page" and an 80-word intro paragraph ("Cabin weekends fail on small things... Before you load the car, confirm four things with the rental..."). A hurried reader scrolls roughly 1,300 px before a single checkbox. At 1280 the first screen is only title plus photo.
- Once reached, the checklist is good: four short groups (Food and water, Walks and ID, Papers and safety, Sleep and cleanup), 25 real checkboxes, one line per item. "Everything here goes in the car" versus "Pick the lines that match your booking. These are situation-dependent: skip the ones that don't apply" separates essentials from optional clearly. "What can stay home" is a useful, rare section.
- Gear appears only once: a single towel card (Soggy Doggy Super Shammy) introduced with "An old towel does the job; a purpose-made one... is a convenience, not a requirement." Gear is shown only where needed.
- The packing tool is linked only at y=7554 ("The packing list tool turns your dog, dates and season into a checklist") and in "What next?" at the very bottom. For this scenario the tool is the fastest route and it is buried near the end. There is no tool link near the top or beside the essentials.

## Roles

1. First-time search visitor: title matches intent; subtitle promises phone-friendly. Answer is there but delayed by the pre-roll. Hero photo is a dog in boots on a bench in sun, not a cabin; decorative.
2. Mobile traveler planning: lists render well at 390, no horizontal overflow (scrollWidth 390), no tables. Section order (essentials, cabin changes, drive, sleep/food/cleanup, seasonal, stay-home, tool, final check) is logical. But "What to bring for the drive" and "Final departure check" re-list items already ticked in the essentials.
3. Skeptical reader: one product, honest framing, maker source link beside it, Amazon link is the only commerce. Safety content is careful (pressure-mounted gate at stairs, bear-proof wording, ASPCA fee caveat, mothballs/rodent bait). The bear-country material is cited to BearWise and NPS inline.

## Observable problems

Repeated answers
- Packing core re-listed three times: Essentials ("Bowls and water for the drive", "Any medication...", "Waste bags") then drive list ("Water, bowl, treats and waste bags", "Medication and the offline folder") then departure check ("Food, medication, leash, ID and offline folder in the car", "Bed, towels and waste bags packed").
- FAQ #1 "What should I pack for a dog at a cabin?" restates the whole list in one sentence.
- Bear-proof container is answered four times: body ("Treat pet food as scented... an ordinary bin or cooler is not a bear-proof container"), stay-home bullet ("A bear-proof container you do not need: many cabins have indoor storage"), FAQ "Can I leave dog food on the cabin deck in a sealed bin?" and FAQ "Do I need a bear-proof container for dog food at a cabin?" (second repeats "An ordinary bin or cooler is not bear-proof.").
- Pressure-mounted gate warning appears in the body and again almost verbatim in the FAQ ("Do not use a pressure-mounted gate at the top of stairs. Use a stair gate only if its maker approves it for that position, which usually means hardware mounting").
- Furniture/pet fee: body ("A pet fee does not mean furniture permission") and FAQ ("A pet fee does not establish furniture permission").
- "Confirm with the host" appears in the intro, in the cabin-changes section, in the FAQ and again as the first departure-check item.

Padding / length
- 6 FAQ entries are nearly all recaps; perceived length is 10.7k px at 390 for a "6 min read" checklist. Cut the FAQ to 2-3 or remove those that repeat.
- Intro paragraph before the checklist is 80 words of framing.

Voice
- Mostly natural and concrete. Mild stock lines: "Waste bags, more than you think", "Cabin weekends fail on small things". Minor.
- Disclosure sentence "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" switches from "we" to "I".
- Sources intro "We research from published sources; we have not stayed at, inspected or tested anything covered here" is slightly defensive but placed correctly (collapsed Sources).

Tables
- None. Good for 390.

Product card
- One card, preceded by a reason in the paragraph. "Why we picked it: For wiping paws and legs at the door...; the maker says to machine wash it alone, without bleach or softener" is fine. The card's "A MICROFIBER PAW-DRYING TOWEL, 31 X 14 IN" label wraps awkwardly to a second line ("IN" orphaned) at 390. Card does not interrupt the checklist (it sits in prose).

Sources / citations
- Inline publisher links present (ASPCA, AVMA, BearWise, National Park Service, CDC, Cornell). The AVMA paragraph "The AVMA travel brochure recommends current ID tags..." is a good example. "Show the 7 sources" list is complete and dated. Two "ASPCA" style parentheticals ("(ASPCA)") duplicate the in-text link slightly.

Disclosure
- Single top notice, Amazon sentence present, "How we earn money" link, no per-card labels. Clear.

Links
- All internal links return 200. External: all 200 except cdc.gov (403, bot block, not a confirmed dead link). Amazon link present with tag.

Safety / facts
- No factual errors noticed. The ASPCA number 888-426-4435 is correct. "Medication... written doses" fine. One omission worth considering: nothing on checking the destination's leash/vaccine requirements; acceptable for scope.

## Verdict: READY WITH MINOR FIXES

1. Surface the checklist sooner on mobile: move the intro under the checklist or trim to two lines, and place a "Pack your dog's bag" tool link right under the Essentials heading.
2. Cut or shorten the FAQ (bear-proof asked twice plus the deck bin question; gate and furniture repeats).
3. Fold "What to bring for the drive" duplicates out of the essentials or mark them as "in reach" only; trim the departure check to items not already listed.
4. Make the disclosure voice consistent ("we", not "I").
