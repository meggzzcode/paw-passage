## Editorial experience
- **Paws & Passage:** Strongest on structure and verifiability: TOC, tables, dated sources, review dates, explicit "not tested". Reads like a careful explainer. Risk is density and a clinical voice; little personality or first-hand experience. Few images in the body; mobile suitability is plausible (short paragraphs, anchored TOC) but 9-minute guides with several tables may be heavy on phones (unverified).
- **GoPetFriendly:** Personality and first-hand travel are the draw (named author "Amy", anecdotes, photos with credits). Scannable via many sub-headings, but no TOC, no tables, no sources list. Dated: closure post says AI trip tools cut traffic; a "Session expired" login dialog showed up in the fetched HTML.
- **TripsWithPets:** Thin, generic content (a 2-minute moving guide with no sources, odd author handle "kim35784"). Useful as a directory/tool site, weak as editorial. Category label typo "Partnes" on the blog page.

## Commercial presentation
- **Paws & Passage:** Disclosure early (under byline, before TOC), repeated in the gear section and footer; separate /affiliate-disclosure page. Few products (3 and 4), each "Our pick" with rationale and "not tested" note; one affiliate Vrbo search link plus official help-page cites. Trust pages: editorial standards, methodology, corrections, "Not confirmed" labels, sample size stated (38 managers). Buttons say "See it at Amazon".
- **GoPetFriendly:** No affiliate-relationship notice in article bodies; only a footer "not a booking agent, no service fees" disclaimer. Partner post (Red Roof) reads like a press release, with no "sponsored" label; the discount is disclosed in asterisked footnotes and is expired (2021) yet still published.
- **TripsWithPets:** Affiliate-looking Vrbo link in footer via a tracking domain (kqzyfj.com) with no disclosure seen; "Trusted Travel Partners" posts (Vrbo, Red Roof, Raw Paws) are partner content with no explicit sponsorship label in the content seen; "Advertise With Us" in footer. Hotel pages push "Book your stay" CTAs.

## Overall impression for a 25-35 traveler
Paws & Passage looks the most trustworthy and the most useful for a booking decision, but the least fun. GoPetFriendly feels most human and trip-inspiring but is stale and loosely sourced. TripsWithPets feels thin and ad-adjacent. BringFido and Rover could not be assessed.

## What makes each feel professional or authentic
- **P&P:** Visible sourcing, review dates, honest "not tested", disclosure first, tables that answer the question.
- **GoPetFriendly:** A real named person, specific local detail, credited photos, candour about the site's own closure.
- **TripsWithPets:** Clear nav and trip-planner tool; brand pet-policy pages state specifics ("no additional pet fee").

## Friction
- **P&P:** Long pages, repeated TOC and disclosure add scroll; Amazon-heavy picks on pages about fees; hedging can blunt the answer; no in-body photos.
- **GoPetFriendly:** No TOC or tables for long pages; no affiliate disclosure; expired offers left live; login dialog in HTML; dynamic "Load More" lists.
- **TripsWithPets:** Short unsourced posts, undisclosed tracked links, typos, inconsistent authorship.

## Patterns P&P should adapt
1. First-hand voice and real photos (GoPetFriendly): add a named author, one personal trip anecdote per guide, credited in-body images with captions.
2. Venue-style "name - pet policy" lists for stays and destinations; give hotel fees and weight limits in one line.
3. Short summary at the very top as a true boxed callout (currently a plain section), plus a lighter one-line "last reviewed" near it.
4. Keep the TOC but show it once, collapsible on mobile, instead of repeating it.
5. Brand/stay pages stating policy in one sentence, as TripsWithPets does, linked from guides.
6. Refresh dates and expire stale offers on a schedule; show "Updated" prominently.

## Patterns to avoid copying
- Undisclosed affiliate/tracking links (TripsWithPets) and unlabelled partner posts (GoPetFriendly).
- Press-release style sponsored content without a "sponsored" tag.
- Thin 350-word guides with no sources or bylines.
- Unconfirmed claims stated as fact; leaving expired discounts live.
- Promo-code banners in page titles (seen in BringFido search snippet; unverified on the page).

## Honest comparison of the two P&P guides
Both guides are the best-sourced pages in this set (10 and 16 cites, access dates, primary sources such as DOJ ADA and Center for Pet Safety). Weaknesses relative to peers: heavy repeated TOC/disclosure blocks, few images, and a cautious voice with no first-hand testing, which the guides openly admit. They should add real photos, a clearer boxed answer, and a stronger human byline before comparing them to larger brands.
