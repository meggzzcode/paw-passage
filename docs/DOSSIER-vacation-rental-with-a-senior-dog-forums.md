## 2. Patterns (3 or more independent sources)

Reminder: small, non-random sample. "Independent" here means different threads by different posters; where several threads sit on one forum this is flagged.

**P1. Stairs and bare floors are a footing problem, and traction is the common answer (4 threads, 2 forums).**
Sources 2 (rubber bath mats), 3 (traction strips), 4 (traction on bare treads), 17 (carpet gave better footing; hardwood slippery for heavy older dogs). Caveat: 2, 3 and 4 are all Tripawds threads about three-legged dogs, so they are not fully independent of each other. This matches the draft's rug and mat advice.

**P2. Carrying a dog up stairs is limited by weight and by the dog (4 threads, 2 forums).**
Source 2 (50 lb dog, carry ruled out), 3 (carrying worked only for dogs under about 40 lb; the 80 lb dog was lifted a step or two at a time), 4 (dog growled when carried; leash-pulling backfired), 17 (owner lifts a 60 lb dog onto a bed; sling suggested). Caveat: three are Tripawds. This supports the draft's "do not assume you can carry a large dog".

**P3. Hosts expect accidents to be reported and cleaned up by the guest, and keep supplies (3 threads, 1 forum).**
Source 7 (cleaner and old towels kept for accidents), 8 (rules: report accidents, guests clean up), 9 (most guests grab a bag or mop). All Airhosts Forum, all hosts, 2017 to 2020. Caveat: a single forum.

**P4. Dog urine odor and staining can persist and is hard to fully remove (4 threads, 3 contexts).**
Sources 5 and 6 (long-term landlords; permanent spots, a $16,000 quote disputed), 7 (host: marked spots get marked again), 12 (guests: cottages that smelled of dog). Contexts differ: 5 and 6 are landlords, not short-term hosts; 12 is about general dog smell, not urine.

**P5. Incontinence in old dogs is common, and owners adapt or stay home (3 threads, 2 forums).**
Source 9 (host mentions pads for older dogs), 10 (owners: some skip trips because of incontinence), 11 (owners and a vet's remark that leaking is common in later life; vet bed, medication, bedding checks). Caveat: 10 and 11 are both Mumsnet, and 9 is a host's passing remark, not a guest's trip report.

**P6. Furniture: dog owners bring their own blanket or throws, and hosts supply or require them (3 threads, 2 forums).**
Source 1 (host supplies throws), 8 (rule: bring a blanket), 12 (guest brings own blanket). The draft already includes "your own sheet or blanket".

## 3. Warnings in these sources that the draft does not cover

Checked against content/guides/vacation-rental-with-a-senior-dog.md and docs/CLAIMS-vacation-rental-with-a-senior-dog.md.

1. **Open-backed, narrow or steep stairs** (3, 4). The draft asks how many flights and whether stairs are bare or carpeted, but not riser type, width or steepness. Single-forum report, but easy to add to the photo list.
2. **Waterproof protection and incontinence supplies.** The accident kit lists towels, paper towels, bags and a sheet. It has no waterproof pads, vet bed, washable layers or diaper or belly-band mention (9, 10, 11; pattern P5). Nothing in the draft says what to do for night-time leaks specifically; the "accidents" mention is only a vet prompt.
3. **Telling the host about incontinence in advance.** The draft's message asks how accidents are handled but never says the dog may leak. Host sources show hosts expect accident reports (8) and one host accepted pads for old dogs (9). No source says hosts want it disclosed before booking; that is an open question.
4. **Odor lasting after cleaning, and cleaner choice** (5, 6, 7, 11). The draft does not mention enzyme versus ammonia cleaners or that urine can soak past a surface. Evidence is mostly from landlords or a 2011 owner thread. Do not state a cleaner recommendation without a vet or manufacturer source.
5. **Host preference for hard floors** (7). Hosts say hard floors are easier to keep clean, which makes the slippery-floor problem the likely case in dog-friendly rentals. This sharpens the draft's floor advice.
6. **Who can make decisions if the dog is critically ill while you travel** (16). The draft covers saving the emergency vet's number but not leaving consent and contact details with whoever is caring for the dog at home or with the clinic, nor a plan for an end-of-life decision away from home.
7. **Sitters and fallback care** (10). Owners say it is hard to find someone to mind an elderly dog; the draft's table says "consider a sitter or boarding" without noting how hard that can be.
8. **Dogs refusing stairs they used before, and avoidance after a bad experience** (3, 4). Draft says to check the stairs, not what to do if the dog balks on arrival (for example, not forcing it).
9. **Platform protection and deposits vary** (8, 15). Hosts disagree on whether deposits are possible on Airbnb and say not to rely on platform protection. The draft only advises asking whether a deposit is involved. Whether any of this applies to a guest's liability is not answered by these threads.

## 4. Anything in the draft these sources contradict

No direct contradictions found.

Points of tension, not contradictions:
- Draft: "Is any deposit involved?" Source 8 has one host saying Airbnb does not allow a security deposit (Como, 2018) and another charging $250 (HelenSpinelli, 2018). The draft question is still fine, but the two hosts differ on how a deposit works on Airbnb, and this is from 2018 and unverified against current platform rules.
- Draft "Hardwood and tile can be slippery": consistent with 17, but host source 7 prefers hard floors. This is a tension in the market, not an error in the draft.
- Draft treats rules about furniture and unattended dogs as things to ask about. Source 12 (an owner) says such rules cannot be enforced, so the rule on the page may not describe what actually happens. No change needed, but the draft should not imply rules are policed.

## 5. Open questions we could not answer, and who to ask

1. Do hosts want advance notice that a dog is incontinent, and do any refuse bookings over it? Search snippets pointed to Airbnb Community and Airhosts remarks (one host saying they sometimes check that a very old dog is still continent), but those pages were not read. Ask: a vacation-rental manager or a host in a direct interview; Airbnb and Vrbo support for platform rules.
2. Can a host charge a cleaning fee or deposit for an accident the guest reported and cleaned? Ask: the specific manager or platform support; Airbnb or Vrbo pet-policy pages.
3. How do guests who did take a senior dog to a rental say it went? We found one trip (2) and none about incontinent or arthritic dogs in US rentals. Ask: owners via a survey, or a breed or senior-dog club.
4. Which cleaners and pads work, and are there safety issues? Ask: a veterinarian or manufacturer. Forum posts only.
5. Is there a safe way to bring a large dog up a flight of stairs (sling, harness)? Ask: a veterinary rehab specialist or a physical-therapy vet; the forum posts are anecdotes.
6. What should someone leave in writing for a carer if the dog becomes critically ill during a trip? Ask: your own vet, and the emergency clinic near the rental.
7. Airbnb Community, Vrbo community, Houzz and TripAdvisor remain unread for this topic. A person using a normal browser could check the Airbnb Community for senior-dog, incontinence and stairs threads.

## Summary

17 public threads read; 6 patterns reach 3 sources, but most are anecdotal and several lean on a single forum (Tripawds, Airhosts or Mumsnet).
