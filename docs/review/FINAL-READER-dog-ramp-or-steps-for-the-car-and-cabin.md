# Independent reader review: dog-ramp-or-steps-for-the-car-and-cabin

Method: Playwright Chromium at 390 and 1280 px; first-screen and full-page screenshots viewed (390 full page in 2400 px slices); body text and link list extracted. Internal links all returned 200. External publisher and Amazon links were NOT fetched (no claim made about them). FAQ answers are collapsed and were not expanded, so FAQ content is not judged. Page is 2,367 words, about 13,900 px tall at 390.

## Scenario: "my dog has difficulty getting into the car"
Yes, the reader finds the answer fast. The deck ("Most cars and SUVs suit a telescoping ramp; steps only suit a fixed door sill or hitch, so measure ground to floor first.") plus the intro and the "At a glance: our picks" list answer the question within the first two scrolls at 390. The size logic (cargo floor about 20 to 22 in vs 23 in or higher) is findable. The problem is that everything after that is the same answer again, in larger and more technical form.

## Observable problems
**First screen (390):** Title, deck, byline, hero, buttons and the affiliate notice are all above the fold; the first real sentence of body copy is below the notice and "On this page" box, i.e. roughly the second screen. The hero photo shows a dog sitting in a cargo area behind a barrier with no ramp or steps, so the picture does not say "ramp". The deck is clear and useful. Disclosure is a single top notice and includes the Amazon sentence, but it switches person: "we may earn a commission... As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases."

**Repeated answers (same facts 3 to 4 times):**
- "Compact ... 28 to 70 in, 300 lb" appears in At a glance ("PetSafe Happy Ride Compact Telescoping Ramp, 28 to 70 in, 300 lb maker rating"), the specs table ("300 lb / 28 to 70 in, 14 lb"), the lead-in paragraph, the card chips ("Extends 28 to 70 in; 14 lbs", "Maker: rated to 300 lb"), the card sentence ("A telescoping ramp that extends from 28 to 70 inches...") and "Why we picked it: 28 to 70 in extended, 28 in collapsed, 14 lb, 300 lb rating...". That is five restatements.
- The Extra Long ("47 to 87 in", "18 lb", "300 lb") and Folding ("62 x 16 x 4 in; 10 lbs", "150 lb") follow the same pattern.
- "Measure first": "so measure ground to floor first" (deck), "so measure first" (Before you buy), "Measure the vehicle before you buy" (section), "Then use the box at the top of this guide" (What to buy first).
- AKC's "ramps are easier to fold" appears in Before you buy and again in the vet section; AKC is cited about four times, VCA about three.
- "Ask your vet" appears in the intro, the Side Door Steps paragraph, the senior item in "What we could not confirm", and the vet section.

**Padding / perceived length:** "Before you buy" (two bullets) pre-empts the measuring section. "What vet and kennel-club sources say" (3 paragraphs) restates points already used in the intro and the buy section, and ends with "that exam is your vet's job, not a product's". "What we could not confirm" restates the "Skip it" and "not listed" caveats already placed on every product paragraph ("the page does not say how the steps attach", "folded size, which is not listed", "The page gives no adjustment range"). For a reader whose dog has trouble getting in the car, the cabin-stairs section is a tangent, though the host message is practical. 8 min / 13,900 px is long for this query.

**Tables:** One specs table, three columns, readable at 390 (no horizontal scroll; product names wrap to three lines). It is not necessary: it duplicates At a glance and the cards. The most useful comparison (max ramp-reach height by length) is buried in prose in the "arithmetic" paragraph ("about 19 in for the 62 in Folding Ramp, 22 in for the Compact at 70 in and 27 in for the Extra Long at 87 in") and is not tabulated, so the table that exists is the less useful one. The key size consideration ("how high is your floor") gets one sentence of reasoning and a technical sine formula.

**Voice:** Mostly plain and direct ("Skip it if you cannot lift 18 lb"). Defensive/hedging trust language: "We are not vets, and this is not advice for your dog. These are the statements we read."; "Every figure is what the maker's page said on 2026-11-18, and the ratings are the makers' own."; "The three ramp pages say the rating was independently tested and name no tester"; "Matching the top step to the sill is our reasoning, not the maker's instruction." Individually fair, collectively the page spends many sentences disclaiming. Raw ISO date "2026-11-18" reads as data, not copy. "(PetSafe, PetSafe)" and "(PetSafe)" after nearly every claim are citation clutter.

**Product cards:** Each card is preceded by a paragraph with a reason and a Skip-it condition, so none are reasonless, and none interrupt instruction steps. But each card then re-lists the specs just stated, and the product icon is a generic triangle glyph, not a photo, so the cards look like placeholders. All five are the same brand ("All five are PetSafe, the only brand whose pages we read"): honest, but a skeptical reader sees one brand and Amazon buttons only, with no alternative maker for comparison. No per-card affiliate labels (good).

**Sources:** Inline publisher links are present (AKC, PetSafe, Petful, CARE, VCA, Cornell, PetMD), and an 11-item collapsed "Show the 11 sources". Each card has both a prose "(PetSafe)" link and a "Source" link to the same URL, which is duplicate attribution. Good that figures are traceable.

**Facts/safety:** Cautious and consistent: vet referral for stiff or sore dogs, "A dog who wobbles on it is a sign to call your vet, not to push on." Slope figure is attributed as one behaviorist's number, not a standard. Nothing wrong spotted. The statement "Most cars and SUVs suit a telescoping ramp" is the editors' judgement, not sourced.

**Layout:** No horizontal overflow at 390 (scrollWidth 390). Cards stack cleanly. No glitches seen.

## Role walk-throughs
1. First-time search visitor: gets the answer from the deck and At a glance quickly; the rest feels like a research dossier.
2. Mobile trip planner: measuring checklist and host message are useful; the ramp-height arithmetic and table are heavy on a phone; cabin section helpful.
3. Skeptic: likes the maker-rating caveats and the "could not confirm" list; dislikes single-brand coverage, five Amazon buttons, and a page that repeatedly says it has not tested anything.

## Verdict: READY WITH MINOR FIXES
1. Cut the repeat: drop "Before you buy" or the vet-section AKC repetition; remove the "Why we picked it" line or the card chips (pick one); keep the specs only once.
2. Replace or fold the specs table into the "arithmetic" content: a small table of "ramp / reach at about 18 degrees / floor height it suits" would answer the reader's real question; drop the duplicate weight table.
3. Shorten "What we could not confirm" to items not already stated at the product, or remove per-product repeats.
4. Make the Amazon sentence first-person plural ("we earn") and drop the ISO date and "(PetSafe, PetSafe)".
5. Use a hero showing a ramp/steps if available; swap triangle icons for photos.
6. Consider trimming the vet-source section to 3 to 4 sentences.
