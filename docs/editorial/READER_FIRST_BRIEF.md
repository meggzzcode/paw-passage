# Reader-first brief: advice from a friend, not an encyclopedia (owner direction, 2026-11-25)

The owner's words, kept as the test: readers care about **what to know, what to bring, and how to take care of their pet on a trip so everyone is happiest and things are easy**. Going heavily into background, studies and detail that does not change what they do is not what they want. "We're not here to be an encyclopedia. We're here to give the information they actually want and make it feel like a friend giving advice."

## The friend-advice test (apply to every guide)
1. **First screen.** In the first ~150 words the reader gets the answer, what to do, and why it matters, in plain words. No scene-setting, no definitions, no "it is important to".
2. **The three reader questions.** Can the reader find, in order: WHAT TO KNOW (the few facts that change a decision), WHAT TO BRING (a short, concrete list or the one or two products that actually matter), HOW TO CARE FOR THE DOG (the practical steps that keep the dog comfortable and safe on this kind of trip)? A guide that is missing the one it should have, or buries it, fails.
3. **Decision value.** Every section must change what the reader does or chooses. A section, table, statistic, source walk-through, definition, history or caveat that does not change a decision is an encyclopedia smell: cut it, shrink it to one line, or move it below the actionable part ("evidence after advice").
4. **Encyclopedia smells to hunt:** long background before the advice; paragraphs that summarise a source; statistics with no 'so what'; stacked hedges and repeated caveats; tables of data not tied to a choice; 'studies show'; lists of every possible case; the same rule restated in the dek, opening, box, body, checklist and FAQ; headings that name a topic instead of a question or action; sentences that need re-reading.
5. **Voice.** Second person, contractions, short sentences, concrete and specific, calm and warm, a little personality where it fits, like a knowledgeable friend who has done this before saying "here's what I'd do". NO invented personal experience ('when I took my dog...', 'we tested'): friendly is not fake. Use 'most people', 'a common mistake', 'here's what usually works' only where supported. No jokes in medical, safety, emergency, legal or fee-dispute content.
6. **Make it easy.** Prefer a checklist the reader can screenshot, a message they can copy, a 'do this, skip that' table, and one clear next step over explanation. Products appear only where they make the trip easier, with 'who should skip it'.
7. **Length follows need.** Typical 900 to 1,600 words; a safety/research guide may run longer ONLY if the answer is at the top and the rest is skimmable. Cutting a third is a good outcome when the cut text did not change a decision.

## What must NOT be cut or weakened
- Safety and legal information that affects the dog's wellbeing or the reader's booking (heat, car restraint, first aid, emergencies, service-animal rules, fee/refund rights): keep it clear and early; trim only the surrounding explanation.
- Accuracy: no claim changes except removing or softening; never add a fact you have not read from a source; the Sources list and frontmatter `sources` stay intact (do not delete sources or change URLs); keep inline links only to listed sources; keep read dates.
- The 38-manager dataset findings that decide something (fees, limits): keep the number and the 'so what'; drop the method walkthrough to one line and a link.
- Disclosure and product-card systems (do not touch notices, `{{pick}}` syntax, `{{planner}}` directives).

## Output format for an audit (per guide, in your report file)
`### <slug> (<words> words, <status>)` then: **Verdict** (ON-TARGET / TRIM / REFRAME / REWRITE) with one sentence; **First 150 words** (does it answer? quote the opening and say what is wrong or right); **Know / Bring / Care** (present, buried or missing: one line each); **Encyclopedia smells** (3 to 6 specific, each with the heading or a short quote); **Voice** (friend or lecture? one example line of each problem); **Edit plan** (exact cuts and merges by heading, what to move below the fold, what to turn into a checklist or one-liner, target word count, a proposed rewritten opening of 40 to 90 words in the friend voice that uses only facts already in the guide); **Do not cut** (safety/legal items to keep). End the report with a ranked list of the guides most in need and an overall summary of patterns.
