# Demand and Rankability Research (Paws & Passage)

Researched 2026-11-17. Scope: ~28 target queries plus close variants, North Georgia and coastal California launch.

## 0. Method and hard limits (read first)

Evidence labels used throughout:

- **SEEN** = appeared in the search results I received, or on a page I fetched.
- **REPORTED** = stated by a source in the results but not verified against a primary source.
- **NOT CONFIRMED** = I could not see it with my tools.

What my tools can and cannot show:

- WebSearch returns a list of titles and URLs plus a short summary. It is not a live Google SERP. I did **not** see ranking order guarantees, "People also ask" boxes, featured snippets, AI Overviews, maps packs or rich results. Every "PAA / AI overview / rich result" cell below is therefore **NOT CONFIRMED**. Do not treat absence as evidence.
- "Who ranks" below means "who appeared in the result set for that query." Order is not reliable.
- **No search volume numbers exist in this document.** None of the sources gave any. Demand is inferred only from proxies: how many sites bother to target the query, whether many near-duplicate pages exist, and whether SEO-farm sites have been spun up for it.
- WebFetch was blocked (403) on gadnrle.org/ParkRules, bringfido.com, and returned an empty body for centerforpetsafety.org. Those are marked NOT CONFIRMED where I could not read them directly.
- Freshness: most official park pages show no "last updated" date (SEEN for gastateparks.org Tallulah Gorge and parks.ca.gov/dogs). NPS Joshua Tree pets page shows "Last updated April 18, 2024" (SEEN).

Cross-cutting observations (SEEN in result sets):

1. **Spam/AI-looking mirrors are common.** Result sets repeatedly included throwaway-looking domains: `pawventures.pages.dev`, `nestward.netlify.app`, `kalitalk.pages.dev`, `blog-hichee-com-git.pages.dev`, `fourcast-ebon.vercel.app`, `agerebackupgraphic.pages.dev` (a mirror claiming dogs are NOT allowed at Tallulah Gorge, which contradicts the official page). This signals both that the topic is attractive to SEO farms and that the engine is still surfacing low-trust pages. It is a weak positive for a trustworthy new site, but expect more of it.
2. **Big brands and publishers dominate "best / guide" queries**: NerdWallet, AKC, Dogster, Hepper, Rover, Kinship, Fodor's, Frommer's, Condé/Matador-type travel sites, Vacasa, Vrbo, Airbnb, BringFido, GoPetFriendly, AAA, Wag Walking.
3. **Official sources (state parks, NPS, airlines, hotel brands) own "rules" queries** but their pages are single-purpose, often undated, and not comparative. The gap is comparison, aggregation and dated verification, not original rules.
4. **No true "pet fee calculator" for guests surfaced.** The only calculator seen was Landlord Gurus' long-term-rental landlord tool (SEEN). A hotel-fee total-cost calculator did not surface either (SEEN: search returned only how-to-compute-it guides).
5. **Source quality is poor on money queries.** Example: hotelierschoice.com "Hotel Pet Fees by Brand (2026)", published 2026-08-14 (SEEN on fetch), has a brand table but cites no brand pages, is internally inconsistent (says flat fees are $50-$125 but lists La Quinta at $25-$40; puts a per-night fee at Best Western while calling nightly fees an upscale trait), and carries Booking.com affiliate links.

## 2. The 10 most winnable verified, dated lookup pages or tools

Ranked by (a) a visible content gap, (b) maintainability for a tiny team, (c) low YMYL risk, (d) fit with launch regions.

1. **Guest-side "Total pet cost" calculator (hotel and vacation rental)** (#1, #2, #3, #5, #29). Inputs: nights, number of dogs, fee model (per stay, per night, per pet, per pet per night, nightly cap), deposit. Output: total plus comparison of structures. Why: the search for "pet fee calculator vacation rental" returned only host-side pricing blogs and one landlord tool (SEEN). Forum answers (Airbnb Community) are thin and contradictory. Airbnb publicly supports four fee structures (REPORTED via help result), so the model is real. Data is easy to verify and does not rot.
2. **Georgia State Parks dog rules lookup (park-by-park, dated)** (#17, #11, #12). Why: official guidance is split across Park Rules, FAQ and per-park pages with no dates (SEEN); third parties are thin or wrong (agerebackupgraphic mirror says "no dogs" at Tallulah, SEEN). Exclusions (cottage-only, 2-dog max, trail closures, beaches and swim areas) are the high-value details. Hard part: I was 403'd on gadnrle.org/ParkRules, so build it from the primary pages and cite and date each.
3. **California State Parks dog rules, reorganized** (#18, #16). Official table exists (SEEN) but has no date and is not searchable by "what can I actually do" (paved road, campground, beach, trail). Pair with a coastal agency-keyed beach rules table (state/county/city) for launch coast regions only. Risk: beach conflicts across sources (Huntington, Arroyo Burro) mean each row needs a primary-source link and "last verified" date.
4. **Airline in-cabin pet policy table with per-airline source link and "checked" date** (#6, #7). Why: sources visibly contradict each other (Delta and American 20 lb in an older table vs none in a 2026 guide; Alaska 20 lb in one summary vs no cap in another). A dated, source-linked table is a clear differentiation. Cost: needs periodic re-verification, so scope to roughly 8-12 US carriers.
5. **Hotel chain pet fee and policy table linking to each brand's own pet-policy page** (#4, #5). Why: top table pages are unsourced (Hotelier's Choice) or stale (Matador 2018, PlanetWare 2021). Strongest framing is "brand-level policy page links and what is verified, not a claimed fee" since fees vary per property (SEEN in NerdWallet-style summaries).
6. **Hot-weather dog walk checker (temp + humidity + surface)** (#23). Competitive, but a programmatic competitor exists (Wyndo, SEEN) which shows the format ranks. Needs a cited primary method; the "temp + humidity >= 150" rule is REPORTED only and I did not verify it against a primary vet source, so do not ship it as fact until verified. UGA Extension chart was mentioned (REPORTED) as a better source.
7. **Leaving a dog in a car: state law lookup with plain-English fields** (#24). Source of truth exists (MSU Animal Legal & Historical Center, 2025, SEEN). Value-add is filters (rescue immunity yes/no, penalty class) and a "heat" explanation. Counts conflict (19 / 31 / ~32) so cite the table and state "not legal advice."
8. **"Questions to ask the host" message generator and checklist** (#26, #27). Low difficulty. Copy is thin in results. Include fee model questions, insurance-driven breed limits (REPORTED in results), indoor vs outdoor, fenced yard height, noise, and a copy-paste text for Airbnb/Vrbo messages.
9. **Rental pet limits by dog count and weight (two dogs / large dogs)** (#28, #29). A table of what specific managers (Twiddy, Brindley Beach, Westgate, Vacasa) state, with links. Lower leverage than #1 but low risk and fits the Outer Banks/Blue Ridge pattern; scope to Georgia and California managers for the launch.
10. **Fenced-yard cabin verification set (North Georgia)** (#20). Not a tool, but a lookup of properties with verified fence height, gate type, per-dog limits, and fee. Commercial intent is strong, competitors are listing pages that do not verify. Cost: first-hand or owner-confirmed data per property.

## 4. Could "Are dogs allowed at X" pages work as a programmatic set?

**Verdict: Yes, but only as a curated, verified set, not as a template-spun mass.** (My judgment, based on the result sets above.)

What I saw (SEEN) of who ranks for these queries:

- **Official park/agency pages** (gastateparks.org, parks.ca.gov, NPS) rank and are accurate. But they are bare: no date, no "how to do this with a dog" detail (Tallulah's official page has the pet restriction under a "Pet Notice" heading, not a pets section; parks.ca.gov has a table with no date). NPS Joshua Tree does carry "Last updated April 18, 2024" and clear rules.
- **Directory aggregators** (BringFido, GoPetFriendly, Wag Walking, The Dyrt, Campendium, DogTrekker, BarkParkFinder, TopDogParks) fill the rest. They use a template, rely on user reviews, and frequently show no policy date. I could not fetch BringFido (403) to check structure, NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Big dog/lifestyle brands** (Hepper, Dogster, A-Z Animals) cover national-park-type queries with near-identical text.
- **Spam mirrors** (pages.dev, netlify.app) are present and sometimes wrong.

Quality findings:

- Official answers conflict with third parties in several places: the Tallulah "no dogs" mirror; Arroyo Burro off-leash location disagreement; Red Top dog-park disagreement; campground counts at Joshua Tree; Yosemite trail claims.
- For Red Top Mountain and Arroyo Burro, **no authoritative park- or county-specific page appeared in the results**, which is the opening (third-party pages carry the query).
- For Joshua Tree and Channel Islands, NPS owns the answer. Differentiation must come from the "what to do instead" content (nearby BLM land, Faria Beach etc.), not repeating the rule.

Recommendation for programmatic use:

1. **Keep the set small and bounded**: Georgia State Parks (about 40 parks; cottage/trail/beach exclusions) and the California coastal parks and beaches in the launch region. Do not expand to all 63 national parks.
2. **Each page must carry what the thin competitors lack**: the rule stated in one sentence, source link to the managing agency, "Last verified" date, what is excluded (trails, beaches, buildings), leash length, nearest alternative that does allow dogs, heat and season note, and who to call. That is a structured, verifiable data record, which suits a static-site data file.
3. **Unique-value gate**: do not publish a page for any location where there is nothing beyond the generic rule. Thin pages across a new domain are a risk. Publish only where you add a real alternative or exception (e.g. Tallulah rim vs gorge floor; Channel Islands "no" plus mainland alternatives; Arroyo Burro on-leash vs the off-leash stretch with the conflicting descriptions resolved via a county source).
4. **Resolve conflicts explicitly** and cite which source you trust and why. Show the conflict when it exists. This is a trust signal competitors lack.
5. **Freshness process**: a "next review" date per record and a changelog. Without it, this set decays like the competitors.
6. Treat the question as likely to be answered directly by search features for the simplest facts (NOT CONFIRMED). Aim to win the click with the additional details above, not the yes/no.
