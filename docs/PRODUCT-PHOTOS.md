# Product photos: how to show them legitimately

Research date: 2026-11-17. This is research, not legal advice. Where a page could not be read or says nothing, it says NOT CONFIRMED. Nothing here was guessed.

## 1. Bottom line

- No brand on our list was found to publish a plain-language "bloggers may use our photos" licence. Not one. Assume every maker/retailer photo is copyrighted and needs permission.
- Two brands publish press/media asset pages (Sleepypod, Tractive), but neither page we could read states usage terms. A press page is a good place to ask, not a licence.
- The cleanest routes are: (1) Amazon's own SiteStripe image links, once we are an approved Associate and only while Amazon serves the image; (2) written permission; (3) our own photos of products we buy; (4) photo-free or illustrated placeholders.
- Do not copy, re-host or hotlink maker photos without permission.

## 3. Amazon

Which of our products Amazon sells is in docs/AFFILIATE-PROGRAMS.md (about 20 of 27, availability changes).

Program Policies (https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies), quoted from our fetch:
- "You will not store or cache Product Advertising Content consisting of an image"
- "you may store a link to Product Advertising Content consisting of an image for up to 24 hours."
- Product Advertising Content is "data, images, text, and other information and content relating to Products."
- "You will link each use of Product Advertising Content to, and only to, the relevant page of an Amazon Site"
- "Special Links must use the Associates ID we have assigned to you." "All Special Links must be accessed directly from your Site."
- Amazon's own marks: "You may not alter any Amazon Mark in any manner."
- The Product Advertising API (PA-API 5) "has been deprecated and is being replaced by the Creators API" (https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/paapiv5-deprecation). New work should expect Creators API; its image terms were NOT CONFIRMED.

Operating Agreement (https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement): "we may make available to you data, images, text, link formats, widgets, links, marketing content, and other linking tools" (Section 1). It covers only "any person or entity that participates or attempts to participate in our associate marketing program". It does not mention SiteStripe or caching itself.

SiteStripe (Associates Central help, via search snippet): "From any product page, you can create an image link by clicking on 'Image' and choosing the size you want." And: "For all of our link types, Amazon provides the graphics from its own servers." The SiteStripe toolbar appears only when signed in to the same account as Associates. A "Text + Image" option gives an HTML box with "the product name, the corresponding product image, and the current price".

What that means for us:
- Allowed route: approved Associate, image served from Amazon's servers via SiteStripe image link, linked to the Amazon page with our tag. Do not download or re-host Amazon images.
- Price: the Text + Image box shows a price. Our rule (no hand-typed prices) is only safe if the price is Amazon-served; an image-only link avoids the issue. Check before using.
- Only works for products Amazon lists, and only for the Amazon product's image, which is the brand's photo shown under Amazon's own licence. It does not give us the right to use that photo elsewhere.
- Images appear only while we are an approved Associate. The sales requirement (3 qualifying sales in 180 days, per our docs) must be met first.
- Whether SiteStripe image links satisfy "image next to a recommendation" for a product Amazon sells third-party only: NOT CONFIRMED.

## 4. General US legal position (not legal advice)

- Copyright exists "from the moment the work is created" (Copyright Office FAQ, https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html). A maker's product photo is protected without any registration notice.
- Fair use is decided case by case. Copyright Office: "Courts evaluate fair use claims on a case-by-case basis" and "Ultimately, only a federal court can determine whether a particular use is, in fact, a fair use". It lists four factors, including "Purpose and character of the use, including whether the use is of a commercial nature" and "Effect of the use upon the potential market for or value of the copyrighted work" (https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/). Our site earns commission, which counts against us on factor one; commentary/criticism are favoured purposes, but a plain "here is the product" photo is not commentary on the photo.
- Credit does not fix it. The Copyright Office pages we read never say attribution avoids infringement. They say "in cases of doubt, the Copyright Office recommends that permission be obtained" (https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html). Do not treat "photo: Brand" as a licence.
- Hotlinking (embedding the image from the maker's server) is genuinely unsettled. The Ninth Circuit's "server test" (Perfect 10 v. Amazon, 508 F.3d 1146) says showing an image hosted elsewhere is not the site's own "display"; later Ninth Circuit decision (Hunley v. Instagram, 2023) reaffirmed it. New York federal courts (Goldman v. Breitbart, 2018; Nicklen v. Sinclair, 2021) rejected the test and held embedding can be an infringing display (search summaries; I did not verify later appeals). The result depends on which court, and hotlinking also burdens the brand's server and can break. Ask a lawyer before relying on it. Re-hosting a copy is the riskiest option.
- Dealing with a brand that already granted a licence (press kit, written email) is different: a written permission removes the issue for the named images and uses.

## 5. Alternatives

### (a) Written permission (best for real photos)
Short email template (send from the site's address, one brand at a time, to the press/marketing contact found above):

> Subject: Permission to show your product photos in a dog-travel gear guide
>
> Hello [name / Press team],
>
> I run [site name], a small independent website with dog-travel gear guides. We recommend the [product name(s)] in [article title / URL] and would like to show your official product photo(s) next to the recommendation, with the caption "Photo: [Brand]" and a link to your product page.
>
> Could you confirm in writing that we may use [image(s) or press-kit link] on that page for this purpose? We will not alter the images, and will remove them promptly if you ask. If you prefer specific wording, a different image, or a press kit link with terms, please send them.
>
> Thank you, [name], [site], [email]

Keep the reply on file (email is fine) with the image names, dates and scope. Use only the images and uses named in the reply. Likeliest to say yes: brands with a media page (Sleepypod, Tractive) and affiliate-friendly brands (Kurgo, Fi, Petmate, AMK).

### (b) We buy the product and photograph it
Legitimate and also strongest for E-E-A-T. Own photos are ours. The site's honesty rules still apply: a photo of our own unit is the only case where it is truthful to imply we handled it. Cost is the product price plus time. Priority: lower-priced items (bowls, bottles, kits, ID tag).

### (c) Licensed stock photos of the CATEGORY
Unsplash licence (https://unsplash.com/license): "All images can be downloaded and used for free", for "Commercial and non-commercial purposes", with "No permission needed (though attribution is appreciated!)". The page says nothing on trademarks or brands in photos, so choose images without visible logos. Honest caption wording (our Editorial Standards forbid passing a generic photo off as the product):
- "Illustration only: a dog in a car harness. Not the product reviewed below."
- "Stock photo of a dog travel crate. Not the GUNNER G1."
Place it away from the product name line or visually mark it (smaller, labelled) so a reader cannot mistake it for the product. If we cannot make that clear, do not use stock.

### (d) Simple illustrated placeholders
Original drawings of a category (harness, crate, bowl) that we make ourselves, labelled "Illustration". No licence risk; do not draw logos or copy the product's exact design.

### (e) Official embeds/widgets
No brand on the list was found to offer an embed or widget for product images. The only official one found is Amazon's SiteStripe Text+Image/Image link (section 3). Others NOT CONFIRMED.

## 7. What we'll do (plain English, one page)

1. Do not copy or link to any maker's photo for now. Nobody on our list clearly allows it, and credit alone does not make it legal.
2. Send the permission email this month, starting with Sleepypod and Tractive (they already have media pages), then Kurgo, Fi, Petmate and Adventure Medical Kits (they have affiliate programmes), then the rest. Save every reply.
3. Join the affiliate networks (Awin, AvantLink, Pepperjam Ascend, Impact; Amazon once the site is live). After approval, read each brand's creative rules and use only what they hand out.
4. Once Amazon approves us, use Amazon's own image links for Amazon-sold products and never save Amazon images ourselves.
5. For a handful of cheap, central products, buy them and take our own photos.
6. Until a product has a real photo, show text only, or a clearly labelled illustration. Stock photos only with the caption "Illustration only, not the product."
7. Re-check in three months. Do not rely on any hotlinking or fair-use argument.

## Risks

- Permission replies may be slow, partial (limited images), or time-limited; keep the email and obey its terms.
- A permission or affiliate asset may be revoked; be ready to swap images fast.
- Amazon images are only usable through Amazon's tools while we are approved; losing the account (or a failed 180-day test) removes them. PA-API is being replaced by Creators API.
- Hotlinking law is split between circuits; a maker could still complain or claim damages. So we do not hotlink.
- Fair use is judged case by case and a commission-earning page weakens it.
- Unsplash and similar licences do not cover trademarks in the photo, and the legal "not the product" caption must be visible.
- Many terms in this file are NOT CONFIRMED (Awin US clause, AvantLink, Impact, Pepperjam terms, Kurgo guide PDF, Tractive and Sleepypod T&Cs). Some facts come from search snippets rather than full pages and should be re-read before acting.
- Brand contacts found in old press releases may be out of date.
