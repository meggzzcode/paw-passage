# Final editorial transformation brief (owner directive, 2026-11-24)

Source of truth for the final editorial workers. This is the last planned editorial pass: implement, validate, freeze. Do not produce another audit, do not start another rewrite cycle, do not chase perfect scores. An article is complete when its purpose is clear, the opening answers the reader, information flows without repetition, length fits the topic, important facts stay supported, products are useful rather than forced, mobile layout reads well, factual and functional checks pass, and no substantive reader-facing defect remains. A genuinely excellent 8.6 beats an inflated 9.4.

## Reader and voice
Dog owner aged about 25 to 35, on a phone, limited time, planning a cabin weekend, a six-hour drive, a holiday rental, two large dogs, a beach or lake trip, or a stay with an unclear pet policy. They will read long when the decision is expensive, complicated or safety-sensitive; they will not read 2,500 words for a 500-word answer. Voice: warm, specific, practical, confident, calm, contemporary, like helping a friend plan a first cabin trip: start with the answer, mention something they may overlook, give options, explain reasoning when it matters, recommend products only when relevant, never keep announcing trustworthiness. Never invent personal experiences, first-hand testing, host confirmation, ratings or prices. Do not write like an academic paper, generic affiliate blog, white paper, legal disclaimer, spec sheet, repetitive SEO article or AI summary.
Sentence rules: short, direct, specific nouns, useful verbs, natural contractions, second person, realistic travel situations. Scrutinize stock phrases ('It is important to note', 'When it comes to', 'comprehensive guide', 'Ensure that', 'Whether you are a seasoned traveler', 'In conclusion', 'Ultimately the choice depends', 'Always check with the host', 'We carefully researched', 'For peace of mind', 'Game changer', 'Must-have'); remove when generic. One idea per paragraph, varied rhythm, a little warmth where appropriate, never jokes in medical, safety, emergency, legal or fee-dispute content. Replace generic advice with specific, supported detail (do not invent rental rules).

## Opening of every article
H1: the reader's question in natural words, keep search intent. Dek: one short promise or takeaway (15 to 25 words) that adds something, not a restatement of the headline. Opening paragraph: 2 to 4 short sentences (about 40 to 90 words): the real situation, the main answer or direction, one useful distinction, a reason to keep reading; no long compound sentences. A short-answer box only when it adds decision guidance beyond the opening (first actions for emergencies, a decision summary for comparisons; unnecessary for a simple checklist). The first H2 advances the topic and does not repeat the opening. Acceptance test: read only H1, dek, opening and first H2: each adds new information; the answer is clear; no sentence could be cut without losing value. The first 150 to 250 words must earn the reader.

## Distinct purpose per article
Before rewriting define: primary reader question, desired outcome, unique value versus adjacent guides, required evidence, useful next step (guide, tool or product). Different intents get different layouts. Not every article is a product roundup, a rental-policy explainer or a packing guide. Adjacent guides must clarify their different purposes; link rather than re-explain. Do NOT merge or redirect any additional published page.

## Length (references, not quotas)
Quick answer 700 to 1,200 words; standard guide 1,000 to 1,700; product decision guide 1,200 to 2,000; complex comparison 1,500 to 2,400; original policy/data research as long as the evidence needs. Do not pad a short guide; do not gut a research guide. Method: core answer, unique details, repeated ideas, remove what does not serve the decision, consolidate repeated warnings, move supporting evidence after actionable guidance, keep essential sources and facts, check flow. A reader should be able to skim in one to two minutes.

## Repetition
After the rewrite do a dedicated redundancy pass: the same concept must not recur across dek, opening, short-answer box, headings, body, product recommendation, closing checklist and FAQ unless each repetition has a distinct purpose. State a rule once (e.g. 'get the pet fee in writing'), explain it where it matters, put the practical question in a checklist. Name the three most repeated ideas in the article and verify by reading that they are not restated.

## Sources and citations (current system; do not redesign)
Citation NUMBERS were removed. Use inline publisher-name links (`[Publisher](exact source URL)`) or a parenthetical `([Publisher](url))` at sentence end (max 2 per sentence), none in table cells, a 'Sources:' line under a table when needed. Every frontmatter source URL must stay linked in the body at least once; do not drop sources unless no remaining claim relies on them; no fabricated read dates; no links to unrelated pages. Research-heavy policy claims need stronger attribution; simple editorial guidance does not need a citation after every sentence. If a source contradicts a claim, fix the claim or flag it.

## Products and disclosures
Every product recommendation must solve a problem the surrounding text discusses; place it right after the explanation that gives it context; never interrupt instructions or emergency guidance with cards; one clear next step rather than competing CTAs; if a product feels inserted for monetization, move or remove it; use the shared product card components (the Gear redesign owns card styling: do not hack cards in markdown); keep catalog coverage (`bunx vitest run src/test/gear-products.test.ts`) and the rule that every published guide carries at least one earning product (a test enforces it: where a page is not about gear, keep ONE honestly justified product). No fake testing, personal experience, ratings, 'best', price claims or 'guarantees safety'. Keep approved disclosure logic: one top affiliate notice with Amazon's sentence where Amazon links exist; no per-card labels; no price-check lines; do not add repeated trust, methodology, independence or generic legal caveats. Keep meaningful safety limitations and specific uncertainty that affects a decision, visible when the reader needs it before deciding.

## Safety and facts
Safety-sensitive guides (car restraints, life jackets, hot weather, lost dogs, first aid, hiking, fireworks and anxiety, rental policies and fees): confirm claims against reliable sources, separate official guidance from manufacturer claims, avoid absolute guarantees and unsupported medical advice (no medication regimens, no guaranteed effects of calming products), keep limitations that affect decisions, do not shorten away consequential safety information. If a fact cannot be verified, rewrite cautiously, remove it or record a blocker. Preserve dates and provenance of the rental-manager research.

## Mobile
Check at 320, 375, 390, 430, 768 and 1280 px: no horizontal overflow, clear heading hierarchy, readable and tappable links, usable tables (simplify columns or use short lists/cards; accessible horizontal scroll only when justified; never remove meaningful comparison data), no excessive repetitive callouts, product cards near their explanation, no clipped details. Shared template fixes only for proven issues, never page-specific hacks.

## SEO
Preserve URL paths, indexability rules, canonical URLs, search intent, structured data, internal links, related-content links, metadata, redirects and server-rendered content. Titles compelling, accurate, not keyword-stuffed or arbitrarily shortened. Descriptive H2/H3 ('What to check before booking', not 'Other things to know'). Internal links only where useful: packing guide to packing tool, fees research to dataset, vehicle-safety to relevant gear, large-dog guides to each other, accommodation research to booking checklists.

## Process per article (single coordinated pass, then stop)
A. Write a short internal editorial brief (not committed prose commentary): reader question, outcome, unique value, repeated or unnecessary material, factual and source dependencies, proposed structure, product placements, related guides/tools, success criteria. B. One structural rewrite of the whole article (not sentence-polishing). C. Line edit the whole revised text. D. Factual and source check (claims supported, citations accurate, no data silently changed, no source lost, safety qualifications kept, no invented testing). E. Render the page (Playwright) at 390 and 1280 and review: first screen, readability, hierarchy, lists/tables, source placement, product cards, ending, related links, perceived length. F/G are done by independent reviewers afterwards; fix only confirmed substantial problems (wrong facts, broken navigation, duplicate answers, confusing structure, misleading commercial statements, major mobile failures, missing important context). Do not rewrite again for a score 0.2 below target.
