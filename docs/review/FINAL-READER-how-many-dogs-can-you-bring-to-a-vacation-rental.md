# Independent reader review: how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental

Method: Playwright Chromium at 390 and 1280, screenshots viewed, text and link attributes extracted. About 2,020 words, page height about 12,970 px at 390. No horizontal scroll; tables fit 350 px. Internal links all 200. Not user testing.

## Role 1: first-time search visitor
- Title "How Many Dogs Are Allowed at an Airbnb or Vacation Rental?" and dek "Two dogs is the usual cap, but every listing sets its own number, weight rule and fee." This answers the question. Good.
- At 390 the first screen is title, dek, byline and a dog photo; the disclosure and the real answer are below the fold.
- First paragraph: "The listing's own pet rule is the only number that counts. Of the 38 managers we read ... 22 cap pets at two, 2 allow three and 14 give no number." Clear and honest about being a convenience sample. The "short answer" box then repeats the point: "Get your exact dog count in writing before you pay."
- The "Airbnb or Vrbo" section is where an actual searcher goes, and it is mostly about what is not known: "Whether a host limit of two stops a guest from declaring three is not stated anywhere we read." A reader hoping for a rule gets an unresolved question, then "What we could not confirm" at the bottom repeats it nearly word for word.

## Role 2: mobile traveler planning a trip
- Useful items: the wording table ("Two dogs allowed, 50-pound maximum" / "Is that 50 pounds each or together?"), the host message for a third dog, the pre-booking checklist. These are the strongest parts.
- The manager table has three rows, and the caption underneath says "Sources for this table: Brindley Beach", although the rows are Twiddy, Brindley Beach and Cabin Rentals of Georgia. Only one source is named. Three rows would read fine as sentences; the table is borderline necessary (the paragraph after it already lists more managers).
- The "What do extra dogs cost?" section duplicates the sibling fees guide and ends with "Our pet fees guide prices all four units in more detail", and the "Next step" repeats it again. The fee figures (Cabin Rentals of Georgia $50 / $75 / $250) also appear in the Georgia guide.
- Mid-page the gate card ("FOR SPLITTING MEALS IN AN OPEN PLAN") and crate card sit inside a bulleted list under "Also check the layout", interrupting the instruction flow: the list item, the card, the next card, then the closing line. The reason is stated in the text, but a reader who is reading about cap wording meets two Amazon cards in the middle of the page.
- Duplicates: "confirm in writing" appears in the short answer, the layout intro, the message, the host-reply advice, and the checklist (twice). The Georgia cross-reference ("See also: Georgia state park cottages take two dogs at $50 each") is a stray one-liner in the middle of a data paragraph.

## Role 3: skeptical reader
- Disclosure: one top box with Amazon sentence; no per-card labels. Meets the brief, although "As an Amazon Associate I earn" uses "I".
- Gate and crate cards: the gate prose says "Skip it if your dogs clear 26 inches or you would use it at a stair top", which is honest. The crate advice "Skip it if either dog has never been crated" is also useful. The relevance of buying a crate to the question "how many dogs" is thin though: the page is about permission and numbers, and gear is a tangent on layout.
- Source presentation: inline publisher links (Airbnb, Vrbo, Guestable, DOJ, Tokeet) plus a collapsed "Show the 15 sources". One Vrbo link goes to a Singapore-locale listing: https://www.vrbo.com/en-sg/p2719492vb. For a US cabin reader that is odd, and the "23 kg per pet" figure is metric because of it. Guestable (a hosting-software blog) and Tokeet carry weight for claims about what hosts "can specify" and what hosts "cite"; both are vendor sources and are flagged only by their names.
- Hedging and defensive trust language: "Confirmed by a source we cite: the two Airbnb numbers and the limits, weights, fees and penalty wording above. Two as the usual cap rests on a small convenience sample." and "though no Airbnb page repeats that wording". The sample caveat is stated at least four times (intro, table caption "a sample ... not a survey", "Across the 38 pet pages we read", the closing section). Accurate, but it is trust talk repeated.
- Safety: the "do not bring the dog and hope it goes unnoticed" line is practical. Service animal section is hedged to the point of not answering ("Whether a service animal counts toward a numeric cap is in the open questions at the end").

## Layout and polish
No glitches. Content flow is long for the question: 7 min read; the answer is on screen two, with cost, gear and open questions around it.

## Defects
1. Table caption names one source for a three-source table ("Sources for this table: Brindley Beach").
2. Singapore-locale Vrbo link (en-sg) and kg figure for a US audience.
3. Sample/"convenience" caveat and "get it in writing" repeated four to six times.
4. Cost section and "Next step" duplicate the fees guide.
5. Gear cards interrupt a bulleted instruction list; crate is only loosely tied to the question.
6. "What we could not confirm" restates the unresolved items already given in the body; service-animal and puppy answers are non-answers.
7. Stray "See also" line about Georgia state parks mid-section.
