## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. Where: the meta description and pin description ("Many rentals ban or limit leaving a dog alone."). What is wrong: the article's own data is four of 38 managers (about one in ten) stating an alone-time rule, and the body says "convenience sample". "Many" in the search snippet and on the pin over-claims what we can support, and a reader who arrives expecting a common ban finds a hedged "it depends". Fix: edit 1 (say "Some").

2. Where: the KONG pick ("For step 2, a stuffable rubber toy is the common choice..." and the card "Our pick | The maker publishes a size chart by dog weight, says supervised use only..."). What is wrong: the settling-in routine tells the reader to leave a stuffed toy while out (the ASPCA), then the product paragraph says the maker's page says supervised use only, so the page recommends a product for an unsupervised use the maker's page rules out. The page admits it ("these two do not fit together neatly") but still labels it "Our pick" in an alone-time guide. A skeptical buyer sees a conflict of interest. Fix: edit 5 (relabel and reframe as a supervised, at-home practice item).

3. Where: end of the product sections and "## How sure we are" (no buying close). What is wrong: the page has three products (crate, gate, KONG) that only apply in different situations, and never tells the reader which to buy for which host rule, or that for a "no unattended pets" rule they should buy none of them. The rubric's "what to buy first" decision moment is missing; the closest is the checklist, which has no products. Fix: edit 3.

4. Where: "**If the rule is "crated when alone."**" crate paragraph. What is wrong: the iCrate is the first pick, but sizing is left to the reader: the page lists sizes (18 to 48 in) and says there is no weight or folded-size data, with no guidance on how to choose; a first-time buyer cannot choose a size from the paragraph. Also "Ask the host first whether a crate is allowed and where it can go" comes after the buying advice. Fix: edit 4.

5. Where: "**What managers say.**" list and "## Before you book" checklist. What is wrong: the Bear Camp line ("pets must be crated if left alone on the property in its pet policy, while another line recommends it") is hard to parse; checklist items show a bullet and a checkbox side by side (double markers); the host message is a plain quoted paragraph while the damage-charge guide formats the same kind of script as a blockquote. Also the point that "house rules decide" is made in "The short answer", "Is it allowed?" and "What do hosts actually write?", about three times in the first screens. Fix: edits 6, 7 and 8.

## What works well
- "What the listing says / what it probably means / what to do": a decision table that turns house-rule wording ("No unattended pets", "Crate required when alone", "By prior arrangement") into a plan for the dinner reservation. Nothing in the top results does this.
- The camera section reads Airbnb's policy precisely (hosts, indoor versus exterior, noise monitors) and states the gap for guests' own devices instead of guessing.
- Honest durations and sources: the ASPCA principle and the Cornell storm advice, with the Psychology Today "about four hours" explicitly labelled one writer's summary; "When should you not leave your dog?" gives clear stop rules.
