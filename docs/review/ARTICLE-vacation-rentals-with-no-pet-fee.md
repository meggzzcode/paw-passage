## Top 5 issues (most important first)
1. No decision shortcut for the reader who wants to pick one of the three managers or actually find a fee-free home.
   - Where: "## What the three fee-free managers say" and "## Next step".
   - What is wrong: The three managers are three long prose subsections; the reader cannot compare them at a glance (region, dog count rule, what else is charged, what the page does not say). The article ends with "Pick two homes" but gives no link to search listings or to the stays. A buyer who arrived to find a no-fee home leaves without a next click except the host-question list.
   - Exact fix: Edits 1, 2 and 9.
2. Mislabeled link and voice slip.
   - Where: "Our [Airbnb versus Vrbo guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work) covers the rest of the comparison." and "I read the official help pages for both."
   - What is wrong: The link text promises a different guide, but the target is the pet fees guide already linked twice, so the reader may think the link is broken or wrong. "I read" breaks the site's "we" voice and implies a person.
   - Exact fix: Edits 3 and 4.
3. The platforms section is a wall of dense, citation-heavy prose.
   - Where: "## How to look for fee-free homes on the platforms" ("**Airbnb.** Add your destination and your pets under Who...").
   - What is wrong: Each platform is one paragraph of eight to ten sentences and up to six citations; the one action a reader wants (open the price breakdown, look for the pet line) is the last clause. On a phone this is the longest unbroken block in the page.
   - Exact fix: Edit 5 (convert to bullets, same facts).
4. Gear is only loosely tied to "no pet fee" and has no "what to buy first" or skip guidance.
   - Where: "## Keep a fee-free stay fee-free" (four picks: crate, bed, doormat, Pawtect Pads).
   - What is wrong: The section opens by saying it "cannot say whether any item changes what a host charges", which is honest, but then lists four items without saying which matters most. The pads are a fit only for puppies, seniors or anxious dogs, which the paragraph admits. The crate rationale (Hapey recommends a travel crate) is the best link to the topic; the rest are generic.
   - Exact fix: Edits 6 and 7.
5. Hero image and one claim read weaker than the rest.
   - Where: hero (a Shiba Inu puppy in a mesh carrier, alt "A Shiba Inu puppy peeking out of a mesh travel carrier") and "The numbers are made up, but the shape is real".
   - What is wrong: The hero says "travel carrier", not "cabin" or "free dogs", so the page and its pin do not signal the topic. "The shape is real" claims realism the site cannot prove; Hapey's remark is a company's own claim about competitors.
   - Exact fix: Edit 8 and the owner question below.

## What works well
- The exact-wording method: each fee-free manager gets "what the page says", "what else is charged" and "what we could not confirm", and the Twiddy section is honest that the $0 comes from a ten-home blog post.
- The contrast table of seven managers that charge (with the "also on the page" column), and the labelled hypothetical total table where the fee-free home is more expensive, which makes the "totals beat headlines" point visually.
- A copy-ready "What to ask the host" list and a "What we could not confirm" table that name specific gaps (Sliding Rock cleaning amount, Hapey damage terms, Twiddy beyond ten homes). Tables stack cleanly at 390 px.
