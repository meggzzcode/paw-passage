A dog who joins in the family fun is a joy, but the best family activities keep everyone comfortable, including the dog. The aim is not a perfect afternoon. It is a shared one where each person, furry or not, can step out.

## The short answer

Choose calm, structured activities, keep an adult in charge the whole time and give your dog an easy way to leave. Short and predictable beats long and wild.

## Six ideas

**1. A two-leash walk.** Attach a second leash to a second collar or harness so an adult handles the dog while a child walks beside them, as the AKC suggests. The child can pick the route.

**2. A treasure hunt.** An adult hides a few dog-safe treats and a child helps point the way. Let the dog do the finding.

**3. A trick show.** Pick one simple trick, take turns asking for it and finish with a cheer. Everyone gets a role.

**4. A backyard obstacle path.** Lay out a few cushions or a low walking course. Keep jumps low and the pace gentle.

**5. A family photo walk.** Choose a favorite spot and take a few pictures. The photos stay on your phone.

**6. A dog documentary.** On a rainy day, children can narrate a day in the dog's life. The dog can nap through it.

## Practical considerations

Adults supervise every interaction between children and dogs, and an adult watches the dog as well as the child. AKC's guidance names stress signals like yawning, lip licking or changes in the eyes and ears. Step in when you see them. Keep fetch controlled and teach "drop it." Do not let children give treats to a dog who is barking, and keep food games calm. We do not ask for children's names or ages anywhere on this site.

## Your dog, your pace

Dogs who are cautious, older or very young may need quieter versions. Give your dog a bed or a gated space for breaks. If you have more than one dog, consider each dog's comfort and keep food and toys apart to avoid guarding. A dog who walks away has made a clear decision.
