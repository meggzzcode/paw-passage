  - q: What are the first signs a dog is overheating?
    a: AKC lists heavy panting, rapid breathing, and bright red gums and tongue as early signs, and pale or bluish gums and collapse as severe ones. Cornell's list includes drooling, vomiting, weakness, confusion and seizures. Heatstroke is an emergency, so call a vet rather than waiting to see.

  - q: Is it illegal to leave a dog in a hot car?

You are packing the car for a week at a rental, the forecast is hot, and the dog will ride, walk, wait and sleep somewhere unfamiliar. To keep a dog cool in the car and on the trip, the sources we read agree on the basics: heat illness is an emergency, a parked car is never a safe place to leave a dog, and shade, water and rest come before any gadget. Gear can help at the margins, and the picks further down say which to buy for which kind of trip.

- **If your dog is showing these signs now:** move it somewhere cool, wet it with cool (not cold) water and call a vet; the sourced steps are [below](#signs-of-heat-illness-and-what-the-sources-say-to-do).
- **Heatstroke is an emergency.** AKC lists heavy panting, rapid breathing and bright red gums and tongue as early signs, and pale or bluish gums and collapse as severe ones. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/)
- **Never leave a dog in a parked car.** AKC says not even for a short time, and Cornell says never unattended. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/) [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/heatstroke-medical-emergency)
- **Surfaces matter.** AKC and the Royal Kennel Club both use a hand test on the ground before a walk. [[3]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/dog-paws-hot-pavement/) [[4]](https://www.royalkennelclub.com/hotpavements)
- **Cooling gear has thin evidence.** The studies we found were small and on working dogs, and we found no independent test of the products named here. [[5]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2615/12/18/2347) [[6]](https://www.vetmed.ufl.edu/2012/04/26/uf-study-looks-at-role-of-vests-in-heat-related-illness-of-working-dogs)
- **Water is the common thread.** AKC says to bring enough for your dog's size and activity and to plan breaks. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/)

## Signs of heat illness, and what the sources say to do

AKC's early signs are heavy panting, rapid breathing, and bright red gums and tongue; severe signs are pale or bluish gums and collapse or coma. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/) Cornell's list includes heavy panting, drooling, vomiting, bloody diarrhea, weakness, confusion, seizures and collapse. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/heatstroke-medical-emergency)

What the two pages say to do while you get help:

1. Move the dog somewhere cool and well ventilated, such as an air-conditioned space. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/)
2. Spray or sponge with tepid or cool, not cold, water, and use a fan. AKC says extremely cold water can cause shock. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/)
3. Keep cooling on the way. Cornell says to wet the dog with cool water, run the car's air conditioning toward the dog and seek intensive care at a veterinary hospital. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/heatstroke-medical-emergency)
4. AKC says to go to a vet right away if the temperature reaches 104 F or cannot be brought below 105 F in a reasonable time. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/)

The pages give different temperatures. AKC's heatstroke page says heatstroke occurs above 105.8 F [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/), its pavement page says over 104 signals heat stress, over 105 heat exhaustion and more than 106 heatstroke [[3]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/dog-paws-hot-pavement/), and Cornell says 105 F or higher [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/heatstroke-medical-emergency). Do not use one of those numbers to decide whether to call a vet. If a dog shows the signs above after heat, treat it as urgent.

We cannot assess a dog from here. Find an emergency vet near your rental before you arrive; our [first-aid kit guide](/guides/dog-travel-first-aid-kit-guide) covers what to carry, including a thermometer.

## Which dogs are at higher risk

AKC names puppies, senior dogs, overweight dogs, dogs with underlying health issues and breeds with short snouts. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/) Cornell lists short-muzzled breeds such as Pugs and Bulldogs, elderly and overweight dogs, thick- or dark-coated dogs, and dogs with respiratory or heart disorders. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/heatstroke-medical-emergency)

These are risk lists, not clearances. If your dog is on either list, ask your vet before the trip how much heat is too much for that dog. Our [senior dog rental guide](/guides/vacation-rental-with-a-senior-dog) and [puppy guide](/guides/puppy-at-a-vacation-rental) cover the rest of those trips.

## Never leave a dog in a parked car

The advice is consistent. AKC says never leave your dog in a parked car, even for a short time, and that temperatures inside vehicles can rise rapidly. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/) Humane World says temperatures inside a car can spike into the 100s on a pleasant 70 degree day, and that a running air conditioner is no guarantee. [[7]](https://www.humaneworld.org/en/blog/heat-wave-wraps-around-us-take-steps) We found no source that gives a safe number of minutes.

**What state laws say.** The Animal Legal Defense Fund says that as of 2025, 32 states plus D.C. and Guam have some form of hot car law, in three kinds: rules on leaving an animal confined in a way that endangers it, rescue powers for officials, and "Good Samaritan" laws that let private people act in limited cases (its own counts differ on the page, so check the statute). It warns that rescuers could still face property damage or trespass charges and that it is best to contact law enforcement first. [[8]](https://aldf.org/hotcars) The Michigan State University Animal Legal & Historical Center keeps a state-by-state table. Its examples include:

| State | What the table says | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| California | Penal Code section 597.7 bars leaving an animal in an unattended vehicle under conditions that endanger its health or well-being; a first conviction carries a fine of up to $100 per animal | [[9]](https://www.animallaw.info/topic/table-state-laws-protect-animals-left-parked-vehicles) |
| North Carolina | G.S. 14-363.3 covers animals confined in vehicles under conditions likely to cause suffering, injury or death | [[9]](https://www.animallaw.info/topic/table-state-laws-protect-animals-left-parked-vehicles) |
| Florida | Section 768.139 gives civil immunity to a person who forcibly enters a locked vehicle to remove a domestic animal, if conditions such as calling 911 are met | [[9]](https://www.animallaw.info/topic/table-state-laws-protect-animals-left-parked-vehicles) |
| Texas | No statute specific to dogs in parked vehicles; in Lopez v. State a man was convicted under the general anti-cruelty law | [[9]](https://www.animallaw.info/topic/table-state-laws-protect-animals-left-parked-vehicles) |

Laws change, and ALDF itself says to consult an official source. [[8]](https://aldf.org/hotcars) Check the statute for each state on your route. The law is not the reason to stay with the dog, though; the heat is.

**Plan the day so the dog is never alone in the car (our suggestion).** Take turns at stops, choose food stops with outdoor seating, and do errands before you pick the dog up or after you drop the dog off. Our [two large dogs in one car guide](/guides/two-large-dogs-in-one-car) covers heat stops on the road.

## Shade and surface heat

AKC says the ground may be too hot at 85 F air temperature or over without a chance to cool, and cites JAMA data that asphalt reaches 135 F when the air is 86 F. Its hand test: hold your hand on the pavement comfortably for ten seconds, and if it is too hot for your hand it is too hot for your dog's paws. It advises walking in the early morning or evening and choosing grass or shade. [[3]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/dog-paws-hot-pavement/) The Royal Kennel Club uses seven seconds, gives no air temperature, and says sun, wind and humidity all change how hot a surface gets. [[4]](https://www.royalkennelclub.com/hotpavements)

Cornell's prevention list adds shade when outside, frequent water, limiting hard exercise in hot, humid weather and avoiding the hottest part of the day. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/heatstroke-medical-emergency)

**Our suggestion, not from a source:** run the same hand test on sand, a parking lot and a rental's deck boards before the dog steps on them. The two sources we read describe pavement only.

## Cooling vest, pad or water: what the evidence and the makers say

Plain cool water is the baseline in the sources: AKC and Cornell both name wetting the dog with cool water for first aid. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/) [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/heatstroke-medical-emergency) Gear adds convenience, and the claims need reading carefully.

What independent evidence we found is thin. A 2022 study in the journal Animals looked at military working dogs training in moderately hot conditions, with and without cooling vests (we could not open the full text to check its findings, so we do not report them). [[5]](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2615/12/18/2347) A 2012 University of Florida study of seven working dogs found that cooling vests with rechargeable packs helped some dogs cool faster, and called the work preliminary. [[6]](https://www.vetmed.ufl.edu/2012/04/26/uf-study-looks-at-role-of-vests-in-heat-related-illness-of-working-dogs) Neither tested a pet dog on a beach holiday, and neither tested the products below.

| Option | What the source or maker says | What we could not find |

| Cool water on the coat | AKC and Cornell list it as first aid; AKC says tepid or cool, not cold | How well it works against a hot beach afternoon |
| Evaporative vest | Maker: soak, wear, re-soak when dry; evaporative cooling [[10]](https://ruffwear.com/products/swamp-cooler-dog-cooling-vest) | Any test of this vest; guidance on humid weather |

**Which one first.** If you walk or sit outdoors with your dog, a soaked vest is the item the small studies looked at (in other designs, on working dogs). If your dog rests indoors or in a cooled car, a pad is the comfort option, with the maker's warnings. For every trip, a bowl or bottle and shade come before either. None of these replaces working air conditioning or a vet's advice for a dog on the higher-risk lists.

### A vest for walks and outdoor stops

**If most of your dog's heat exposure is on foot, this is the item to look at.** For a dog who walks, hikes or sits through an outdoor lunch with you beside them, a cooling vest is the kind of gear the studies above looked at, in other designs and on working dogs. The Ruffwear Swamp Cooler is sized by chest girth from XXS (13-17 in) to XL (36-42 in), is soaked in water, worn, and re-soaked when it dries; the maker calls the cooling evaporative, says the light-coloured outer layer reflects solar radiation, and lists UPF 50+ wording. [[10]](https://ruffwear.com/products/swamp-cooler-dog-cooling-vest) Skip it if your dog mostly rests, or if you will not carry water to re-wet it. This is the vest to buy first if your trip is hikes, markets and patio lunches.

### A pad for a cooled room or a cooled car

**If your dog spends the hottest hours resting, a pad is the comfort option.** A gel pad is for a dog resting beside you, in a cooled car or on a cooled rental floor, not in the sun. ## At the beach

Cornell advises frequent half-hour breaks from the water, because a dog in and out of water all day can swallow a lot of it, and says to make sure the dog has had plenty of fresh water and is well hydrated before heading to a beach. [[13]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/summer-safety-tips) Our [life jacket guide](/guides/dog-life-jacket-for-lakes-and-beaches) covers saltwater, algae and beach rules at rentals.

Put shade first, using the hand test on the sand and avoiding the middle of the day. A beach umbrella or pop-up shade is our suggestion; none of the sources we read tested one. Nothing we read says a vest or pad is enough for an exposed beach.

## At the rental

Ask the host in writing whether the air conditioning works and covers the room where the dog will sleep, and whether the home has shaded outdoor space (our suggestion). We read no source on safe indoor temperatures for dogs, so do not assume a closed-up house is cool. Our [guide to leaving a dog alone in a rental](/guides/can-you-leave-a-dog-alone-in-a-vacation-rental) covers the rules on hours away. Pool and hot tub rules are in the [pool guide](/guides/can-dogs-go-in-a-rental-pool-or-hot-tub).

A pad on a cool floor in the room you spend the evening in is one place the gel pad fits. It does not replace working air conditioning.

## Hydration on the go

AKC says to bring enough water for your dog's size and activity level and to plan breaks, and to offer room-temperature water, if the dog is alert and able to drink, during cooling. [[1]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/heat-stroke-in-dogs/) We found no source that gives an amount in ounces, so base it on your dog and the day, and ask your vet. Chewy's page recommends insulated bowls and bottles [[12]](https://www.chewy.com/education/dog/health-and-wellness/the-best-products-to-keep-dogs-cool-in-the-summer); neither product below states any insulation.

**In the car.** A bowl that stays put matters on a seat. Kurgo says the Splash Free Wander has a wedge shape that lays flat on a seat, a tapered lip that prevents splashing, a 24 oz capacity and food-grade silicone, with top-rack dishwasher care. If the dog rides with you, this makes water easy to offer at every stop (our view). [[14]](https://www.kurgo.com/p/splash-free-wander-dog-water-bowl/K01812)

Use it at a stop with the engine and air conditioning on and someone with the dog, not as a reason to leave water for a dog alone. A bowl on a seat will still spill on a sudden stop, so check where you put it (our suggestion). Skip it if your dog drinks mainly on walks; the bottle below suits that better.

**On a walk or at the beach.** A bottle with a bowl attached lets you offer water with one hand. Springland's 22oz Original (listed on its page as the 22oz Classic) is squeeze to fill the attached bowl and release to drain the excess back, in food-safe, BPA-free polypropylene with an aluminum carabiner and a leak-proof lock design. A 22 oz bottle is not much for a big dog on a long hot walk, and the page states no insulation, so water will warm in the sun. ## How sure we are

- **Confirmed by a source we cite:** the signs, first aid steps, risk lists, hot car statements, hand tests, the ALDF count and table examples (read 2026-11-19), and the maker specifications and warnings for the four products.
- **What most people report:** nothing here rests on reports from many people.
- **Unknown:** whether the vest or pad cools a pet dog, a safe time in a car, and anything in the table above. The studies are small, and on working dogs.
- **Our suggestions, labelled in the text:** testing sand with the hand test, shade at the beach, taking turns at stops and asking the host about air conditioning.

## What to do and buy first

Check the forecast for each day of the trip and plan walks for early morning and evening. Find an emergency vet near the rental. Then add water, a bowl and a bottle to the [packing list tool](/tools/packing-list), and run the [pre-booking checklist](/tools/checklist) with the air conditioning and shade questions added.

**What to buy first, in order:**

1. **A bowl or bottle that suits how your dog drinks.** The Kurgo Splash Free Wander is the flat bowl for the car seat; the Springland 22oz bottle is the one-hand option for walks. Water comes before any cooling gear.
2. **A vest if your trip is mostly on foot.** The Ruffwear Swamp Cooler, sized from the maker's chest-girth chart.
3. **A pad if your dog mostly rests.** The Green Pet Shop Cool Pet Pad, with its warnings read first.

Treat the vest and pad as optional, and read each maker's warnings before you pack. More options are in the [comfort and cooling gear](/gear/comfort-and-cooling) and [feeding and hydration gear](/gear/feeding-and-hydration) sections.
