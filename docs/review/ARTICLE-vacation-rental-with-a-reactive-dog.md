## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. **Ruffwear Web Master harness pick contradicts the article's own argument.** Where: "A harness with a handle for tight spots." What is wrong: the catalog record and the paragraph both admit the maker page is about "technical terrain", says nothing about reactive dogs, and mentions pulling only for a chest loop. A handle harness is offered to people whose dog lunges, with no stated link to any trigger on the reader's list (the section intro says every item must match a trigger). A skeptical buyer sees a filler pick, and it is the one card that weakens trust in the other three. Fix: either drop the harness (go to three picks) or keep it only if the paragraph states the single job it does for this reader ("a handle to hold a dog still beside you in a tight hallway while a door is open") and says to skip it if the dog already wears a well-fitted harness. Recommended: drop it from the body and from `relatedProducts` (see edit 6).
2. **No "what to buy first" close and no skip guidance.** Where: end of "Gear that makes the first day easier", which jumps straight to "Our packing list covers the rest." What is wrong: four gear picks, no order, no "who should skip". The crate is only for crate-trained dogs, the gate only if there is a hallway, the KONG only if the dog takes food toys, but this is scattered across paragraphs. The reader reaches the decision moment with no shortcut. Fix: add a 4-line "If you only buy one thing" block (edit 5).
3. **Invented-experience phrase.** Where: "Doors are where, in our experience, surprises happen." What is wrong: the site stance is researched, not tested; "in our experience" claims first-hand experience the editors do not have, in an article whose Trust section says it uses no community reports. Fix: edit 1.
4. **Hedge fatigue: "our suggestion, not a source claim" appears about six times.** Where: "The table is our own checklist, not a source claim."; "Our suggestion: if the behavior is new..."; step 1 "This is our suggestion, not a source claim."; step 4 "(our suggestion)"; "We suggest talking to your vet"; "In our view". What is wrong: the honest labelling is right, but it is repeated until it reads as nervous, and the vet advice is stated three times (short answer, vet section, FAQ). Fix: edits 2 and 3.
5. **Short answer spends two of five bullets on definitions rather than decisions.** Where: "The short answer", bullets 2 and 3 (Humane Colorado definition; ASPCA/Cornell management quotes). What is wrong: a reader landing from "vacation rental with a reactive dog" wants the four things to check and the next action before the theory; the check-list and the host message are far below the fold (the host message is about 40 percent down the page). Fix: edit 4.

Smaller issues: citation numbers appear out of order in the gear section ([15] to [18] appear before [14]); the "Questions people ask" block does not answer "hotel or cabin?" or "what if a neighbor's dog barks at us?" (see edit 9); the "See it at Amazon" button is dark green text on a rust fill and is hard to read at both widths (site-wide, but visible on all four cards here).

- Product cards are each introduced in the paragraph where the need appears, with honest limits written into the prose (the wire crate "does not block the view"; the 26 in gate "will not stop a dog that can jump it"; "Use a crate only if the dog is used to one"). Product names in prose link to the Amazon listing and every card carries "Details and specs", "Source", sponsored rel and a disclosure line near the top and in the "Gear for this trip" block.
