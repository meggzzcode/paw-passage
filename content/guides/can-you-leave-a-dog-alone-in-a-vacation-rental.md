Airbnb and Vrbo leave this to each host, and "pet-friendly" says nothing about it. Four of the 38 managers in our dataset wrote an alone-time rule, and no two match: never, crated only, or crate recommended. Settle it before you pay, not on the afternoon of the dinner reservation.

## The short answer

- **Silence is not a yes.** [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463)'s hosting guidance says hosts should state in their house rules whether it is okay to leave a pet unattended.
- There is no standard number of safe hours; it depends on your dog, and the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety)'s test is whether you are back before your dog gets upset.
- A new place changes things. A dog that is fine alone at home may howl or be destructive in unfamiliar surroundings ([Afar](https://www.afar.com/magazine/how-to-find-a-pet-friendly-airbnb-or-vacation-rental)).

A message you can copy is [below](#what-should-i-ask-the-host-before-booking).

## Is it allowed?

Check the House rules section: Airbnb says a listing's pet policy lives there, and Vrbo says guests must agree to the house rules before they book. If they are silent, you have an unanswered question, not permission ([Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/86), [Vrbo](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-House-Rules)). Neither platform sets a rule on leaving pets alone. Our [pet fees guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work) covers other differences between them.

## What do hosts actually write?

Wording varies, so read it literally: one host-forum thread ranges from "dogs must not be left on their own except by prior arrangement" to "Do NOT leave your dog at the cabin unattended ([Airhosts Forum](https://airhostsforum.com/t/are-these-reasonable-rules-for-dogs/24289))."

| What the listing says | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "No unattended pets" or "pets may not be left alone" | Plan a sitter, daycare, or dog-friendly restaurants, or pick another rental |
| "Crate required when alone" | Ask whether you must bring the crate and any time limit |
| "By prior arrangement" | Ask in writing, with dates and likely duration |

**What managers say.** Four managers in our dataset (a convenience sample, re-read on their live pages 2026-11-18) wrote an alone-time rule:

- [Cabin Rentals of Georgia](https://cabin-rentals-of-georgia.com/policies): no dog is to be left unattended in the cabin while the guest is away.
- [Brindley Beach Vacations](https://www.brindleybeach.com/node/3691): never leave dogs alone in the beach house unless they are in a crate, and never lock a dog in a bedroom.
- [Hearthside Cabin Rentals](https://hearthsidecabinrentals.com/frequently-asked-questions): pets must be crated when left alone.
- [Bear Camp Cabins](https://www.bearcampcabins.com/blog/gatlinburg-cabins-pigeon-forge-pet-friendly-cabins): its pet policy says pets must be crated if left alone on the property, while another line on its page only recommends crating.

None of these pages mentions cameras. Our [rental checklist](/guides/pet-friendly-vacation-rental-checklist) lists the other questions to settle first.

## What about noise and neighbors?

Barking is the risk the sources flag most. A dog left in an unfamiliar place may howl, and barking can alert a nearby host; hosts and neighbors judge by what they hear, not by how long you were gone ([Afar](https://www.afar.com/magazine/how-to-find-a-pet-friendly-airbnb-or-vacation-rental)). If your dog reacts to noises or passers-by, see our guide to [a rental for a reactive dog](/guides/vacation-rental-with-a-reactive-dog).

[Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/pet-friendly-holiday-rentals-tips.html)'s guide advises against leaving pets alone if they do not handle unfamiliar settings well or bark constantly when left alone. If your dog does either at home, treat that as a no for the rental too. Ask whether anyone lives on the property and how close the nearest unit is. If a complaint turns into a request for money, our guide to a [pet damage charge after checkout](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout) covers the clocks and replies.

## Can I use a crate, and what about the furniture?

Follow the listing, and only crate a dog that already accepts it. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety) says a crate helps some dogs and adds stress for others, and suggests a baby gate in one room if a dog is distressed in a crate.

[Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463)'s hosting guide does not mention crates. Ask whether the host requires a crate, forbids one, or has space for yours; if the rules say no furniture, pack a bed or throw.

**If the rule is "crated when alone": a folding crate that travels flat.** A dog that has never been shut in a crate is not ready to start at a rental, and you should skip this if the host provides a crate. The maker's page for the MidWest iCrate gives no weights or folded dimensions, so check the folded size against your car space before you buy ([MidWest Homes for Pets](https://www.midwesthomes4pets.com/product/dogs/crates-carriers-kennels/icrate-dog-crate/)).

## Can I leave a pet camera on?

Not without asking. Airbnb's policy says hosts may not have cameras or recording devices that monitor indoor spaces, and it defines these as any device that records or transmits video, images or audio. The page does not address guests' own devices ([Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3061)).

What the page does say, about hosts:

- Indoor cameras are not allowed, even when turned off.
- Exterior cameras are allowed and must be disclosed in the listing, including where they are.
- Noise decibel monitors must be disclosed, though their locations need not be.

For a guest, the practical step is to ask the host before bringing a camera or any device that records or transmits. You may also see disclosed exterior cameras or noise monitors.

## When should you not leave your dog?

These sources are general pet guidance, not rental rules. Skip the solo plan, or arrange a sitter you trust, in these cases:

- **The first night or first hours.** The place is unfamiliar and you cannot know how your dog will react yet.
- **Storms or fireworks.** Cornell's veterinary college says to stay home to comfort a fearful dog, that absence may make them more stressed, and to use a sitter the dog already trusts if you cannot be there. It also says frightened dogs may bolt, so ID collar and microchip details should be current ([Cornell Vet](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/fear-fireworks-and-thunderstorms)).
- **Known separation anxiety or barking.** Signs include persistent barking, chewing and digging near doors and windows, escape attempts, and house soiling that does not happen when you are home; the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety) says anxious dogs should not be left alone outside planned practice sessions and suggests a sitter or daycare. A doorway used by a cleaner or visitor is a new escape point; see our [secure yard guide](/guides/vacation-rental-secure-fenced-yard).
- **Hot or very cold weather, or unreliable heating or cooling.**
  - Heat: the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/hot-weather-safety-tips) says never to leave animals alone in a parked vehicle, and to give plenty of fresh water and a shady place. Do not solve "dogs not allowed on the trail" by leaving the dog in the car.
  - Cold: the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/cold-weather-safety-tips) says a car can hold the cold, and pets need a warm place to sleep, off the floor and away from drafts.
- **A "no unattended pets" rule.** Do not rely on being quick or quiet.

## How long is too long, and a settling-in routine

There is no agreed number, so judge your dog: the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety) plans absences shorter than the time it takes the dog to become upset, and in a new place that may be shorter than at home. A 2018 [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/all-dogs-go-heaven/201802/how-long-is-too-long-leave-dog-home-alone) column says trainers and vets loosely agree on about four hours for an adult dog, with puppies less; that is one writer's summary, not a study.

From the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety)'s guidance: give 30 minutes of aerobic activity before you go, leave a stuffed food toy that takes 20 to 30 minutes to finish, keep goodbyes quiet, and start with a short trial while you are nearby. Panting, trembling or yawning means shorten it.

For the food toy, practise with a KONG Classic at home while you are in the room, so the first stuffed toy is not also the first night in a strange house. Do not leave one with your dog alone unless your vet agrees ([KONG](https://www.kongcompany.com/kong-classic/)).

## What should I ask the host before booking?

Copy and paste this, filled in with your details:

> Hi, we are considering booking [dates] with one [size] dog, [age]. Your house rules mention pets. Are we allowed to leave the dog alone in the rental for a short time, for example a dinner or a few hours, and if so are there conditions such as a crate or a time limit? Is the dog permitted on furniture and beds? Does anyone live on the property or nearby who might hear barking? Are there any cameras or noise monitors we should know about? We would be grateful for a written reply, and we will follow your rules.

Then confirm the totals in our [pet fees guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work), and check your dog with the [will-my-dog-be-accepted tool](/tools/will-my-dog-be-accepted). Before you book, make sure the host's reply is in writing, the host has agreed to any pet camera, your dog has stayed calmly alone before, and you have a first-night plan. Add the answers to your [trip checklist](/tools/checklist) and [packing list](/tools/packing-list).

## What we could not confirm

Your host's rule, any guest-camera rule, and how long your dog copes in a new place. Ask the host, and test your dog. Our sources are Airbnb's hosting and camera pages, ASPCA and Cornell guidance, a small convenience sample of managers and a few forum posts.
