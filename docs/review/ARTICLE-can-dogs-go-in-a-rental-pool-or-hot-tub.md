## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. Where: the intro bullet "**Six managers bar dogs from the pool, and several name the hot tub or spa too.**" and the paragraph "So the dataset's one-home entry understates Twiddy...". What is wrong: the page opens by saying seven managers, then the bullets say "Six managers", and the Twiddy handling ("the Twiddy page our dataset used names only one home", "we count it as one of the seven because that is how it was recorded") exposes internal dataset mechanics to a reader who just wants to know if the dog can swim. Counting the table, five of the seven quotes name a hot tub or spa, and Twiddy's separate pet page does too. Fix: edits 1 and 2.

2. Where: "## Drying off and cooling down without the pool" and the four picks (towel, mat, cooling pad, wipes). What is wrong: the question is a rule question and the page says so ("What to buy: nothing for the pool itself"), but about a third of the article is then gear. The cooling pad has no link to the pool question (a hot day is not a pool rule), the wipes are admitted not to be "a rinse after chlorinated or salt water", and the mat and towel are general rental gear. A skeptical buyer sees a rule article with gear bolted on. Fix: keep towel and mat as the core, label the pad and wipes as optional with a "skip if" line, and add a decision shortcut (edits 5 and 6).

3. Where: the Cool Pet Pad card ("Best cooling mat | NBC Select names it its best cooling mat..."). What is wrong: the card's kicker reads "Best cooling mat" as if it were our ranking; the card's "Source" link goes to an NBC Select page that is not in the sources block and is never mentioned in the body; the paragraph above the card cites only the maker. The why-we-picked text and the prose do not match. Fix: edit 4.

4. Where: top of page, "## Buying checklist" (five numbered items before the evidence table). What is wrong: a buying checklist appears before the reader has seen what the managers wrote, and it duplicates the closing "What to buy first" section; items 2 to 4 describe products before the page explains why any are needed. It also pushes the main asset, the quote table, further down on mobile. Fix: delete the section and keep item 5's reminder in the closing section (edit 3).

5. Where: hero image, pin image and "Is a pool safe for your dog anyway?". What is wrong: the hero and the Pinterest image show a dog swimming happily with a ball in a pool, while the article's answer is that managers say no; a pinner can read the pin as "yes". The section "Is a pool safe..." and its closing paragraph ("That advice is for a pool the dog knows...") argue for the ban in the page's own voice. The intro also repeats its sample caveat three times ("our reading", "convenience sample", "not a survey"). Fix: edits 7 and 8, and see the question for the owner on the image.

- Sourcing is strong (CDC, VCA, Cornell, Airbnb and Vrbo help pages) and the liability section links this rule to the damage-charge guide; the host message covers pool, hot tub and deck separately.
