Airbnb's help pages set three clocks: you have 24 hours to respond to a damage request, the host has 14 days after checkout to file it, and you have 60 days to appeal a charge. You can pay in full, pay part or decline, and Vrbo also gives the host 14 days. Find out which clock you are on before you pay or reply.

This is general information from the platforms' help pages, not legal advice, as of 2026-11-18 when we re-read each cited page. Already have a request? Jump to [what to write back](#what-to-write-back).

## Do this first

1. **Check the checkout date against 14 days.** Airbnb says hosts must file damage claims within 14 days of checkout [[5]](https://www.airbnb.com/d/guarantee), and Vrbo gives the host 14 days after your stay [[8]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-happens-if-my-card-on-file-is-charged-for-property-damage). If the request came later, say so in your reply and ask Airbnb support whether it is still allowed.
2. **Reply through the platform inside 24 hours.** Airbnb steps in after that, and the payment method can be charged if no timely payment or appeal is made [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1415).
3. **Gather your evidence.** Arrival and checkout photos, the listing's pet wording and your messages. Vrbo asks both sides for documentation [[8]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-happens-if-my-card-on-file-is-charged-for-property-damage).

You do not have to accept the number as sent. On Airbnb you can pay a different amount or decline [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3591).

## How long does a host have to charge for damage? The three clocks

| Clock | Airbnb | Vrbo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guest response | 24 hours before Airbnb steps in, for damage requests [[3]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/4291), [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1415) | Not stated on the pages we read |
| Host claim window | 14 days from checkout for damage [[5]](https://www.airbnb.com/d/guarantee), [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3591) | 14 days after the stay [[8]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-happens-if-my-card-on-file-is-charged-for-property-damage), [[9]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-file-a-damage-deposit-claim) |
| Appeal | 60 days after you are charged [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1415) | Contact Us dispute; no deadline on the page [[8]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-happens-if-my-card-on-file-is-charged-for-property-damage) |

Two things trip people up. The 60 days to appeal is separate from the 60-day limit for new Resolution Center requests, which excludes damage claims [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3591). And an unanswered Airbnb request can be cancelled and resent within 60 days of checkout, so a late request is worth questioning [[3]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/4291). A host blog, Hometime, says 72 hours to pay and 30 days to involve Airbnb Support; that does not match Airbnb's current pages [[13]](https://www.hometime.io/blog/how-to-file-a-damage-claim-with-airbnb), so go by Airbnb.

Vrbo's pages say there is no pre-authorization hold and your card is charged only if the host submits a claim. To dispute, select Contact Us. Its host page recommends the host contact you before filing and gives no dispute timeline [[9]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-file-a-damage-deposit-claim). For how both platforms display pet fees, see [how pet fees work](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work).

## What happens in the 24 hours after a request?

If you pay part, decline or stay silent, the host may involve Airbnb or its insurer [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1415). Airbnb also says guests are expected to pay reasonable requests for damage, missing items or extra cleaning, and does not define "reasonable" [[4]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2894). Reply inside the window even if only to ask for details. If you are charged anyway, keep your photos, the claim and your messages together for the appeal.

## I paid a pet fee. Can they still charge for dog hair?

Airbnb says pet fees should cover expected cleaning and that host protection covers unexpected damage, such as a chewed sofa [[5]](https://www.airbnb.com/d/guarantee), [[6]](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463), [[7]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623). Its host page also lists extra cleaning for pet-accident stains as reimbursable. No platform text defines hair, odor or stains, so the argument is about "expected": normal hair is arguably what the fee is for, a chewed couch is not. Hosts disagree. In one forum thread the host waived under-$100 extra cleaning after guests who had paid dog fees did not offer to pay, and a commenter said a fee likely blocks a cleaning claim but not drywall damage. One thread, not a rule [[12]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/530/topics/1253487-anyone-successfully-use-vrbo-damage-protection-for-extra-cleaning-sub-200). See our [pet fees guide](/guides/how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work) for how fees are structured, and our [puppy rental guide](/guides/puppy-at-a-vacation-rental) if a puppy is coming.

| Situation | What the sources say | Your focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary hair after a paid pet fee | Fee covers expected costs | Ask what extra work was needed |
| Stain, odor or scratches | Pet accidents and stains listed as reimbursable | Dated photos, itemized claim |
| Dog not declared | Vrbo claims can cover undeclared pets | Read the listing and booking terms |
| Listing said no pets | Airbnb says not to bring pets to such listings | Manager penalties for unapproved dogs can apply too |

## What to write back

Keep replies short, polite and on the platform, and replace the brackets.

**Ask for detail first:**

> Thanks for the request. Please send an itemized breakdown, dated photos of each item, and a receipt or quote. I paid a pet fee of $[amount], which Airbnb says covers expected costs.

**Pay part:**

> I will pay $[X] for [specific item], which I agree my dog affected. I decline the remaining $[Y] because [reason]. My photos from [date] are attached.

**Decline:**

> I am declining this request. The listing allowed pets, I paid the pet fee, and I left the home as I found it. My checkout photos from [date and time] are attached.

Send the decline only if it is true; if your dog did cause damage, the partial script is more honest. These are drafts, not proven winners.

## Should you file a chargeback?

Treat it as a last resort, after the platform process. A card-privacy company's guide says once you file, Airbnb can no longer resolve the issue directly, the bank investigation may take up to 90 days, and a positive outcome is not guaranteed [[10]](https://privacy.com/blog/airbnb-chargeback). In one 2024 Vrbo case a bank repeatedly denied a chargeback before crediting after an advocate stepped in [[14]](https://elliott.org/advocacy/such-a-bizarre-rental-experience-on-vrbo-but-do-they-even-care/). In a 2018 Airbnb case a chargeback failed; the page says the guest had not followed Airbnb's dispute steps first [[15]](https://elliott.org/case-dismissed-2/this-airbnb-complaint-has-a-big-problem/). Anecdotes only; ask your card issuer what it requires and by when.

## What evidence helps against a pet damage claim?

Dated, per-item photos from arrival and checkout are your strongest protection, and evidence cannot be created after the request arrives. In one 2026 consumer-news item, a guest with two large dogs and a $300 pet fee was asked for $90.47 about two weeks after checkout and argued the host's photos showed items before washing; no outcome was reported [[11]](https://www.thecooldown.com/?p=815147). The claims we read ($64, $90.47, a $100 rug) were mostly around $100 or less, though people post what they choose to post.

- [ ] On arrival, dated photos or video of floors, furniture, bedding, screens and fences, plus close-ups of flaws.
- [ ] Save the listing, house rules and pet terms as they read when you booked.
- [ ] Keep every message with the host on the platform.
- [ ] At checkout, photos or video of each room, the dog's areas and the yard.
- [ ] Note how you left it clean (towels, vacuuming, bedding as the rules say).
- [ ] Save your receipt for the pet fee.
- [ ] If you get a claim, ask for the itemized amount, dated photos per item and a receipt or quote.

## What if the dog is a service animal?

Airbnb's ground rules say service animals, and in some locations emotional support animals, are not pets [[4]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2894). Its pet-fee page says service animals are always free of pet fees, but if one damages property the host can still request payment under AirCover [[7]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3623). Ask Airbnb or Vrbo support how they treat your situation, and save the reply.

## Before your next booking: message to send the host

> Hi, we are bringing [number] dog(s) for [dates]. Please confirm in writing: (1) what the pet fee covers, including normal dog hair; (2) whether there is a separate fee for an undeclared pet and where it is written; (3) whether you keep move-in photos and will share them if a claim arises; (4) your process if there is a damage claim. Thank you.

Next trip, add the photo steps to the [trip checklist](/tools/checklist). For a wet or muddy dog, our [rental cleanup gear page](/gear/rental-cleanup) covers mats and towels that keep the mess off the host's floors; none of it changes how a claim is decided, and your photos do the real work.

## What we could not confirm

Confirmed by pages we cite: the 24-hour response, 14-day host window and 60-day appeal on Airbnb, and the 14-day window and no pre-authorization hold on Vrbo. Help pages change, so check the live page for your date. The forum and news items are a small, non-random sample that mostly show small claims and arguments turning on photos and listing wording.
