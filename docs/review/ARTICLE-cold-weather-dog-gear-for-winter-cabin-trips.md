## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. **Trust: the dek claims something the body does not support.**
   - Where: dek "Vets and kennel clubs give different cold thresholds, and the maker pages give no temperature ratings."
   - What: No kennel club gives a threshold. The AKC page is cited only for boots and the research block itself records "No temperature guidance" for AKC. The temperature numbers come from PetMD (vet-written), a Cornell veterinarian quoted by NBC Select, PetSmart (a retailer) and Outward Hound (a maker blog). The first thing a reader sees therefore overstates the sourcing. This is the sort of line that makes a skeptical reader doubt the rest.
   - Fix: TEXT: replace the dek with "Veterinary sources and pet retailers give different cold thresholds, and the maker pages give no temperature ratings. Here is how to decide whether your dog needs a coat or boots, size them, and keep a rental clean."

2. **Reader flow and buying: the decision shortcut is at the very bottom.**
   - Where: final H2 "What to buy first" ("Start with the surface and the dog, not the catalog.").
   - What: A buyer lands from "dog boots for snow" or "dog coat for winter", scrolls past about 1,000 words of temperature bands before reaching a coat, and only learns the ranked order of buying after four pick cards and two "could not confirm" tables. There is no quick chooser near the top. The bullets in the opener are all research findings, none says "if X then buy Y".
   - Fix: STRUCTURE: after the five opener bullets insert a short chooser (no new claims, all from the body): "**Quick chooser:** salted or icy paths: boots or a wipe-down routine first. Small, thin-coated, old or sick dog: a fleece or vest. Dog already on a harness: the vest saves a layer. Everyone: a towel for the door." Keep "What to buy first" as the full version and add "Skip boots if your paths are clear and dry; skip the fleece for a thick double-coated dog that mostly needs paw care."

3. **Buying advice: missing "who should skip" and the harness-vs-coat decision is split across the page.**
   - Where: pick cards for the Timberline Fuse vest and the Polar Trex boots; paragraph "For a dog that wears a harness on cabin trails."
   - What: Each pick says what the maker states but not who should not buy it. The fleece paragraph says it is a dry-weather layer and the shell advice sits one section later ("If it is colder than 'mild to cool' or wet, neither is the insulated, windproof shell..."), after both cards. A reader with a wet-snow trip buys the fleece card first. For the vest, the page admits no load rating exists for the harness but the card does not repeat it as a limit on use, and the page says "a walking harness and not a car restraint" only in prose.
   - Fix: PRODUCT/TEXT: move "If it is colder than 'mild to cool' or wet, neither is the insulated, windproof shell PetMD describes. We do not feature one in this guide." up to directly after the first sentence of the coat section's PetMD line, before the fleece card. Add to the fleece card text "Skip it for wet snow: the maker states no water or wind claim." Add to the vest card "Not a car restraint: the page gives no load rating and no crash claim." Add to the boots card "Skip if your paths are clear; try them indoors first."

4. **Layout: dense citation walls and a cramped mobile table.**
   - Where: the paragraph beginning "Salt is the part people underrate." (about 10 citations in one block) and the "How cold is too cold, by dog type" table.
   - What: The salt paragraph is one block of roughly 230 words with five citations, hard to scan on a phone. On the 390 px view the three-column table has a narrow first column and the third column wraps to 8 lines per cell, so the table is about 1,450 px tall; the sticky header overlaps rows in the capture. The hero is good and relevant (dog in a coat in snow).
   - Fix: STRUCTURE: split the salt paragraph into a short bold-led list: "What it does", "Why 'pet-safe' is not zero risk", "What to do (wipe paws, limit licking)", "What to ask the host". Shorten the table's third column to one clause per row (for example "Paw care first; do not clip short" and "Pack a layer for any walk near or below 32 F") and keep the long wording in the paragraph below.

5. **SEO fit: title tag drops the real search hooks and the page never answers "how cold" in one visible line.**
   - Where: seoTitle "Cold-Weather Dog Gear for Winter Cabin Trips"; FAQ "How cold is too cold for a dog?".
   - What: The meta description is good, but the title tag is a copy of the H1 with no hook. The highest-volume question ("how cold is too cold for dogs") is answered only in the FAQ and by a table that opens with four different numbers. The pin title "What Helps and Fit" is vague for a Pinterest scroll.
   - Fix: SEO: set seoTitle to "Dog Coat or Boots for a Winter Cabin Trip? Fit and Cold Limits" (62 characters, check the template suffix keeps it under about 60 plus brand). Add one sentence under the H2 "How cold is too cold, by dog type": "Short version: PetMD puts the first worry below 45 F for most dogs, below 32 F for small, thin-coated, young, old or sick dogs, and below 20 F for all dogs; other sources differ." Retitle the pin to "Dog Coat or Boots for Winter? Cold Limits and Fit".

## What works well
- Honest limits with sourcing: PetMD, Cornell, ASPCA and AKC are named per claim, the maker pages' lack of temperature ratings is stated four times in different forms, and the Ruffwear in-between-size contradiction (chart says up or down, fit guide says smaller; boots say size down) is surfaced rather than papered over.
- Sizing detail a buyer can use tonight: chest-girth bands for fleece and vest, paw-width range and step for the boots, "measure all paws", "check strap tension 15 minutes in", "lugs are for outdoor use only, take them off at the door".
- The cabin angle is real: de-icer on the host's steps, drying gear, laundering the towel with the rental's machine, and antifreeze in the parking area. That is not in the competing pages the research block lists.
