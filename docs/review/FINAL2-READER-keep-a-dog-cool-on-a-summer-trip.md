# Reader review: keep-a-dog-cool-on-a-summer-trip (390 and 1280 px)

Roles: first-time search visitor, mobile traveler, skeptical reader. Judged on the built page only.

## Defects (observable)
1. Emergency steps are not on the first screen. At 390 px the H2 "If your dog is overheating right now" starts at y=737 of an 800 px viewport (steps begin on the second screen, after hero image, affiliate notice, "On this page" and a lede about habits). At 1280 px it starts at y=1286, so the steps are about 1.6 screens down. A panicked owner needs a visible jump or the box at the top.
2. Vet step is last and buried. Step 4 "Call your vet or an emergency vet now, on the way if possible" packs the vet call, in-car cooling and two temperature thresholds into one paragraph. The call-the-vet action should come before or alongside cooling and be a short line of its own.
3. Temperature numbers confuse. "AKC says to go right away at 104 F, and Cornell treats 105 F or higher as heatstroke, so do not wait to see if a temperature comes down." Nothing says how to take it (rectal), that a normal dog is about 101-102.5 F, or what to do if the owner has no thermometer (most do not; the sentence is the only temperature guidance). Two sources quoted against each other is not an instruction.
4. Cooling instructions are ambiguous: step 2 "cool water, not ice-cold", step 3 "tepid or cool water, never cold". Water is "cool" in one and "tepid" in the other. No instruction on when to stop cooling, no "do not use ice", no "do not cover the dog with a wet towel", no "do not force water". Signs paragraph ("Heavy panting... early signs") sits after the steps, so the reader cannot recognize the condition first.
5. Cross-guide contradiction: this page says "wet the dog" / "Spray or sponge" while the first-aid guide says wet towels at neck, armpits and groin and "Do not put the dog in a tub of cool or cold water". Not the same method; a reader of both is unsure.
6. Repeated answers: "never leave in a parked car" appears in the lede, in its own section and in the FAQ; the water bowl is repeated in the buying-order list. Mild.
7. Law paragraph is dense and mixes states (Florida, California fine "$100 per animal", Texas) with no cue for a rescuer's immediate action beyond "call law enforcement first"; "call 911" is never stated as the step for a dog in a car in distress.
8. Citation style inconsistent: "(AKC)" plain text and linked "AKC" in the same sentences; some citations read as clutter in a safety box ("(Cornell Vet). AKC says...").
9. Commercial: two product cards (bowl, bottle) sit before the vest and pad; the bowl and bottle do have stated reasons. The Cool Pet Pad card says "Cools up to 3 hours (maker)" while the text notes a maker claim of preventing overheating; handled honestly. No broken links, raw markdown or layout overflow seen (scrollWidth 390).

## Verdict: NEEDS WORK
Content is accurate and well sourced, but a safety-critical page buries the emergency steps below the first screen (more so on desktop), puts the vet call last and gives a muddled temperature threshold. Fix placement and the step order, and it moves to ready with minor fixes.
