**Voice:** Direct and calm; correct for the content. No jokes. Hedge line: "Its page also claims the pad prevents dangerous overheating; no test is cited, and no pad makes a parked car safe." (keep the second half).

Keep Ruffwear sizing in the card, the "needs a water source, less in humid air" line, and "no pad makes a parked car safe". Delete warranty and size list from prose.

## Pattern summary

- **Openings are mostly good; middles are the problem.** 11 of 14 openings already give the answer. The three that do not (leave-alone, no-pets, and partly ramp) bury it behind a dataset count or a platform rule. The fix is rewording the first paragraph, not restructuring the guide.
- **The same product recurs across guides.** The Soggy Doggy mat is carded in two guides (damage, costs) with a sister towel in a third (pool), and the Springland bottle and Quencher bowl recur across four (cooling, Georgia, hiking, bowls), the MyPet gate and iCrate in three (leave-alone, how-many, no-pets). A reader who follows three guides sees the same cards. Recommend one home guide per product and a one-line link elsewhere.
- **Dataset numbers are carrying decisions and being repeated as stat dumps.** The 38-manager figures are useful once per guide (22 / 2 / 14; 7 of 38; 14 of 38). Red-flags, costs and no-pets stack three to five counts in one section; keep the single count that changes the reader's question and link `/data/pet-policies` for the rest.
- **Source-by-source paragraphs.** Ramp ("What vet and kennel-club sources say"), life jacket (fit, water and flotation), cooling (law and study citations), leave-alone (How long, manager bullets) and Georgia (park inventory) each narrate AKC, VCA, Cornell or a manager's page where one sentence and a link would serve. The brief's "evidence after advice" rule suggests moving what remains below the checklist.
- **Hedging stacked on a recommendation.** "That is our reasoning, not the maker's instruction", "one person's figure, not a standard", "Anecdotes only", "One thread, not a rule" and the "What we could not confirm" tails (leave-alone, damage, pool, hiking, ramp FAQ) add trust but are often repeated. One honest caveat per claim is enough; delete the closing summary lists.
- **Anecdote items cut the damage-charge and cooling guides.** BiggerPockets, The Cool Down, a 2024 Vrbo chargeback and a 2018 Airbnb chargeback do not change what the reader does; trimming them is safe.
- **Safety and legal text is well placed and should not move.** Heat steps, hot-car law, life-jacket "never unattended", service-animal rules, damage-clock figures and "do not pay or decline yet" are early or clear in each guide; trims around them should leave them verbatim.
- **Merge note for Article 7.** Put the worksheet and two-dog arithmetic in `how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental` as a new H2 between the numbers and the third-dog message, replacing the current layout bullets and the 70-word "What do extra dogs cost?" paragraph, and trim the same guide by about 500 words first.
