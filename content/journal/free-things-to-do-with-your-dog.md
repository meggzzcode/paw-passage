A good day with your dog should not need a budget. Many "free" lists quietly include a campsite fee, a parking meter or a pet store treat. This one sticks to things that cost nothing if you use what you already have.

## The short answer

The best free dog activities are sniff walks, scent games, short training sessions and quiet time. They cost nothing because they use your attention, your dog's nose and things already around the house.

## Seven free ideas

**1. A sniffari.** Pick a route and let your dog stop to sniff for as long as they like. The walk is about their nose, not the distance.

**2. A find-it game.** Use a few pieces of your dog's regular food and hide them for a quick search. ASPCA's enrichment guide describes this as scent play you can make harder over time.

**3. A towel roll-up.** Roll a few pieces of kibble inside a towel and let your dog unroll it. Take the towel away if it gets chewed.

**4. A trick you will never need.** Teach something silly like spin or "touch." It takes five minutes a day.

**5. A new route.** Walk a block you have never used. Novelty is free.

**6. Porch time.** Sit outside together and watch the world go by. No agenda.

**7. A photo walk.** Take a few pictures of your dog doing what they do. They stay on your phone and cost nothing.

## What "free" really means

Some ideas are free to do but cost something to reach: a parking fee, a trail pass or a drive. Ideas that use your own home and street are free in the strictest sense. Some are free to complete but need small supplies you already own, like a towel or a box. Always check for fees before you go.

## Practical considerations

Use only what your dog normally eats, and keep anything small enough to swallow out of play. Supervise DIY toys, and take away anything your dog tries to swallow.

## Your dog, your pace

A cautious dog may prefer porch time to a new route. A senior may love a slow sniff. If you have more than one dog, give food games separate spaces. If your dog turns away, change the plan.
