# AUDIT-d: six newest/longest guides

Reviewer stance: skeptical buyer. All six files read in full. Word counts (wc): no-pets 3,001; damage 4,144; how-many 4,394; NYE 3,758; breed 6,390; senior 6,422 (includes frontmatter, which is roughly 400-900 words of each; body is shorter but still long).

Labels: [MEASURED] = I saw it on a page today (search result list or file in this repo); [ESTIMATE] = my judgment. No search volume, difficulty or ranking numbers exist for this site; none are given. Competitor evidence note: today I ran one WebSearch per article. I did NOT fetch competitor pages; I saw URLs and search-tool summaries only. Those are marked "[MEASURED: seen in results]". Competitors the previous editors recorded in each file's frontmatter are marked "[from frontmatter, not re-checked]".

## Cross-cutting findings (apply to all six)

1. **The certainty-label system reads clinically, and it is the main style problem.** Every guide opens with a paragraph explaining "Confirmed / What most people report / Unknown (level 3)", then repeats the explanation in a "How we label certainty" section near the end, then uses "Level 2, small sample" and "Level 3" inside the body and in table columns. Examples [MEASURED, in files]: "Level 3, unknown: only 2 of the 8 forum and community items we collected..." (how-many); "**What most people report (level 2, small sample).** Four of the 17 forum threads we read..." (senior); "Our three levels: CONFIRMED means a primary source we cite says it; 'most people' means at least 3 of the items we read agree (a small, non-random sample)" (NYE). A reader does not care that 3 of 17 threads agree. The label itself is a good trust idea; the implementation is a lab-report. Fix: keep one plain-English habit ("the platform says" / "hosts say" / "nobody publishes this, so ask"), drop numeric levels and "n of N items" counts from the body, and move methodology to a short "How we researched this" box linked from the byline. [ESTIMATE] A first-time reader will not read the methodology and will find the repetition tiring.
2. **Over-hedging is pervasive.** Counted occurrences of phrases like "we could not", "we found no", "did not confirm", "Unknown", "small sample", "non-random" [MEASURED via grep]: no-pets 3, damage 12, how-many 10, NYE 13, breed 25, senior 27. Many sections exist mainly to say nothing is known (senior: stop intervals, heat limits, vet distance; NYE: lost-dog statistics; breed: vet letters, DNA tests, Denver). An "unknowns" table at the end of the guide is a good honesty device once; here it is 8 to 14 rows long in four guides. A buyer reads this as either rigorous or as a site that cannot commit to an answer; the typical visitor will read it as the latter.
3. **"Bottom line:" prefix on nearly every H2** (damage guide has about 16; senior and NYE similar) followed by a body that restates the same bottom line. It turns each section into a template. The "Do this first", "The short answer" and then the section bottom lines say the same thing three times in the top third of the page (damage, senior, NYE, how-many).
4. **Length versus reader value.** The panic-intent pages (damage charge, no-pets) can justify 1,800 to 2,500 words. Planning pages (breed, senior) at 6,000+ words with 40+ sources are past the point where a reader scrolls; the ask is "can I bring my dog / what do I check". [ESTIMATE] Target body lengths: no-pets 2,200; damage 2,800; how-many 2,800 (move the 38-row table out); NYE 2,500; breed 3,500; senior 3,500.
5. **Frontmatter sprawl and off-topic sourcing.** Breed lists 42 sources, senior 36, damage 22. Many are landlord forums (BiggerPockets long-term tenancy), Mumsnet UK, three-legged-dog forums. For a buyer, "we read 17 forum threads, many UK or years old" is a confession that the evidence is off-target; it weakens, not strengthens, trust. Cite fewer, better, on-topic sources.
6. **Strength worth protecting:** every guide offers a copy-paste host message, a before-you-book checklist, and cites primary platform pages with dates. The honesty about what is not known is a genuine differentiator against thin affiliate blogs. The problem is dosage, not intent.
7. **Original data is under-exploited.** The 38-manager dataset appears as a table at the bottom of how-many-dogs, and is referenced in damage (11 of 38 undeclared-pet penalties) and breed (5 of 38 mention breeds). It is a linkable, quotable asset that is buried at the foot of a 4,400-word page. See how-many for details. The breed guide uses a different, smaller 14-row table rather than the 38-manager set.
8. **Monetization:** none of the six has a natural buy button beyond tools. [ESTIMATE] Best affiliate fit among these six: senior (ramps, rugs, orthopedic beds, harnesses), NYE (GPS trackers, ID tags, possibly calming gear though the guide itself says evidence is weak, which is honest but limits conversion). The platform-policy guides (no-pets, damage, how-many, breed) lead to Airbnb/Vrbo, which are not approved affiliates here; the nearest fit is travel/pet insurance or rental-protection gear (there is already `dog-friendly-rental-protection-gear-guide.ts`).
9. **Cannibalization map [MEASURED, from relatedGuides]:** how-many vs traveling-with-two-dogs-accommodation-guide (count questions); breed vs vacation-rentals-that-welcome-large-dogs (weight caps) and vs no-pets and how-many (unapproved-dog penalties repeated in four guides: no-pets, damage, how-many, breed each have their own unauthorized-dog table); damage vs how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work; senior vs can-you-leave-a-dog-alone, large-dog, pet-friendly-checklist. The "what happens if you bring an unapproved dog" content is duplicated four times; pick one home (no-pets) and link.
