# Forum dossier: breed-restrictions-at-vacation-rentals

Gathered 2026-11-18 from public pages only (ordinary web search plus normal page fetches). No Reddit, logins, proxies, mirrors or archives. Pages that blocked us were skipped.

**Important caveats**
- This is a small, non-random sample of 17 sources. It is not a survey and says nothing reliable about how common anything is.
- Most of what we could read is host-side. We found almost no first-person guest account of a breed refusal at a vacation rental.
- Several BiggerPockets threads are about long-term tenants, not vacation stays. They are marked "long-term".
- Quotes were returned by the page-fetch tool, not copied by hand from a browser. Each is 25 words or fewer. Re-check each against the live page before it goes into the article.
- Not read, so not used as evidence: TripAdvisor threads (HTTP 403, skipped); Airbnb Community threads (the old URLs redirect to communitycenter.airbnb.com, whose pages showed unrelated threads, not the ones we wanted; skipped); Vrbo help article "Are service animals allowed" (showed an error page).

## 2. Patterns (small, non-random sample)

Rule used: a pattern needs 3 or more independent sources. "Independent" here means different threads or articles. Where one regular poster appears in several threads we say so.

**Pattern A. Landlords and hosts name insurance as the reason for a breed rule or a worry.**
Supported by 4 threads: #3 (BiggerPockets 725277), #6 (BiggerPockets 1044345), #2 (BiggerPockets 512374, insurer-cancellation fear), #5 (BiggerPockets 669597, insurance question). Caveat: all four are BiggerPockets and appear to be long-term rental threads, so this does not show what vacation-rental hosts say. The draft already cites two other BiggerPockets threads and one Airhosts thread for the same idea.

**Pattern B. Many host posters say the individual dog, or its training, matters more than breed or size.**
Supported by 4 threads: #14 (Airhosts 49489 p2), #23 (Airhosts 60921), #22 (Airhosts 56922, about size), #7 (BiggerPockets 1044345). Caveat: #14, #22 and the replies in #23 involve the same few Airhosts regulars (the "KKC" and "muddy" screen names appear across threads), so the number of independent voices is closer to 3. Hosts who accept dogs are likely over-represented in a thread about whether to accept dogs.

**Pattern C. Service-animal or emotional-support claims collide with breed rules, and hosts are unsure what they may do.**
Supported by 3 sources: #16/#17 (Airhosts 49537), #1 (BiggerPockets 512374, long-term), #26 (WSB-TV, where the dog's owner calls it a service dog). #9 and #27 are related but not about breed. These are opinions of hosts, not legal rulings.

**Single reports (not patterns)**
- A host with a 35 lb limit found two undisclosed dogs (#20); another host had two large dogs arrive against house rules (#10-#11). Two sources, so not a pattern.
- Pet rules must be written into the listing rules to be enforceable (#13, one poster's claim).
- Vrbo's pet house-rule field is very short (#24-#25, one thread).
- A host quoting an Airbnb message with an 80 lb combined pet limit (#19, one report).
- One host keeps a personal banned-breed list that includes corgis (#18).
- Pet rules are "inconsistently communicated" (#21, one poster).
- A guest's dog was killed by a pit bull at an Airbnb (#26, one news report).

## 3. Warnings and issues the draft does not yet cover

(Checked against content/guides/breed-restrictions-at-vacation-rentals.md and docs/CLAIMS-breed-restrictions-at-vacation-rentals.md.)

1. **Rules that do not fit or are not shown.** Vrbo's house-rule field is reported as very short (#24-#25), and an Airhosts poster says pet info is inconsistently communicated (#21). The draft says to read house rules and the manager's page, but not that a breed or weight line may be cut off, missing, or only in a separate agreement. Single-thread evidence.
2. **A rule must be in the listing to be enforced.** One host says so (#13). If true, it affects what a guest should save (screenshots of the listing text). Not verified.
3. **Service-animal claims versus breed rules.** The draft has a short service-animals section about fees. It does not say that hosts openly argue about this, that some doubt claims, or that a host trying to cancel over a service dog has been reported by a family (#16-#17, #27, #1). Do not turn this into advice to claim a service animal. The draft already says not to.
4. **Hosts' own cancellation advice.** Hosts advise asking the platform to cancel when a dog breaks a size rule (#11). The draft covers fees and eviction but not that a host may cancel at arrival.
5. **Combined weight limits.** One host quotes Airbnb's pet message with a combined limit of 80 lb (#19). The draft's table has "60 lb total" for one manager but does not mention combined caps as a trap for two-dog trips. Host report only; Airbnb's current message not verified.
6. **Host-held personal lists beyond insurance.** One host reports a personal banned breed (#18), and the draft attributes breed rules mainly to insurance. We cannot say how common this is.
7. **Safety on the guest side.** One news report of a guest's dog killed by a host-side pit bull (#26). The draft has no section on a guest asking what dogs live at or visit the property. This is a single incident; do not generalize about any breed.
8. **Look-alike confusion.** A poster conflates American bulldogs with pit bulls (#5). This supports the draft's mix section but gives no evidence on what hosts do.

## 4. Anything in the draft these sources contradict

Nothing is directly contradicted. Two points to review:

- **Vrbo help pages.** The draft and ledger (#6) say Vrbo's help pages did not load. On 2026-11-18 https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-House-Rules loaded (it is not a forum) and says hosts can tell guests "how many, what type of pet, and any size restriction." It does not mention breed. The other Vrbo help article we tried ("Are service animals allowed") returned an error page. The draft's statement about Vrbo may be worth a re-check and a possible update; we did not edit it.
- **"No first-person story" claims.** The draft says it found no first-person account of a restricted-breed dog getting a yes. That still holds: nothing here is such an account. Rows #26 and #27 are guest accounts but about an attack and a service dog, not a breed approval.
