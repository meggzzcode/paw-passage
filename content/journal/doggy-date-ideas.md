A doggy date is just an outing with a theme and nowhere else to be. Search for it and you will find a lot of lists built around restaurants, breweries and stadiums, which suit some dogs very well and others not at all. The idea here is simpler: an unhurried hour or two that is about your dog.

## The short answer

The best doggy date is one your dog finds easy. Pick a quiet place, bring a few comforts and let your dog set the pace. A blanket and a view can beat a crowded venue.

## Seven date ideas

**1. The blanket and a view.** Choose a bench, hillside or quiet corner of a park, spread a blanket and bring water and a chew. Stay as long as it feels good.

**2. A dog-friendly patio.** If your dog is calm around strangers and noise, try a patio that welcomes dogs. Call ahead, sit at the edge and bring a mat to settle on.

**3. A slow sunset walk.** Walk somewhere your dog loves, at a time when it is cooler and quieter, and let them lead for a block or two.

**4. A stay-in movie night.** Dim the lights, bring a stuffed chew and cuddle up. This is a lovely option for dogs who prefer home.

**5. A paint-and-pawprint evening.** Use pet-safe, non-toxic materials and make a simple pawprint card. Skip it if your dog dislikes having their paws handled.

**6. A treat tasting.** Choose two or three treats your dog already eats well and give small tastes in a quiet spot. Avoid anything with xylitol, grapes, chocolate or other foods on the ASPCA list of people foods to avoid.

**7. A scenic drive and a stop.** Pick a short drive to a lookout, a lake edge or a quiet main street. Secure your dog with a crash-tested harness or crate, never loose, and stop somewhere calm for water and a sniff.

## Practical considerations

Confirm a venue's dog policy before you go, and choose off-peak hours. Bring water, waste bags and a plan to leave if your dog seems unsure. Keep the date short for a first visit. Watch for signs that your dog is enjoying it, like loose posture and interest in sniffing, and for signs they are not, like yawning, lip-licking or turning away.

## Your dog, your pace

A senior dog may love a bench and a slow sniff; a young dog may want more to do. A shy dog may prefer a date at home. If your dog is reactive or easily overwhelmed, quiet places and early hours help. Skip anything that needs them to meet strangers.
