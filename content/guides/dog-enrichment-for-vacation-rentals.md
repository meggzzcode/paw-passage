Pick the quiet, contained options first: a lick mat, a sniffing game or a stuffed rubber toy, used on a towel while you are in the room. Supervise anything edible or chewable, freeze runny fillings, and know where you will wash the toy before the dog starts. Match the toy to your dog's size and chewing style, and skip it if your dog breaks off and swallows pieces. A toy is enrichment, not a fix for a dog that struggles when left alone.

## Low-mess to high-mess: what to try first

The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/canine-diy-enrichment) groups enrichment into food puzzles, scent games and indoor play, and says "sniffing can be stimulating and calming." In a rental, the useful question is how much a given activity leaves behind. The ratings below are our judgment, not test results, and your floor changes them: a towel on carpet and a tile kitchen are different problems.

| Activity | Mess (our judgment) | Noise | Needs | Stay in the room? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scent game with a few treats rolled in a towel | Low | Quiet | A towel and a few treats | Yes |
| Settling on a mat after a walk | Lowest | Quiet | The mat or blanket your dog knows | Nothing edible, so supervision is lighter |
| Short training session | Low | Quiet | Small treats | Yes |
| Lick mat with a spread filling | Low to medium | Quiet | Soft filling; a towel under it | Yes |
| Snuffle or sniff mat with kibble | Medium (crumbs) | Quiet | Dry food you already packed | Yes |
| Stuffed rubber toy, frozen | Medium (drips as it thaws) | Quiet, though it can skid on tile | Freezer space, or fill it fresh | Yes |
| Rigid treat-dispensing feeder | Medium (kibble scatters) | May be loud on hard floors | Dry food or small treats | Yes |
| Chew bones and hard chews | High (grease, fragments) | Varies | Not recommended in a rental | Yes |
| Raw or greasy fillings | High | Quiet | Skip in a rental | Yes |

The first three rows need no product at all. For a dog that already settles on a mat and loves to sniff, a walk plus a scent game may be all the enrichment a rental evening needs.

## Quiet activities that need no product

- **A towel scent game.** Roll a few small treats into a towel on the floor and let your dog work them out. The ASPCA says it is "incredibly important to allow your dog to use their nose."
- **A short training session.** Five minutes of cues your dog already knows, rewarded with small treats, uses the same food you packed and leaves nothing behind.
- **A settle on a mat.** Practise lying on the blanket or bed from home after a walk. The familiar mat gives your dog a spot that is theirs in a place that is not.
- **A sniff outside.** A slow walk where your dog sets the pace is also enrichment, and it keeps the house clean.

## Supervise, and know what that means

The [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/lick-mats-for-dogs/) puts it plainly: "There's no such thing as a 'leave-it-and-forget-it' dog toy." [KONG](https://www.kongcompany.com/faq/) recommends supervising a dog whenever it plays with any non-edible toy, and the ASPCA's enrichment page asks for close, active supervision while its do-it-yourself items are within reach. This guide assumes you are in the room. We found no source that supports leaving a dog alone with a particular toy in a rental.

Be ready to intervene. The ASPCA says to remove an item immediately if a dog tries to swallow it. The [American College of Veterinary Surgeons](https://www.acvs.org/small-animal/gastrointestinal-foreign-bodies/) lists toys among the things dogs swallow that can block or damage the gut, warns that string can cut through the intestine, and lists vomiting, loss of appetite, abdominal pain and diarrhea as signs. If your dog swallows part of a toy or shows those signs after chewing, call a vet or an emergency clinic.

Many rentals also set rules on leaving a dog alone. See our guide to [leaving a dog alone in a vacation rental](/guides/can-you-leave-a-dog-alone-in-a-vacation-rental) before you plan a dinner out.

## Food compatibility

- **Check for xylitol.** The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/updated-safety-warning-xylitol-how-protect-your-pets) says xylitol can cause a sudden drop in blood sugar in dogs, with vomiting, weakness and sometimes seizures, and that very large doses can cause liver failure. It names sugar-free peanut butter among the foods that may contain it. If your dog eats it, the ASPCA says to call its Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) or, if your dog shows symptoms, go to a vet immediately with the packaging.
- **Mind the ration.** The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/destructive-chewing) suggests putting part of the dog's daily food in a food toy. The AKC notes peanut butter can carry syrups and oils that add up, and suggests plain yogurt without artificial sugars or flavors.
- **Ask your vet about limits.** For allergies, a medical diet or a weight plan, the ASPCA's advice is to speak with your own veterinarian about what is safe.
- **Freeze runny fillings.** The AKC suggests freezing low-sodium broth on a mat, and KONG says freezing a stuffed KONG for a few hours makes it last longer. Whether the rental freezer has room, and whether the host minds, is something to ask.

## Match the product to your dog

Maker instructions are the best guide, but they are the makers' own claims, not independent tests. KONG says to size its Classic by weight, to size up if between sizes, and that the widest part of a KONG should be bigger than the back of the dog's mouth.

| Your dog | What the sources say | Likely fit | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gentle licker | The AKC describes lick mats for licking, not chewing | Lick mat; towel scent game | Mat slipping on tile |
| Average chewer | KONG says the red Classic is for average chewers | Stuffed rubber toy sized by weight | Cracks, missing pieces |
| Power chewer | KONG says power chewers should size up one or two sizes or try its KONG Extreme; a power chewer using a soft rubber toy "may quickly damage it" | Ask the maker; consider skipping product enrichment | Pieces coming off |
| Dog that tears or swallows pieces | The ACVS lists toys among swallowed items | None of these toys | Vet advice |
| Between sizes | KONG says to size up | The larger size | Pieces that fit the dog's mouth |

A rigid dispenser has its own limits. KONG's Wobbler comes in a Small for dogs up to 20 lb and a Large for 30 to 65 lb, so a 25 lb dog falls in between, and the maker calls it "not a chew toy" and says to supervise. For toys sold as tough, a veterinary dentist writing in [Veterinary Practice News](https://www.veterinarypracticenews.com/durable-treats-dental-risks/) says durability often goes with hardness, lists antlers, bones, hooves and nylon bones as hard items linked to tooth fractures, and says rubber toys can be durable and still flexible enough not to cause them.

## Protect the floor and the furniture

- Choose a hard-floor spot, like a kitchen or entry, for anything with food, and put a towel or mat under it. A lick mat or toy on carpet is where a stain starts.
- Keep food toys off beds, sofas and rugs.
- Photograph the floors and furniture at arrival, as in our [damage-charge guide](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout).
- Pick up crumbs and wipe drips as you go, not at checkout.

## Clean and dry it in a rental

- **Wash right after use.** The AKC says to clean a lick mat after each use; for natural rubber, soap and warm water. KONG says to wash rubber toys in the sink with warm water, soap and a bottle brush or toothbrush, and to avoid bleach or chemicals. Soak stuck-on food in warm, soapy water.
- **Dishwasher only if the maker says so.** KONG says its rubber toys are top-rack dishwasher safe and to skip the drying cycle, and that toys with rope or fabric should be hand washed. The AKC says some mats, particularly food-grade silicone ones, are dishwasher safe, and natural rubber ones should be hand-washed. Ask the host before running dog items in their dishwasher.
- **Use your own brush and sponge.** Pack a small bottle brush rather than the kitchen's.
- **Dry on a towel you packed.** Leave the toy where your dog will not get it.
- **Throw out worn toys.** The AKC says eroding material, punctures and jagged ridges are threats to your dog's health, and KONG says to discard a toy with cracks or missing chunks. In a rental, bag it and take it with you.

## Pack it

Bring only what you will use: the toy your dog already knows, a mat or towel, a bottle brush, dry food portions and a sealable bag for used toys. If you plan to freeze a filled toy, ask the host whether the freezer has room. ## When a product is the wrong choice

Skip it if your dog:
- bites off and swallows pieces, or has swallowed toys before;
- takes a lick mat and eats it rather than licks it;
- guards food or items, or reacts when you take them away, in which case the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/destructive-chewing) says to ask a Certified Applied Animal Behaviorist or board-certified veterinary behaviorist;
- is a puppy that is still teething (see our [puppy at a rental guide](/guides/puppy-at-a-vacation-rental));
- has a diet limit you have not cleared with your vet.

## If your dog struggles in a new place

A toy is not a treatment. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety) says a stuffed toy is one way to build good associations with short absences, that it only works for mild cases, and that highly anxious dogs usually will not eat when their people are away. If your dog pants, trembles, will not eat or cannot settle, drop the product, stay with your dog and keep the routine from home. For ongoing distress or for a dog that reacts to sounds, see our [reactive dog guide](/guides/vacation-rental-with-a-reactive-dog) and talk to your vet.

## Checkout

Wash and dry every toy, wipe the spot under it, check under furniture for crumbs and hidden pieces, and bag anything damaged. If something is stained or broken, tell the host promptly rather than hope it goes unnoticed; our [damage-charge guide](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout) covers what comes after.
