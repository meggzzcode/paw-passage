## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. **Where:** the whole "Spacing and weight, by the maker's numbers" section plus the pick cards. **What is wrong:** the article is about two LARGE dogs, but every harness it can sell stops at 75 lb (Ruffwear's tested mannequins 25/45/75 lb, Kurgo's "crash tested for dogs up to 75 lbs"), and the only product with a named independent test to 90 lb (Sleepypod Clickit Sport, CPS passed July 2014) has no pick card, no maker page (the catalog says the maker URL returned 404 on 2026-11-19 and Sleepypod now sells a Clickit Sport Plus that was not matched to the CPS listing) and is sponsored-linked from prose three times. So a reader with two 80 to 100 lb dogs reaches "What to buy first" with nothing to buy. The "Unknowns" table admits the gap but the buying advice does not give a decision shortcut. **Fix:** add a plain decision box under the harness section: "Dogs under 75 lb: the two harnesses below. Dogs 75 to 90 lb: the only independent result we found is CPS's Sleepypod Clickit Sport listing (tested 2014, test dogs to 90 lb); confirm with Sleepypod that the current product matches it. Dogs over 90 lb: we found no tested harness; use a strapped crate." Do not add products that are not in the catalog; decide with the owner whether the Clickit Sport should get a card or lose its Amazon links (see questions).

2. **Where:** "## Layouts by vehicle" first row ("Two crates, side by side, in the cargo area") and "Spacing and weight". **What is wrong:** the first layout in the table is two crates, and the article does the weight arithmetic for the Lucky Kennel (38 lb Intermediate, 45 lb Large), but there is no crate pick card, even though lucky-duck-lucky-kennel is an active catalog product (maker says CPS crash-test rated; CPS lists the Large tested with a 110 lb dog). A buyer who chooses the crate layout has no product to click. Lucky Kennel is linked to Amazon in prose twice but is not in relatedProducts and has no "why we picked it". **Fix:** add the product to `relatedProducts` and a pick card after the arithmetic paragraph: `{{pick:lucky-duck-lucky-kennel|For two crates in a wagon or SUV|The maker lists Intermediate (under 70 lb, 38 lb kennel, 20.5 in interior width) and Large (70 to 110 lb, 45 lb kennel, 22 in interior width) with a ratchet strap kit sold separately; CPS lists the Large tested with a 110 lb dog and that kit, one crate and one dog.|https://www.luckyduck.com/products/lucky-kennel}}`. Add: "Two of these side by side were not tested together."

3. **Where:** opening paragraph ("Two big dogs, one back seat, and a long drive...") and the first bullet list. **What is wrong:** the first screen is one 120-word block with the answer ("What to buy:") buried mid-paragraph in bold; there is no "short answer" heading, so the table of contents on desktop begins at "Buying checklist" and the reader on mobile scrolls past about 2,180 px (hero plus intro) before the first section heading. It also tells the reader to go to another guide before they have the answer, and the intro, the four bullets, the "Buying checklist" and the closing "What to buy first" all state "one restraint per dog" (five times by my count). Reading time says 7 min but the body is about 2,490 words (roughly 11 to 12 min). **Fix:** split the opener: first sentence the scenario, then a bold, stand-alone line "The short answer: one restraint per dog, each rated for that dog's weight." Then add `## The short answer` above the bullets so it appears in the table of contents. Delete the "Buying checklist" item 1 and 5 duplication (see edit 4). Set `readingMinutes: 11`.

4. **Where:** the "Layouts by vehicle" table (4 columns: Layout, Fits, What the sources say, What is unknown). **What is wrong:** at 390 px the table scrolls inside its wrapper (scrollWidth 373 vs 350); columns collapse to about 75 px so the "What the sources say" cells wrap to one word per line and the last column ("What is unknown") is cut off at the right edge. A reader on a phone sees only half the argument. The "Unknowns" table below fits better but is also three columns of citations. **Fix:** convert the layouts table to four short labelled blocks (H3 per layout: "Two crates in the cargo area (SUV, wagon)", then "Sources", "Unknown" lines), or reduce the table to three columns by merging "Fits" into the first column ("Two crates, side by side (SUV, wagon)"). Keep the footnote numbers.

5. **Where:** the Kurgo pick paragraph ("Kurgo says its Tru-Fit is crash tested for dogs up to 75 pounds, although...") and card "For the dog under 75 lb". **What is wrong:** for an article about two large dogs the Tru-Fit is positioned for the smaller dog only, and the card contradicts the paragraph's own tone ("its size chart goes higher and the size selector lists only Small, Medium and Large, so confirm the size you need"). The paragraph sentence "this is the harness to size the smaller dog with, not the larger" gives the reader no alternative for the larger dog, so the net buying advice is incomplete. Also the Backseat Barrier and Happy Ride cards are good, but nothing tells a buyer which single barrier suits a sedan with two dogs on the bench (answer: the Backseat Barrier does not make sense when dogs ride on the seat it clips behind; the text says "for dogs on the rear seat" though the barrier sits behind the front seats). **Fix:** reword the Kurgo paragraph ending to: "If one dog is over 75 lb, use the decision box above for that dog; the Tru-Fit's tested ceiling is the maker's own figure." Reword the Backseat Barrier trigger: "For a sedan or wagon where the dogs ride on the rear seat, a barrier behind the front seats keeps them out of the front."

## What works well
- The two-dog arithmetic is labelled as arithmetic ("2 x 70, 2 x 38, 2 x 20.5", under 216 lb total, over 41 in interior width), with the maker figure and the CPS test condition (one crate, one dog) side by side. No competitor does this.
- The "Unknowns" table and "How sure we are" are rigorous: it states that no independent test covers two dogs side by side and says who to ask. Maker claims are separated from CPS results (the Ruffwear testing page excerpt, "not tested to the CPS protocol", is surfaced rather than hidden).
- Heat, legal and "what not to do" sections are short, sourced (CDC, Cornell, AAA Northeast) and carry the "not legal advice" caveat. Affiliate disclosure appears above the first heading; Amazon buttons are rel="sponsored nofollow"; there are no prices or ratings.
