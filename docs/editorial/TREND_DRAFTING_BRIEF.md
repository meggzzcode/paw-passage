## Research (read before you write)
- Read the sources named in the plan's brief for your article and find more where a claim needs them: official/primary first (veterinary colleges, AVMA, ASPCA, Airbnb and Vrbo help pages, campground and RV park rules, Center for Pet Safety, NHTSA, RVIA, manufacturer manuals and sizing guides), then reputable secondary sources. WebFetch/WebSearch public pages only; do not bypass bot walls (403/401/captcha): mark the source UNVERIFIED and do not cite claims from it.
- Every consequential claim must be supported by a page you actually read; cite it in frontmatter `sources` (title, publisher, URL, date read 2026-11-25) with natural inline links where the sentence names the source; every frontmatter source appears in the Sources list; inline external links go only to listed sources; no `[[n]]` numbers; no trailing parenthetical publisher tags.
- Never invent statistics, personal experience, host confirmation, ratings, prices, testing, or availability. Quote numbers exactly with scope and date. Convenience samples (the 38-manager dataset) are never "the market". Compute any dataset figure from `public/pet-policies-38-managers.csv` with a recorded method; the plan flags figures that may be wrong (Smokies weight caps): do not publish them.
- Per-article source record `docs/editorial/trend-sources/<slug>.md`: a table (claim or section | source title | URL | organization | source date | date checked | what it establishes | confidence or limitation), the unreadable sources, and the list of open items for Cowork.

## Writing standard
- Final voice: answer-first, warm, specific, practical, calm; no generic 'ultimate guide' filler, no repeated reassurances, no fake anecdotes, no keyword stuffing, no awkward affiliate transitions. H1 = the reader's question in natural words; dek 20 to 28 words that adds information; opening 40 to 90 words with the real answer; first H2 advances the topic; descriptive H2/H3; one original asset per article (checklist, decision table, message template, worksheet, fit guide, troubleshooting sequence); short FAQ (2 to 4 real questions); length follows the reader's need (not all equal; typical 1,200 to 2,000 words; narrow comparisons shorter).
- Safety-sensitive topics: confirm claims against reliable sources; separate official guidance from maker claims; never absolute guarantees; no medical or behavioural advice beyond what veterinary sources say; state when to see a vet.
- Products: reuse existing ACTIVE catalog ids only where they honestly fit; write the 'what to look for / who should skip it' text so the article stands without any product; in the text mark each intended new product slot as an item in `research.unverified` (for example 'PRODUCT-SLOT: <use case>, candidate ids from the matrix, pending Cowork') and do NOT add {{pick}} cards for products that are not active and verified. No prices, stock, ratings, 'tested', 'best', or urgency. No product photos. The publish rule needs one earning product per published guide: record which existing product you propose as the required one.
- Distinct purposes: follow the plan's overlap decisions exactly (winter road trip vs boots/wax vs RV; Smokies vs multi-dog; enrichment vs gifts). Link, do not re-explain, adjacent guides; list proposed inbound links from existing guides in your source record (do not edit the old guides).
- Packing planner and Gear: link to /tools/packing-list (with ?trip= presets where valid) and the relevant /gear category or trip collection where it serves intent; use the `{{planner:<trip>}}` directive only where the existing pattern fits.
