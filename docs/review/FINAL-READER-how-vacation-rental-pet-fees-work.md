# Independent reader review: how-vacation-rental-pet-fees-work

Method: Playwright Chromium at 390 and 1280, screenshots viewed (first screen, fee-structure table region), text and link attributes extracted. About 2,160 words, page height about 13,440 px at 390. No horizontal scroll; tables fit 350 px. Internal links all 200. Not user testing.

## Scenario test: "one cabin has a pet fee and another does not: which costs less?"
- Key cost differences: well explained. The "four fee structures, priced" table (two dogs, four nights: $80 / $100 / $100 / $160) is the best element on the page and shows that "The smallest headline number gives the largest bill." Readable at 390.
- Worked comparison of the actual scenario: weak. The only sentence that addresses it is in "Compare the full stay, not the pet fee": "A no-pet-fee home can cost more overall: a rental with a $60 pet fee can still be cheaper than one with none if its rate or cleaning fee is lower." There are no numbers for the two cabins, no nightly rates, no total. A reader asking this exact question must construct the example. The break-even example ($90 per stay versus $20 nightly, crossing at 4.5 nights) compares two pet-fee policies, not a fee versus a no-fee cabin. The $800 / $120 / $90 / $250 example sums one stay, not two. A two-column worked example (Cabin A: $180 per night, $90 pet fee; Cabin B: $195 per night, no pet fee; same four nights) would answer the question directly, and the page has the material for it.
- Additional charges: covered well: cleaning, refundable deposit, card hold, damage waiver, taxes, cap, platform display differences, post-checkout claims. Findable, but spread over four headings. The "Hypothetical example" summing $1,010 definite and $1,260 up front is clear and correct arithmetic.
- The "Compare the full stay" section sits after the Airbnb/Vrbo display section and before service animals, so the answer to the scenario is the sixth section, a quarter of the way down the page at 390 it is easy to miss.

## Role 1: first-time search visitor
- Title/dek: "A pet fee is a formula: per stay, per night, per dog or per dog per night. See what 38 managers charge and how to total it." Clear. First paragraph gives the stats (25 of 38 state a fee, $20 a night to $300 per pet, 3 charge nothing). Good, but these figures plus the "Short answer" bullets are heavy before the first useful example.
- At 390 the first screen is title, dek, byline and a photo of a dog behind a laptop; the answer begins after the disclosure and the "On this page" accordion.
- The 38-manager table is arguably unnecessary: its four rows give ranges ("$75 to $300 per pet") that the paragraph above already states, and the next paragraph restates them ("a $25 nightly fee and a $300 per-pet fee are different animals"). "Different animals" is a stock-flavored pun on a pet page.

## Role 2: mobile traveler planning a trip
- Useful: the message template, the five-item price check, "If a charge surprises you".
- Repeats: "get one total for your dates and dogs" appears in the intro ("learn the price by asking"), the short answer ("Get one total for your dogs and your exact dates before you book"), the message template ("Please confirm the complete pet-related charge"), and the checklist ("The pet-fee unit and complete pet charge are confirmed"). Deposits-versus-hold is stated in the short answer, the fees/deposits section, the $1,010 example and the checklist. Service animals appear in the short answer and again in their own section with the same ADA fact.
- Two near-identical Vrbo CTAs: "start a pet-friendly search on Vrbo (affiliate link)" and "enter your exact dogs and dates in a pet-friendly search on Vrbo (affiliate link)". The inline "(affiliate link)" labels duplicate the top notice, against the one-notice rule.
- Final gear section "Protect your deposit" appears after the checklist and next steps, at the very end, with three cards (mat, towel, pads). The framing is honest ("Nothing you pack guarantees a clean return"), and each card has a stated case and a skip rule. Still, the section arrives after the page's natural ending, and the pads card ("for a puppy or senior dog") is a leak product placed in a fee guide. The card brand label "PET PARENTS" with the name "Pawtect Pads (washable pee pads, 2 pack)" and the "Soggy Doggy Microfiber Doormat (no bone)" title have catalog leftovers.

## Role 3: skeptical reader
- Disclosure: one top box with the Amazon sentence, no per-card labels; the Vrbo link is the only "(affiliate link)" inline mark. Mostly meets brief.
- Source presentation: inline links (Airbnb, Vrbo help pages, DOJ) and a collapsed "Show the 7 sources". Seven sources for a page quoting 38 managers is thin in the list; the manager data is attributed to a "pet policy dataset" page, not individually. Named managers (Resort Realty "up to $1,000", Elk Springs $50) have no inline link in the text.
- Hedging: "Hypothetical round numbers" and "hypothetical" appear six times; the repeated label makes the numbers feel soft, but they are legitimately examples. "Fees as stated on each manager's public page (a convenience sample ... not a survey; what any one home charges for your dates, only the host can confirm)" is a long defensive parenthetical.
- Accuracy checks: arithmetic holds ($20 x 2 x 4 = $160; $15 x 2 x 7 = $210; 4.5-night crossover; $1,010 / $1,260). Service-animal text is careful and correct about the ADA's limits.

## Layout and polish
No layout glitches; tables are legible at 390; cards render cleanly. Perceived length is high (13,400 px on a phone) for what is mostly one idea (unit x dogs x nights + extras).

## Defects
1. The core scenario (pet-fee cabin versus no-fee cabin) has no worked numbers; it gets one sentence with a bare "$60" figure.
2. The scenario answer sits mid-page under "Compare the full stay, not the pet fee", after the display-platform section.
3. The "38 managers" table duplicates the paragraph around it.
4. Repeated "get one total" and deposit/hold messages (intro, short answer, template, checklist, example).
5. Two inline "(affiliate link)" labels plus two near-duplicate Vrbo CTAs.
6. Gear section after the "Your next step" ending; pads card is only loosely tied to fees; catalog-ish titles.
7. "Hypothetical" repeated six times; stock phrase "different animals".
