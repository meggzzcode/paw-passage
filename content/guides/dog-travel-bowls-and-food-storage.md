You are loading the car, the kibble is in a half-open bag, and the dog's bowl is the heavy one from your kitchen at home. The rental may also be a cabin where food storage is a safety rule, not a tidiness one. This guide covers what to pack for feeding and watering a dog away from home, with each product's capacity, materials and cleaning instructions taken from the maker's own page, read on 2026-11-18.

- **Bring water from home.** The ASPCA says to opt for bottled water or water from home on a road trip, because unfamiliar water could upset a pet's stomach. [[1]](https://www.aspca.org/news/traveling-pets-follow-these-expert-dos-and-donts-keep-them-safe)
- **Keep kibble in its bag.** The FDA says to put the entire bag into any storage container. [[2]](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/proper-storage-pet-food-treats)
- **In bear country, scent is the issue.** Dog food has to be handled like the rest of the food. [[3]](https://www.nps.gov/articles/bearsafetyfood.htm)
- **Wash bowls by hand in a rental.** That is our advice, explained below.

## Water first: what the sources say

The ASPCA's travel kit is food and a bowl, a leash, waste bags, grooming supplies, medication, a first-aid kit, your vet's contact details and any travel documents. [[1]](https://www.aspca.org/news/traveling-pets-follow-these-expert-dos-and-donts-keep-them-safe) The CDC's pet travel page says to bring plenty of water, food and a bowl, and to feed your pet 3 to 4 hours before leaving. [[4]](https://cdc.gov/healthy-pets/travel/index.html) Both put a bowl on the list, which is why a bowl that packs flat earns its space.

Water you meet along the way is a separate question. The CDC says dogs can get Giardia by drinking from a contaminated creek, pond or other water source, that puppies and kittens are more likely to get sick than adults, and that water bowls should be cleaned and disinfected regularly, especially while a pet is sick. [[5]](https://www.cdc.gov/giardia/about/about-giardia-and-pets.html) Our takeaway, which is our judgment and not a source's rule: carry enough water and a bowl on every walk so the dog is never drinking from whatever is nearby.

## Keeping dry food fresh in the car and the rental

The FDA's advice has four parts. Store pet food and treats in the original container or bag. If you want another container, put the entire bag into it instead of pouring the kibble in. If you do pour it, the container should be clean, dry and have a lid that fits snugly. And the temperature should be less than 80 F. [[2]](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/proper-storage-pet-food-treats) The page says the original bag keeps the lot number and "best by" date, which is useful if a food is ever recalled. The FDA page was marked current as of 2024-05-23.

That makes a rigid container such as the Vittles Vault a holder for the bag, with the maker's 6-cup figure a guide only, since its page says capacity depends on kibble shape. [[9]](https://www.petmate.com/vittles-vault-travel-trainer-portable-food-storage-container/product/4504) The Kibble Carrier's maker says it holds about 20 cups and rolls down to the amount you carry, so a few days of food can go in it without carrying the whole bag. [[10]](https://www.kurgo.com/dog-travel/kibble-carrier) The FDA's 80 F figure is the number to check a hot car trunk against; no source we read says how fast kibble spoils in a car.

## Cabins and bear country: food is the rule, not the bowl

For cabins, the National Park Service says bears treat anything with a scent as food, that its list of items to store properly includes toiletries and ice chests, and that regulations differ from park to park. Some parks let you keep food in a car, out of sight with the windows closed, in daylight hours only; others require lockers. The page does not name pet food, so treat dog food as scented. [[3]](https://www.nps.gov/articles/bearsafetyfood.htm) West Virginia's wildlife agency is more direct, advising campers and homeowners that "all food, including dog food" belongs in a vehicle or a bear-resistant container. [[15]](https://wvdnr.gov/bearwise-tips/)

Where does that leave a travel container? The Vittles Vault page claims an airtight seal that keeps out "pests and insects", and no maker page in the table above makes a bear-resistance claim. An airtight lid is not a bear-resistance rating; that is our reading. So the dependable plan is the host's plan: ask where food goes inside the home, whether the property has a bear-resistant bin, and what local rules say. Our [cabin packing list](/guides/dog-friendly-cabin-packing-list) covers the rest of bear-aware packing, so we will not repeat it here.

## The rental kitchen: our advice, not a rule

This section is our opinion, not a source's, and it is cautious by design.

- **Do not use the host's dishes, pots or glasses for the dog.** Pack bowls of your own, including a spare, so a bowl in the wash never means a bowl borrowed.
- **Wash the dog's bowls by hand and dry them on your own towel.** The FDA says to wash and dry pet food bowls after each use. [[2]](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/proper-storage-pet-food-treats) Do the washing with your own sponge, not the host's, and keep it in a bag.
- **Feed on a mat you brought, not a rental floor or rug.** Spills and stains are what a damage claim is made of; see how the [damage-charge process works](/guides/airbnb-pet-damage-charge-after-checkout).
- **Ask before you run bowls through the dishwasher.** Several makers state top-rack dishwasher safety, but that is about the bowl, not the host's house rules.

Message to send the host:

> Hi [name], we are bringing one [size] dog on [dates]. Where would you like us to store the dog's dry food, and is there a bear-resistant bin or a local rule about pet food we should follow? We bring our own bowls and will wash them by hand. Is there a spot you would rather we feed him, such as the kitchen floor or the porch? Thank you.
