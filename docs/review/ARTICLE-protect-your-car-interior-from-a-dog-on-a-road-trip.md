## Top 5 issues (most important first)
1. **Layout (score 3): the vehicle-and-dog comparison table fails on a phone.** Where: the table under "Cover, hammock or cargo liner: pick by vehicle and dog" ("Setup | Maker size | Where it goes | Who it suits"). What is wrong: at 390 px the four columns collapse to roughly 70 to 90 px each, with one to three words per line, so the "Maker size" cell (with a long quote and citation) becomes 15 or more lines and each row is over a screen tall. This is the page's decision table and the main differentiator. Fix: shorten the cells to facts and move quotes into the prose below. Use columns Setup | Size | Goes | Suits with cell text like "Bench cover | 45 x 56 in | Rear bench | Car or SUV, dog on the seat", "Extended hammock | 63 x 56 in | Rear bench, headrest to headrest | Large SUV or truck, big dog or two dogs", "Cargo cape | 52 x 62 in | Cargo area | Wagon or SUV, ideally with a crate", and put the quoted maker phrases and citation numbers in a sentence under the table. Alternatively render as a stacked card list at small widths.
2. **Title and meta promise what the page does not deliver.** Where: the H1 "...Cover, Hammock or Cargo Liner" and the meta "Seat cover, hammock, cargo liner or backseat extender? Compare them". What is wrong: there is no cargo liner as such (the product is a "cargo cape") and the "backseat extender" gets one paragraph that says "This guide does not recommend a specific one", with no size, weight or product. A searcher for "dog back seat extender" gets no pick. Fix: change the H1 and pin title to "...Cover, Hammock or Cargo Cape" or keep "cargo liner" and add "(a cargo cape here)" in the first sentence of the cargo section; reword the meta to "Seat cover, hammock or cargo cape for a dog on a road trip: compare by vehicle and dog size, with seat belt access, cleaning and rental-car rules." and either drop "backseat extender" from the meta and `dek`, or add a catalog-backed extender pick in a later pass.
3. **Over-hedging with "we read" language.** Where: "no page we read claims it protects the dog", "None of the guides we read tested these problems", "We did not read the U.S. pet terms of other companies", "We have no source on how rental companies treat cover straps". What is wrong: the researched-not-tested stance is stated in the opening, the dek, the table, "What we could not confirm" and "How sure we are", sometimes four times in one screen; the repetition makes confident, well-sourced sentences read as uncertain and blunts the core message that covers are not restraints. Fix: keep the opening statement and the "What we could not confirm" table; cut the repeated phrasing from the dek and the bullets. New dek: "A cover keeps hair, mud and claws off the upholstery, but none of these is a crash restraint. Here is how to match a cover, hammock or cargo cape to your vehicle and dog." Remove "If you read nothing else here, read that part first." from the opening paragraph.
4. **Booster seat and crate placement dilute the argument.** Where: the paragraph "For a small dog, a booster raises the dog" and its "Our pick" card inside "Seat belts, anchors and headrests", and the PetSafe collapsible crate paragraph inside "The cargo area". What is wrong: the booster is a comfort and tether product (the catalog category is car-safety), not interior protection, and it interrupts a four-step belt-check list; the crate is described as a back-seat soft crate in the cargo section and has no card even though it is the one product that fits the "rental car requires a carrier" advice. Fix: move the booster paragraph and card to its own H2 "A small dog: booster seat" placed after the cargo section, and move the crate sentence to "Rental and borrowed cars" (where it is already mentioned); retitle the booster card label to "For a small dog on a bucket seat" (already so). If the owner wants the crate recommended, add a catalog record in a separate pass; do not add claims.
5. **Buying advice stops short of a decision shortcut and dog-size fit.** Where: "What to buy first" and the table. What is wrong: the page never says "if you have X, buy Y". Weight limits are "not listed" for all covers, yet the reader is not told which of the three suits a 20-lb dog versus a 90-lb dog beyond "big vehicle and big dog". Fix: add a three-line quick pick under "What to buy" in the opening: "Small or medium dog on the back seat of a car: the bench cover. Big dog or two dogs in a truck or large SUV: the extended hammock. Dog rides behind the rear seat: the cargo cape, with a crate." (all from the cited maker facts and the vehicle sizes already in the table). In "What to buy first", replace item 2 with "A cover sized to where the dog rides: bench cover, extended hammock or cargo cape, after you measure."

## What works well
- The core message is sharp and repeated at the right place: a cover is not a restraint, with ASPCA and Vetstreet used as support and a link to the crash-test guide.
- Genuinely differentiating content: a rental-car section built on Enterprise's own page, belt-and-tether checks ("Click each belt in with the cover installed"), and a "What we could not confirm" table that names unknowns instead of hiding them.
- Product copy matches the catalog exactly, the pick cards use accurate "does not restrain" wording, and the buying checklist (measure, anchors, belt access, cleaning) is specific to covers.
