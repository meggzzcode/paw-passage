# SEO brief for every article (maximize traffic without going too niche)

Owner goal (2026-11-24): every article should be optimized to win as much search traffic as realistically possible against its competitors, while avoiding keywords so niche that nobody searches them. We have NO keyword-volume tool, so volume is judged from evidence, labeled honestly as ESTIMATE: (1) real Search Console impressions for pawsandpassage.com (first six days: "best pet grooming kit for renters" 7, "helen ga pet-friendly cabins" 4, "car safety for dogs" 4, "is airbnb or vrbo better for traveling with a dog" 3, "fully fenced yard" 3, "best dog car harness" 3, "dog car safety" 3, "best dog car seats" 2, "what's the best way to find a vacation rental that welcomes large dogs" 2, "santa barbara vacation rentals pet friendly" 2; average position 15.2); (2) Google autocomplete and People Also Ask for the candidate phrase (use WebSearch with the phrase and read what the result pages and related questions show); (3) how many strong, mainstream publishers rank (a SERP full of brands such as AKC, PetMD, Wirecutter, Rover, BringFido means high demand AND high difficulty; a SERP of thin forums, pages.dev blogs or off-topic results means low difficulty and possibly low demand); (4) the phrase's shape: a 2 to 4 word head phrase with a clear intent (for example "dog car harness", "pet friendly vacation rental checklist") beats a 9-word sentence.

## Method per article
1. Pick ONE primary keyword (2 to 5 words, real searcher language) and 3 to 5 secondary phrases. Prefer the highest-demand phrase the article can honestly rank for. A "too niche" phrase is one with no autocomplete, no PAA and no competing pages. A "too broad" phrase is one a 3-day-old site cannot realistically rank for (for example "dog travel"); step down one level to a phrase with a clear purchase or planning intent. Record the choice and the evidence in `docs/review/SEO-<slug>.md` (primary, secondary, three competing URLs you read and their title tags, 3 PAA questions the article answers or should, ESTIMATE of demand: high/medium/low with the reason).
2. Title tag: 50 to 60 characters (the repo tests enforce a 60 limit), primary keyword at or near the start, one concrete benefit or number only if the page delivers it, no "Guide:" filler, never claim anything the page does not contain. Meta description: 130 to 155 characters, primary keyword once, a clear reason to click, a call to action verb. H1 can differ from the title tag but must contain the primary keyword or a close variant. The first 100 words answer the primary question directly.
3. Headings: H2s phrase real questions or tasks (use PAA wording where it fits), one idea each; add a short "quick answer" or comparison near the top where a featured snippet is plausible (a 40 to 60 word answer paragraph, a 5 to 8 row table, or a numbered list). FAQ block: 4 to 6 questions taken from PAA and autocomplete for the primary keyword, each answered in 40 to 70 words with a source where a claim needs one; do not duplicate answers already in the body word for word.
4. Internal links: 3 to 5 contextual links to genuinely related published guides using descriptive anchor text (never "click here"), plus the matching /gear category page where a product decision is involved. Do not link to unpublished pages or to the page itself. Every published guide must have at least 2 inbound links from other guides (check and add one if needed).
5. Differentiation: name in one line what this page has that top results lack (original 38-manager pet-policy data, a decision table, sourced specifics) and make sure it appears above the fold or in the first H2.
6. Image: hero alt text and file relevance to the keyword; the pin title and description in docs/pinterest-pins.csv should echo the primary keyword (edit the row; do not change the image file here).
7. Honesty rules still apply: no invented numbers, rankings, tests, stock, prices or testimonials; do not stuff keywords; do not change factual claims to chase a keyword; keep citations consistent.
8. Structured data: the site already outputs Article and Breadcrumb JSON-LD. Do not add FAQPage markup (removed on purpose; see docs/recovery/FAQPAGE-DECISION.md).

## Done when
`docs/review/SEO-<slug>.md` exists with the evidence above, the guide's title/seoTitle/seoDescription/H1/first paragraph/FAQ/internal links are updated, tests pass (title and description length tests), and the changes are listed in the file. If the SEO choice conflicts with the 9/10 article review edits, keep the review fixes and change only the keyword wording.
