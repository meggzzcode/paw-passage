## 2. Cost-to-rebuild / asset-value method

Published inputs:
- The Website Flip (Mushfiq Sarker), "Valuing Non-Revenue Niche Sites" (undated page; fetched 2026-11-24)
  https://thewebsiteflip.com/guide/buy-website/non-revenue-niche-sites/ [SC: author is a flipper selling this framework]
  - "I value pre-existing content at 2 cents/word maximum." [PF as a statement; SC as a method]
  - Writer tiers quoted: $0.01-0.02 (non-native), $0.03-0.04 (inexperienced native), $0.05+ (top-tier).
  - Formatting $3/article (0.5 h of VA at $6/h). Domain = about $10 x years. Build-out fixed $300 (VA installs WordPress, theme, plugins).
  - Backlinks per referring domain: DR0-20 $30; DR20-30 $50; DR30-50 $100; DR50+ $200-$1,000; about $40 typical for a starter site.
- Gaurav Tiwari (2021-12-13, upd. 2026-08-31) https://gauravtiwari.org/flippa-value-sell-a-website-with-no-revenue [SC blogger]
  - "A website with no revenue is worth roughly what it would cost someone else to build the same thing."
  - "For most pre-revenue sites that lands somewhere between a few hundred dollars and about $3,000."
  - Bands: under $500 (template pages, no indexed content, under 3 months old); $500-$1,500 "the most common band for a genuine content site"; $1,500-$3,000 needs a build "visibly better than what a buyer could commission cheaply"; above $3,000 needs traffic, revenue or an independently valuable asset.
  - He states the bands are "reasoned from replacement cost and the published market structure," not compiled sales. So these are opinion, not comps.
  - "Your cost is a floor you would like to reach. The replacement cost is the ceiling, and only the ceiling binds."
- Niche Pursuits (Spencer Haws), "Costs Associated with Niche Sites ... Project 4", last updated 2022-03-14
  https://www.nichepursuits.com/?p=11664 [PF for his own spend]
  - "The average cost per article has come out to $80." Some early ones "$200+ each."
  - US writers "$10 to $15 an hour"; 1,500-word articles "$30 to $45"; 3,000-4,000 word articles "$80 to $150".
  - No per-word rate stated. Valuation shown only for profitable sites (34x monthly net).
- Niche Website Builders: about 6 cents/word from in-house UK writers [SR; vendor price, one source]. 1,000 words = $60 [AR].
- Who uses the cost method: Flippa (starter guidance, SR), Website Flip, Tiwari. Brokers with multiples (Empire Flippers, FE, Quiet Light) do not apply it to pre-revenue sites.

## 4. Aged domain and backlinks: published effect on price

The only sold-price comps that I could read are in the Website Flip case studies (all sold via Escrow; [SC] seller-reported):

| Site | Articles | Words | Domain age | Backlinks | Author's calc. | Sold | Sold / calc. [AR] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| #1 Family/parenting | 69 | 39,394 | 4 yr | 5 at DR20-30 | $1,582 | $1,600 | 101% |
| #2 Pet | 106 | 105,664 | 2 yr | 9 at DR0-20 | $3,021 | $1,500 | 50% |
| #3 Home | 56 | 52,975 | 4 yr | 6 at DR0-30 | $1,847 stated ($1,747 by my re-add of his components) | $1,700 | 92% stated / 97% re-added |

Note: the user's three known comps ($1,500-$1,700, two-year domains) look like these or very similar sites; site #2 is
the only one at two years. I cannot confirm the user's comps are these.
Implied sold $ per word [AR]: #1 $0.041; #2 $0.0142; #3 $0.0321. Implied sold $ per article [AR]: $23.2, $14.2, $30.4.

Effect of age and links:
- In the Website Flip method, domain age adds only about $10 per year, and backlinks add $30-$200 per link, so on these comps
  domain plus links were about $100-$300 of $1,500-$1,700 (roughly 5-18%) [AR]. Age and links are small next to content value.
- Domain guide (domaindetails.com) claims a 0-10% premium for 1-2 year domains and 50-200% for 15 year domains, attributed to NameBio 2020-2024 [SR, unverified].
- Dynadot (SR): value of aged domains comes from the backlinks, not the age; a "it's aged" pitch alone is not worth a premium.
- No published sold-price dataset comparing otherwise identical new vs aged content sites was found.
- A one-week-old domain gets about $10 domain value and $0 links in this method [AR]. Flippa's Starter view caps at 6 months old, so a
  week-old site has no age credibility; that is the main gap versus the comps.

## 5. Discounts, risks, and evidence that raises price

- AI content: Originality.ai study of Motion Invest sales, published 2025-11-13, Jonathan Gillham
  https://originality.ai/blog/ai-website-sales-study
  - "Human content-only websites sold for 39% more than websites that had AI content on them." [PF as reported]
  - n = 28 sales (11 AI, 17 human), May 2024 to April 2025, only sales of $6,000 or more.
  - Sites without AI averaged 35 days on market; AI sites 54 days, "19 more days, or 54% longer."
  - Caveats stated by authors: correlation; "other valuation factors may also come into play"; seller self-reported AI use; author's company sells the AI detector. [SC-grade evidence]
  - Not applicable to sub-$6,000 sales, which is where this site sits. Use as a directional discount only.
- Forecast-blog claims of 15-30% and 30-50% discounts for AI-heavy catalogs: no primary data; not used.
- Flippa guidance (SR): clean up low-value AI pages before sale.
- Market-wide: broker blog reports content-site multiples fell from 35-40x monthly profit (2021) to about 24x (2025), blamed on AI-search traffic loss [SC/SR]. Another broker reports sales at 82% of ask versus 90% in 2023 [SC/SR]. Both are about profitable sites.
- "No traffic" risk: the Tiwari bands put sub-$500 on "no indexed content, under 3 months old" [SC]. The key buyer worry is that nothing is proven.
- Evidence that raises price (from the sources above; none gives a dollar uplift): indexation, Search Console impressions history, earned backlinks (priced $30-$1,000 each in the Website Flip method), verified analytics (Motion Invest asks for Google Analytics access and dashboards), first revenue (Motion Invest minimum $20/month; Empire Flippers needs $2,000/month for 12 months), and a build "visibly better than what a buyer could commission cheaply" (Tiwari).
- Affiliate accounts not transferable: no source prices this directly. Buyers re-apply to Amazon Associates etc.; note the Associates 180-day qualifying-sales rule is not verified here, so I do not rely on it. Effect on price: unquantified.
- Original dataset, 3 interactive tools, 500-test codebase, 32-page programmatic directory: no published valuation method prices these. Dev-time at market rates could argue a higher replacement cost, but buyers of $1-3k sites do not pay for engineering; I found no data either way. Treat as [unpriced upside].

## 6. Time to sell and failure rate

- Motion Invest, sales of $6,000+ (May 2024 to Apr 2025): 35 days (human) and 54 days (AI) on market [PF, Originality.ai study].
- Niche Pursuits: "about 11.1 days to sell a site on average," 5-7 days to get live [SC/SR, Motion Invest founder-linked source].
- StreamSEO: broker average 55.8 days, self-reported; Quiet Light claims 12 days [SC/SR].
- Exitbid: brokered sales 2-6+ months; marketplace listings 1-3 months before an offer, plus 30-60 days diligence and transfer [SC/SR].
- All of these are for profitable sites that passed vetting. No source gave a share of listings that fail to sell, and none covers starter sites. The sell-through rate for starter sites is UNKNOWN.
  Flippa's many $9,999-asking starter listings suggest asks far above realized prices, but that is my inference [AR/opinion].

## 7. Calculator (asset-value method)

Inputs: 30 articles, 77,000 words (avg 2,567 words/article [AR]), domain 1 week old, no backlinks.
Fixed adders from Website Flip [SC]: formatting $3 x 30 = $90; domain $10 x 1 = $10 (use $10 minimum); build $300; backlinks $0.
Fixed sum = $400 [AR].

### 7a. Per-word method (words x $/word + fixed)

| Case | $/word (source) | Content [AR] | + $400 fixed | Total [AR] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $0.01 (non-native tier) | $770 | | $1,170 |
| Mid | $0.02 (Website Flip max for existing content) | $1,540 | | $1,940 |
| Mid-high | $0.03 (inexperienced native) | $2,310 | | $2,710 |
| High | $0.05 (top-tier writer cost; Flippa "5c" example) | $3,850 | | $4,250 |
| Flippa 10c cited for top-tier (SR) | $0.10 | $7,700 | | $8,100 (treat as theoretical cost, never a sale-price method) |

### 7b. Per-article method (articles x $/article + fixed)

| Case | $/article (source) | Content [AR] | + $400 | Total [AR] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | $45 (Haws 1,500-word article, top of $30-45) | $1,350 | | $1,750 |
| Mid | $80 (Haws reported average) | $2,400 | | $2,800 |
| High | $150 (Haws 3,000-4,000 word article, top of range) | $4,500 | | $4,900 |

Our articles average about 2,567 words, between his 1,500 and 3,000-4,000 buckets, so the $45 to $150 span brackets them [AR].
Replacement cost for a research-heavy article (sources, FAQs, fact-checks) would be above the cheap-writer rate; I have no published figure.

### 7c. Haircut from realised sales (comps)

Sold/calc ratio in the three comps: 50%, 92-97%, 101% [AR]. Applying 50% to 100% to the mid per-word figure ($1,940):
$970 to $1,940 [AR]. Applying to the 7a Low-to-High span: $585 to $4,250 [AR].

### 7d. Comparison to the three known comps

- Direct $/word scaling of comps to 77,000 words [AR]: $1,093 (pet), $2,471 (home), $3,127 (family).
  Those comps include 2-4 year domains and 5-9 backlinks (worth about $100-$300 in the method), so subtract about $200 for our site: roughly $900 to $2,900 [AR].
- Direct $/article scaling to 30 articles [AR]: $426 (pet), $696 (family), $912 (home). Shows that per-article scaling understates us, since our articles are far longer (2,567 words vs 372-997 words per article in the comps [AR]).
- Our content is about 77k words, inside the comps' span (39k-106k), so the $1,500-$1,700 band is a fair anchor for pure content value.

### 7e. Adjustments (all [AR] on published percentages)

- AI discount, if a buyer judges the content AI-assisted: x0.61 (from 39% more for human content: 1/1.39 = 0.72, so a discount of about 28% is the strict inverse; 39% off is the harsh reading). Applied to $1,940: $1,396 (strict inverse) to $1,183 (harsh) [AR]. The study is not about sub-$6k sales.
- No-age adjustment: the comps' domains were 2-4 years old; our week-old domain gets about $10 and Flippa's "Starter" view targets under 6 months, so the comps are an upper anchor.
- Possible upside: a better build, original data and tools are listed by Tiwari as the way to reach his $1,500-$3,000 band; no dollar rule exists.

## 8. What the methods imply

| Scenario | Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Floor / quick sale (distress, cheap-bundle comparison) | $300-$800 | Tiwari "under $500" band for thin sites; Motion Invest starter $800-$1,000 [SR]; Flippa $300 parenting listing [SR] |
| Realistic realised price, untested no-traffic site | $800-$2,000 | Per-word mid $1,940 [AR]; comps $1,500-$1,700 [PF/SC]; 50% haircut $970 |
| Optimistic with a motivated buyer valuing the build, tools and data | $2,000-$3,500 | Tiwari $1,500-$3,000 band [SC]; 7a mid-high $2,710 [AR] |
| Theoretical replacement-cost ceiling | $4,000-$5,000 | 7a high $4,250, 7b high $4,900 [AR]; no published sale evidence at this level |
| Not reachable | $9,999 asks | Seen on Flippa starter listings, no sold evidence |

Realistic central estimate (my judgement from the above): about $1,000-$2,000, with a hard ceiling near $3,500
unless Search Console history, indexation, impressions or first commissions can be shown. Confidence:
moderate on the order of magnitude (several independent sources, including actual sold comps, land at
$1,500-$3,000); low on the exact figure, because the sold-comp set is three seller-reported sales from
one author, Flippa and broker primary pages were blocked, and there is no data on sell-through
rates, the effect of non-transferable affiliate accounts, or any premium for the dataset, tools and codebase.
