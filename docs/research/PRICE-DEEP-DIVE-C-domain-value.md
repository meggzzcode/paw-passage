## 4. Domain-only vs domain-plus-content (as published)
[PUBLISHED] General guides (IONOS, Hostinger, Flippa, Ahrefs, via search 2026-11-24):
- Domain worth is the standalone value of the name; website value covers traffic, content, revenue
  ("plot of land versus the house").
- Domain is one component of a website's valuation; websites are commonly priced as monthly net profit x multiple.
- Starter sites sell for low (three-figure) prices on marketplaces; Empire Flippers lists only sites with
  $2,000+/month net profit and prices from $50,000, so it is irrelevant to a starter site.
- Marketplaces take a commission; direct sales avoid broker fees.
- No source gave a fixed % for the domain's share of a starter-site price. [NOT VERIFIED]
[ESTIMATE] For a site with no traffic or revenue, nearly all price is domain + build labor/content cost + affiliate
setup; buyers of starter sites typically anchor on replacement cost (hours of work) and the domain asking price.
The domain can be priced at the top of its range only if the site demonstrates a concrete use case.

## 5. New-domain age premium or discount
[PUBLISHED] Sedo (vendor claim) says aged domains carry higher resale value
(https://sedo.com/us/about-us/news-press/newsroom/why-the-age-of-a-domain-matters/). A NamePros NameBio analysis
found only a weak price-age correlation, with oldest names selling higher mainly because they are higher quality.
Investor commentary: a "mortgage.com registered yesterday" would be worth no less; age is a slight bonus
(domaininvesting.com daily poll; Spaceship and Dynadot blogs say age alone does not guarantee value).
Conclusion [ESTIMATE]: no evidence of a premium or discount for being under one month old per se; the "no history"
point matters only through absence of backlinks/traffic and, for a starter-site buyer, absence of proven SEO.
One real discount: a domain registered 8 days ago has no renewal track record and no proof it is not a speculative
registration that can be copied; and new domains cannot be transferred for 60 days after registration under ICANN
policy [ESTIMATE from general knowledge; not re-verified today], which affects delivery timing.

travel niche exactly; "paws" is a strong pet keyword (BrandBucket asks $1.6K-$4.3K for paws-derived names).
Down [ESTIMATE]: 18 characters (pawsandpassage = 16 letters + ".com" is 20; letters-only count is 16, the 18 in the

niche startups; animal-themed names showed weaker STR in Squadhelp data; brand risk (below); fresh registration

## 7. Realistic value range (all ESTIMATES; no hard comps obtained)
- Wholesale / what an investor would pay today for a fresh unproven brandable: roughly $0 to $200.
- Realistic retail end-user outcome if sold: low hundreds to about $1,000-$1,500, most plausibly $300-$900 to a
  pet-travel entrepreneur, after weeks to years of exposure. Probability of any sale within 12 months is low (a ~1%
  portfolio STR figure is cited by investors for $2-5K-priced names).
- Ceiling anchored on BrandBucket asks: $2,000-$3,500 as an ASK on a curated marketplace for a shorter, cleaner
  name; this name's 'and' construction and length argue for the lower end, and asking is not selling.
- In a starter-site sale: domain is plausibly 30-60% of a no-traffic site's price (ESTIMATE; no source gives a
  split). Content, design, and affiliate setup are priced by replacement cost, usually small-three-figure to
  low-four-figure total.
Realistic domain-only range: about $250 to $1,000. Confidence: low to moderate (about 35-40%) because NameBio, DNJournal,
Sedo, and Afternic sold data were not reachable and no verified comp exists for this exact pattern.
