# Independent reader review: protect-your-car-interior-from-a-dog-on-a-road-trip

Method: Playwright Chromium at 390 and 1280 px, first-screen and full-page screenshots viewed, rendered text and links read. Page height 11,278 px at 390 (about 13 phone screens), about 1,850 words. No horizontal overflow; all internal links return 200. No user testing was done.

## Scenario: "I need something to protect my car for a road trip"
Can the reader tell which interior protection fits their vehicle arrangement? Mostly yes. The title names three product types, the dek says "Find where your dog rides, and the right one is usually obvious", and "Start with where your dog rides" gives three bullets: back seat, cargo area, front seat ("Skip it"). "Options by setup" then maps setups to products with a "FOR ..." kicker on each card ("FOR ONE DOG ON A CAR OR SUV BACK SEAT", "FOR A BIG DOG OR TWO IN A TRUCK OR LARGE SUV", "FOR A DOG BEHIND THE REAR SEAT", "FOR A DOG UP TO 30 LB IN A BUCKET SEAT", "FOR A RENTAL THAT REQUIRES A CARRIER"). A reader can see why each product appears. Gaps: nothing addresses an SUV with the rear seats folded down and the dog on the extended floor (the lead mentions "folded seat backs" but no pick says how to cover them); the one-dog back-seat pick does not say what size/temperament it assumes, while the prose says bench covers suit "small, calm dogs" and the card is labeled only "one dog"; the reader with a medium dog on a car back seat is left choosing between a bench cover and a hammock on weak guidance ("small, calm" vs "large or active", both from a seller, Seat Cover Solutions, which the page itself flags as "a seller").

## Role 1: first-time search visitor
- First screen (390): title, dek, photo of two pugs on a back seat. The decision frame is not visible until after photo, buttons, disclosure and TOC. Once reached, the opening paragraph is good and short.
- Repeated answers (same three-way split restated): lead paragraph ("On a back seat it's the cushions and the footwell. In an SUV it's the cargo floor..."); bullets ("Back seat... A bench cover or a hammock covers it. Cargo area... A cargo liner... covers it."); "Bench cover. A flat cover over the rear seat..." / "Hammock..." / "Cargo liner..." paragraphs; then "Options by setup" repeats each again with a card; then FAQ ("What is the difference between a bench seat cover and a hammock?", "Which one is right for an SUV where the dog rides in the cargo area?"). Also "does not restrain / not crash protection" appears in the lead ("It doesn't restrain your dog, and nothing here claims crash protection"), the Vetstreet sentence, card chips ("Not a restraint"), "Common mistakes" ("Trusting a cover to keep a dog safe in a crash. It doesn't.") and the FAQ.
- Terminology wobble: "cargo liner" in headings and prose, "cape" in the title and cards ("Kurgo calls its version a cape"), "Cargo area" kicker. Minor but visible.
- A "short cleanup routine" and "Common mistakes" sections at the end are generic and partly repeat earlier advice (towel off the dog, lint roller, vacuum).

## Role 2: mobile traveler planning a trip
- Layout at 390 is clean; cards stack with kicker, chips, summary, "Why we picked it", button. No tables (none needed; a three-row "where the dog rides" table would not be better than the bullets).
- Cards break the flow only mildly: each follows its lead-in paragraph. Five cards at about 330 px each is the bulk of the page; two of them (Heather booster, Happy Ride crate) are not interior protection and the prose says so ("comfort plus a tether point, not upholstery protection"; "contained, not restrained"). For someone who asked to protect the car, they are detours, though the rental-car paragraph (Enterprise "pet hair and dander", carrier rule) is a genuinely useful travel point.
- Useful practical checks ("Buckle access", "Tether reach", "Headrest straps", "Slip check") are clear and honest that they are "our suggestions".
- The full-page screenshot showed the sticky header overlapping the hero; this is a capture artifact of the sticky nav, not a layout bug in normal scrolling.

## Role 3: skeptical reader
- Disclosure: one top notice with Amazon Associate sentence, "How we earn money" link, no per-card labels. Person shift "we may earn ... As an Amazon Associate I earn". Notice sits below the hero image.
- Sources: inline named publisher links (ASPCA, Chewy, Seat Cover Solutions, Vetstreet, Nationwide, NAPA, Play Pet Brands, Enterprise, maker pages). Good, and the page says "Those are retailer views, not tests." Sources list collapsed (13). The sentence "None of the maker pages we read lists a vehicle compatibility chart or weight limit, so the tape measure is the real check" is honest. Possible mismatch to check: the "Kurgo" hammock source URL contains "wander-hammock-extended-width" while the card is named "Rover Hammock, Extended Width".
- Card text repeats: "Why we picked it" duplicates the line above it and the chips (PetSafe bench: "45 by 56 in, waterproof quilted cover with headrest straps, seat anchors and a high-grip bottom; seat belt access without removing it; machine washable" repeated verbatim in spirit from the lead-in, summary and chips; "machine washable" appears three times in one card).
- Safety: the guide is careful: no crash claims, ASPCA restraint guidance, hammock "isn't an alternative to more secure methods". A cargo cape for a dog riding unrestrained behind the rear seat is mentioned with "A cape isn't a barrier or a crate", but the link to restraint advice is a cross-reference rather than a warning at the point of recommendation. Acceptable.
- Hedging: "Those are retailer views, not tests", "We read no other company's terms", "may satisfy a carrier rule, but confirm that with the company, and it may not fit a compact car" is a lot of qualification on one product.

## Verdict: READY WITH MINOR FIXES
1. Collapse the three passes over bench / hammock / cargo (bullets, subsection, "Options by setup") into two; drop the repeated "not a restraint" statements down to the lead and one card chip.
2. Add one line for the SUV with folded rear seats and for a medium dog on a car back seat (which of bench cover or hammock), instead of relying on seller views.
3. Use one term (cargo cape or cargo liner) consistently.
4. Reconsider the Heather booster card (not interior protection) or move it out of the main option list; shorten the Happy Ride hedging.
5. Fix "As an Amazon Associate I earn" to "we"; verify the Kurgo hammock source URL matches the Rover Hammock product; trim duplicate "Why we picked it" text.
