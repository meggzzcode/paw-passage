You found a cabin with a "pets welcome" badge, paid attention to the pet fee, and then, on the manager's separate pet-policy page, found a list of banned breeds that your dog may or may not be on. Or there is no breed list at all, just a weight limit that your dog is a few pounds over. This guide is for checking that before you pay.

It is built on 17 real pet policies we compiled, with a source and a date for each row. It is a small sample, not a survey of the market, and policies change. We label how sure we are: CONFIRMED means a primary source we read says it; WHAT MOST PEOPLE REPORT means at least three independent items we read agree, in a small non-random sample; UNKNOWN means nothing we read settles it. Where we quote forums, they are mostly host-side, and we say how many sources agree.

## The short answer

- Hosts and managers set their own pet rules. The Airbnb pages we read say hosts decide which pets to accept and do not mention breed. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463)
- The managers who explain their breed lists point to insurance, for example Costanoa: "Due to our property insurance policy breed restrictions must apply." [[12]](https://www.costanoa.com/stay/pet-policies)
- In our sample, weight caps were more common than named breed lists. By our inference, a cap can exclude your dog without any breed being named.
- "Pet friendly" does not tell you your dog passes. In several policies we read, the breed or weight rule sat on a separate policy page or in the house rules.
- A breed or weight line can be missing, cut short or kept in a separate agreement. Screenshot the listing's pet wording when you book.
- Ask first and get the answer in writing. Unauthorized dogs can cost real money (see the table below).

## Can a vacation rental really refuse my dog because of its breed?

**Short answer:** In the policies we read, yes. Airbnb's hosting page says hosts clarify what types of pets they accept, and the managers below publish their own breed lists, weight caps and approval rules. Nothing we read gave a traveler a right to bring a dog the host did not approve, apart from service animals.

Airbnb's ground rules say guests must not bring pets into a listing with a "no pets" rule or "more pets than are approved." [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2894) The Airbnb hosting-pets page lets hosts set a pet limit of 1 to 5 and says the pet fee is built into the nightly price. It does not mention breed. [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463)

Vacasa's pet page says limits are "clearly stated at the top of each listing," that only dogs authorized in the reservation are allowed, and it has no breed rule on that page. [[3]](https://www.vacasa.com/pet-friendly-vacation-rentals) Vrbo's house-rules help page tells hosts to say whether guests can bring pets, "how many, what type of pet, and any size restriction." It does not mention breed. [[28]](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-are-House-Rules) Vrbo's service-animal help article returned an error page for us, so we make no claim about it.

Two forum reports about where rules show up, each from one thread, so not patterns. On OwnerRez, a host-side forum, a poster said Vrbo's pet house-rule field has a limit of 50 characters and was cut off, and a second poster agreed the space is very limited (2024). [[29]](https://www.ownerrez.com/forums/general-help/issue-with-vrbo-house-rules-for-pets) And one Airhosts host wrote that pet rules "MUST be listed in the rules or it is not enforceable" (2019). [[32]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/pet-addendum-provided-by-host-for-guests-to-sign/36443) We could not check that claim, and a host's view is not a ruling. The practical point is for you: a breed or weight line may be short or missing in the listing, so ask in writing and save a screenshot of what you saw.

One accurate correction to a common belief: "Airbnb bans certain breeds" is not supported by the pages we read. The rule you hit is usually the host's or the manager's.

## Why do vacation rentals restrict breeds?

**Short answer:** The managers who explain their lists usually cite insurance. Hosts in forum threads say the same. We did not read an insurance policy, and insurers differ, so treat insurance as the stated reason, not a proven one in every case.

What managers say:

- Costanoa: "Due to our property insurance policy breed restrictions must apply." [[12]](https://www.costanoa.com/stay/pet-policies)
- Beach Realty: "certain breeds considered dangerous by insurance companies are not permitted." [[14]](https://www.beachrealtync.com/node/707)

What insurance and host-software pages say (vendor pages, so read them as such):

- Betterpet, a pet-focused content site (it does not say it sells insurance), says some host policies "may not cover any damages caused by excluded breeds." [[4]](https://www.betterpet.com/learn/how-to-design-a-pet-policy-for-your-vacation-rental)
- Proper, a specialist insurer, says its policy "does not have exclusions for types of animals or dog breeds." This is marketing copy and we did not check policy wording. [[5]](https://www.proper.insure/blog/pros-and-cons-of-allowing-pets-at-your-rental/) If it is accurate, a host with a different insurer is not necessarily forced into a breed ban, which is a fair thing to ask about politely.
- Hostaway's host guide says hosts must enforce insurance or HOA limits "regardless of your personal feelings." [[6]](https://www.hostaway.com/vacation-rental-guide/pet-friendly-hosting/policies/)

Anecdotes from host forums (not rules): on one Airhosts thread a host named breed insurance as the concrete risk, such as rate rises or an exclusion, while several hosts advised disclosing a pit bull with a photo, and one said they would not stay at a listing with a pit bull. [[20]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/disclosing-breed-specific-pit-bull-etc-dogs-in-listing/49489) Landlords on BiggerPockets, writing about rentals leased to tenants (apparently long-term, not vacation stays), also name insurers as a source of breed rules. [[21]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/52/topics/944633-restricted-breed-pets-and-insurance) [[23]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/52/topics/327336-dogs-and-landlords) One landlord there says a breed list scares dog owners away and he accepts that. [[22]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/52/topics/1010645-do-you-guys-restrict-dog-breeds-at-your-rentals)

**What most people report (small, non-random sample, mostly host-side):** insurance is the reason named most often. It appears in 6 forum threads we read (one Airhosts thread and five BiggerPockets threads, of which five appear to be about long-term rentals) and in 2 manager pages (Costanoa and Beach Realty). The three newest BiggerPockets threads: a landlord whose tenant's pit bull is "on my insurance co's list of dogs they won't insure" [[38]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/52/topics/725277-ideas-on-tenant-pet-liability-insurance-for-pitbulls); another who writes "I don't allow certain breeds because my insurance will not cover them" [[39]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/897/topics/1044345-everybody-has-a-pitbull); and a third more afraid of "my insurance company canceling on me" than of refusing an emotional-support animal [[40]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/52/topics/512374-pet-policy-breed-restriction-san-diego). One poster in the first thread adds that "There are companies which do insure pit bulls." [[38]](https://www.biggerpockets.com/forums/52/topics/725277-ideas-on-tenant-pet-liability-insurance-for-pitbulls) Opinion splits on whether breed rules make sense: some hosts and landlords back them, and the Best Friends article quotes an expert calling them a false sense of security. This is a pattern in what we read, not a rule, and it says little about what vacation-rental insurers do.

**Not every rule is about insurance, as far as we can tell.** One Airhosts host wrote that "Corgis now on the (very short) banned list!" (2021), a personal list with no insurer named. [[34]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/pets-allowed-cats-vs-dogs/48769) That is one report, so we cannot say how common it is. Going the other way, hosts in three Airhosts threads said the individual dog or its training matters more than size or breed. One wrote "Any size or breed of dog can cause or not cause issues." [[36]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/extra-charge-for-dogs/60921) Another: "I prefer to treat dogs as individuals rather than breeds when possible." [[20]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/disclosing-breed-specific-pit-bull-etc-dogs-in-listing/49489) A third wrote that "some of the biggest dogs are the best dogs." [[37]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/dogs-or-no-dogs-pros-and-cons/56922) Those threads share a few regular posters, and hosts who take dogs are likely over-represented in a thread about whether to take them, so we do not count this as a pattern of three independent voices.

Best Friends, an advocacy group, says insurers can act on a pet's appearance, and that a model act NCOIL passed in 2022 bars action "based solely on the specific breed." [[7]](https://bestfriends.org/node/679665) We could not confirm whether that act reaches short-term-rental host policies, or which states adopted it.

## Which breeds are on the lists, and what about weight?

**Short answer:** When a manager publishes a list, it can be long. Elk Springs names 12 breeds. But the wall is often a weight cap instead. In our 17 rows, 7 have a weight cap of 35 to 60 lb (one of them reported only), and 3 name breeds (again one reported only). This is a small sample, not a measure of the market.

Examples from the table below: Elk Springs bans dogs over 50 lb and lists 12 breeds. Costanoa lists breeds and their crosses, with a 50 lb limit. Cabin Rentals of Georgia says "we only allow certain breeds" without naming them. Cabins of the Smoky Mountains lists no breed but caps combined dog weight at 60 lb. Dog size and breed are different things, and a rule that names one can catch dogs that are the other.

**Our inference, from a small non-random sample:** a cap in the 35 to 60 lb range can exclude many large dogs, shepherds, retrievers, hounds and mixes alike, without any breed being named. So when you read "no breed restrictions," also read the weight line.

In our separate read of 38 manager pet pages for the [how-many-dogs guide](/guides/how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental), only 5 mentioned breed limits and only 2 named breeds; 31 said nothing. Both samples are small and non-random, and neither is a survey, so use them as a reason to read your own listing, not as odds.

**Two dogs: watch for combined limits.** Our table has a manager that caps dogs at "60 lb total" (row 8) and a reported combined 60 lb at row 16. An Airhosts host in 2021 quoted what they said was an Airbnb pet message: "the maximum combined pet mass is 36 kg (80 lbs)." [[33]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/airs-new-pet-policy/50459?page=4) That is one host's quote from 2021, and we could not confirm that Airbnb sends it today. With two dogs, add their weights and ask whether the cap is per dog or combined. Our [how-many-dogs guide](/guides/how-many-dogs-can-you-bring-to-a-vacation-rental) covers pet counts.

**Rules may be shown differently in different places.** One host who also travels with a dog wrote on Airhosts that "this info is inconsistently communicated," and described a hotel whose pet weight limit was shown as 40, 45 or 50 lb depending on where they looked. [[30]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/sneaking-pets-in/55303) One poster, so a single report, but it is a good reason to compare the listing, the house rules and the manager's page and ask which one applies.

A "pet friendly" label can also cover surprising lists. One traveler with a Golden Retriever described an agency listing, dated in the post, whose shedding concerns named Labs, Goldens, Bernese, Corgis and Newfoundlands. They did not book. ## What if my dog is a mix and nobody can tell the breed?

**Short answer:** Ask how the host treats mixes. Costanoa's list covers crosses of the breeds it names. For others we could not find what is done with mixes, or what proof a host accepts. Do not guess a breed for your own dog on a form.

Search summaries we saw said shelter staff are often wrong about the breed makeup of mixed dogs and that DNA tests can disagree between brands. We could not open those studies, so we do not give figures. No host or manager page we read says what proof it accepts for a mix. Whether a DNA result convinces a host is something we could not confirm.

Best Friends' piece on pets and housing quotes an expert calling breed and size limits "a false sense of security," and the ASPCA says it opposes state or local laws that regulate dogs based on breed. [[8]](https://bestfriends.org/node/711828) [[9]](https://www.aspca.org/about-us/aspca-policy-and-position-statements/position-statement-breed-specific-legislation) These are advocacy positions. They do not bind a private host, and we are not saying any breed is safe or dangerous. A host's rule is about a category, not your individual dog, and we are not judging any breed.

## Do local breed laws affect a trip?

**Short answer:** Possibly, but we could verify only one example. Check the official code for the place you are visiting. We could not confirm rules in other cities, so we do not list them.

Denver is the one we checked, through a news report rather than the city's own page. The Colorado Sun says pit bulls are legal in Denver with a breed-restricted permit, with a maximum of two per household and a $30 annual fee. It does not address visitors. [[10]](https://coloradosun.com/2025/10/06/are-pit-bulls-legal-to-own-in-denver/) We could not confirm whether that applies to a visiting dog. Look at the local animal-control page for the destination, and ask the host or manager.

## What about service animals?

Service animals are not pets. Airbnb's ground rules say service animals "are not considered pets," and the hosting-pets page says a host cannot charge a pet fee for one. [[1]](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2894) [[2]](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/what-you-need-to-know-about-hosting-pets-463) Where the ADA applies, DOJ says a fee or deposit required of pet owners must be waived for a service animal. [[25]](https://www.ada.gov/resources/service-animals-2010-requirements/) Emotional support animals depend on location, according to Airbnb. This is general information, not legal advice; rules vary by place and host. Do not describe a pet as a service animal to avoid a rule.

Hosts argue about this in public, and what they say is opinion. On one Airhosts thread a host asked whether a retired pit bull service dog counted, and a reply said hosts cannot refuse a service dog "unless it's a shared space." [[35]](https://airhostsforum.com/t/can-we-refuse-a-service-dog/49537) A Manitoba family told CTV News that "a host tried to cancel their booking because of a service dog" (2022); that is the outlet's summary of one case, with no breed mentioned. [[42]](https://vancouverisland.ctvnews.ca/winnipeg/article/hes-allowed-everywhere-family-soured-on-airbnb-after-service-dog-push-back) We read no ruling on whether a dog's breed can ever be a ground to refuse a service dog at a vacation rental. For a real question, ask the platform and a disability-rights or housing attorney.

## Should I ask about the dogs already at the property?

**Short answer:** It is a fair question, and cheap to ask. This is one reported incident, not a pattern, and it says nothing about any breed in general.

WSB-TV reported in October 2026 that a Florida guest said a pit bull killed her emotional support dog at a Smyrna, Georgia Airbnb, after breaking through a wooden privacy fence in the backyard. Per the article, animal services said the dog may stay because its owner identifies it as a service dog, Airbnb said it gave her a full refund, and the host added reinforced fencing. [[41]](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/local/woman-demands-answers-after-pit-bull-kills-her-dog-airbnb/KCVBCSP2LZA7BMXBW2HP7ABPBE/) It is a single news report of an unproven account. What it supports is a plain habit: ask whether other dogs live at or visit the property, and how the yard is fenced.

## What should I send the host?

Documents and gestures sometimes suggested on advice pages for long-term renters (REPORTED by those pages, and we did not read them in full): vaccination records, proof of spay or neuter, a city license, training certificates, a Canine Good Citizen certificate, a letter from a vet or previous landlord, photos, a meeting, and a DNA test.

**We found no first-person story of a restricted-breed dog getting a yes at a vacation rental, and no evidence that vet letters, Canine Good Citizen or DNA tests work for one.** Advice pages we saw (REPORTED, not read in full) say some insurer-driven managers cannot make exceptions. The evidence is thin; these are the things an owner can offer, not things that are known to change the answer. Our own advice is to be accurate about breed and weight, because the booking depends on the dog you describe.

Copy and paste, then fill in the brackets:

> Hi [Host], we are interested in [listing] for [dates]. We would like to bring [name], a [weight] lb [breed or mix], [age], who is spayed/neutered, current on vaccines, house-trained and crate-trained. Could you tell me whether your insurance or house rules restrict breed or weight, the total pet fee, whether the yard is fenced, and whether other dogs live at or visit the property? Does your rule cover mixes, and would you consider any documents for an exception? I can send vaccination records and a short reference. We would like your reply in writing before we book, and the pet rule to appear in the listing or house rules.

If there is no reply, ask once more through the booking platform, and do not book on a guess. If the answer is no, ask politely whether a sister property takes your dog.

- Use the platform's pet filter, then read the house rules and the manager's pet page for breed and weight lines. For example, Twiddy's page lists no manager-wide breed rule but says individual homes can set limits on "number, size, or type." [[17]](https://www.twiddy.com/outer-banks/guests/pets/)

## How we label certainty

**Bottom line:** Confirmed is the strongest label, "what most people report" is weaker than it sounds, and Unknown means nothing we read settles it.

**Confirmed** means a manager, platform or agency page we read says it, with a citation. **What most people report** means at least three independent items agree, always from a small, non-random sample that is mostly host-side forums (Airhosts and BiggerPockets, dated 2017 to 2024, plus one OwnerRez thread), and we give the count. We use it only for insurance being the reason named most often. A claim from one or two sources is written as a single report. **Unknown** means nothing we read settles it. We found almost no first-person guest account of a breed refusal at a vacation rental. TripAdvisor and Airbnb Community pages were not readable for us, and we did not use Reddit. Quotes are from the pages as we read them on 2026-11-18, and forum posters are not named.
