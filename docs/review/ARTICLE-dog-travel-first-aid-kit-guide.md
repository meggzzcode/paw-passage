## Top 5 issues (most important first)

1. **The only kit featured contains the items the article tells the reader to avoid, and there is no alternative (Buying advice 3).**
   - Where: the Trail Dog paragraph ("For a trail layer, Adventure Medical Kits' Trail Dog kit...") and "Add-ons worth carrying, and what to leave out".
   - What is wrong: the catalog and the card list "3 single-use triple antibiotic packets, 3% hydrogen peroxide (1 oz) and 2 diphenhydramine 25 mg tablets". The article says "Skip human medications unless your vet says otherwise", "Leave out... hydrogen peroxide... antibiotic ointment you have not cleared with your vet" and that the AVMA says not to use peroxide on wounds. The text handles it with one line ("take that list to your vet before the trip") but the buyer still has only one product to buy, and it is a trail-sized kit. The "Pick by situation" table recommends "a larger ready-made or built kit" for the cabin week with no product, no example of what to check, and no link. The reader who needs a cabin kit leaves without a pick.
   - Fix (PRODUCT/TEXT): there is no second kit in the catalog, so do not invent one. Either (a) say plainly in the Trail Dog paragraph and in the table that the cabin and car layers are "a kit you build or a larger ready-made kit held up against the checklist above; we have not featured one because we could not read a maker's contents list for it", and give a short "build it yourself" shopping list from the AVMA brochure with no brand; or (b) ask the owner to add one or two more kit records (maker pages read and recorded) and then add cards. Option (a) is applicable now. Add to the Trail Dog card's `watchOuts` (in the `gear` array) a line: "Skip it if you do not want a kit that includes medications; remove or set them aside with your vet's advice."

2. **The second pick, dog wipes, is not first aid and sits in a first-aid article (Product placement 3).**
   - Where: the paragraph "Mud, pond water and trail dirt are the other thing a trail or cabin day brings back." and its card.
   - What is wrong: the page itself says "They are a cleanup item, not wound care". It is a rental-cleanup product with a different category (`rental-cleanup`), placed in the middle of the kit-buying advice, and the page's own buying checklist does not mention wipes. A buyer looking for a kit may read the card as part of the kit. In "Picks by criteria" the two cards appear as equals.
   - Fix (STRUCTURE): move the wipes paragraph and card out of "A kit for the cabin, the car and the trail" into a short last section "After the trail (not first aid)" placed before "Keep the kit current", or drop the card from this guide and keep the product in the cabin or rental cleanup guides. Whichever is chosen, remove it from `relatedProducts` if dropped.

3. **The headline claim "what ready-made kits usually lack" rests on one source and one kit (Trust 4, SEO 4).**
   - Where: meta description, pin description, "The short answer" bullet 2, FAQ "Is a ready-made dog first-aid kit enough?".
   - What is wrong: the claim that ready-made kits "usually lack a digital thermometer, contact numbers and your dog's records" is supported by: Preventive Vet's statement that "many lack necessary items", and the observation that the Trail Dog lists no thermometer, muzzle or flashlight. That is one veterinary guide and one product, not a survey of kits; the page does not say so. In addition "Picks by criteria" prints "We haven't tested these products. They were chosen against the criteria above using the maker's published specifications and independent standards, which are linked for each one", but the page links no independent standard for either product (only maker pages).
   - Fix (TEXT): change the meta description to "A travel kit built on the AVMA checklist, plus the items worth adding to a ready-made kit: a digital thermometer, contact numbers and your dog's records." In "The short answer" bullet 2 and the FAQ say "one veterinary-written guide says many pre-made kits lack necessary items; the Trail Dog, for example, lists no thermometer, muzzle or flashlight". Ask the owner to correct or remove the boilerplate "independent standards, which are linked for each one" on the page template for this guide (it is not accurate here).

4. **No pick or concrete guidance for the three items the page calls essential: thermometer, muzzle, cabin kit (Product placement 3, Buying advice 3).**
   - Where: "Add-on: a muzzle" and "Add-ons worth carrying".
   - What is wrong: "a digital thermometer, a fitted muzzle and a card with your contacts" are the "What to buy" answer, yet there is no product and no checklist of what to confirm for a thermometer (the page says only "ask your vet how and what readings should prompt a call"). The muzzle section gives buying criteria (basket style, sizing method, use limits) which is good, but it ends with no card, and the heading "Heat, cold and the outdoors" contains only heat advice.
   - Fix (TEXT/STRUCTURE): add to "Add-ons worth carrying" a buying line for the thermometer using only AVMA wording: "The AVMA checklist lists a digital thermometer; ask your vet which type and what reading should prompt a call." Rename the heading "Heat, cold and the outdoors" to "Heatstroke and the outdoors" (the section has no cold content). If the owner wants cards, add catalog records for one thermometer and one basket muzzle from maker pages first; do not add them unsourced.

5. **Length and structure: 11 minutes and a late product section (Reader flow 4).**
   - Where: order of sections; "Picks by criteria".
   - What is wrong: the products first appear well after the AVMA checklist, poison control, travel changes and ready-made-vs-build; "Ready-made kit or build your own?" is only two sentences and is the actual buying decision. "Pick by situation" table comes after the product cards it summarizes. "Picks by criteria" at the end repeats both cards in full (spec bullets plus watch-outs) after they have already appeared inline. The page is about 22,000 px on a phone.
   - Fix (STRUCTURE): move "Pick by situation" to directly after "Ready-made kit or build your own?" so the decision comes before the product detail; expand "Ready-made kit or build your own?" by one sentence stating the practical test: "hold the contents list up against the AVMA checklist; if more than a few lines are missing, build or supplement." Keep "Picks by criteria" (template block) but shorten the in-body Trail Dog paragraph by removing the contents list that the card repeats (two sterile gauze dressings... forceps, saline and a Pet First Aid Handbook).

## What works well
- "The short answer" opens with a concrete scenario, states what a kit is for ("buys time", AVMA quote), gives both poison control numbers with the fee note, and says "Do not induce vomiting unless a veterinarian or poison control tells you to" in the first screen. It is accurate to the AVMA source and carries the right caution.
- The page handles the medication question honestly: it lists the items with conditions attached (charcoal, peroxide), declines to give doses, flags the Trail Dog's medications in the text and in the card's check-before-you-buy block, and cites which source says what.
- The three-layer model (cabin, car, trail), the "Pick by situation" table, the muzzle buying criteria and the pre-trip checklist are practical, specific to rental stays and not found in the competitor list in the research block.
