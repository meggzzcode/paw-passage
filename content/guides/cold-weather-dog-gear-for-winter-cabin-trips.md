Salt and ice decide whether your dog needs boots; size, age and coat decide whether it needs a layer. A thick double-coated dog on clear, dry paths can skip both, and neither the sources nor the maker pages we read give a safe temperature for your dog, so you judge by the dog.

## How cold is too cold, by dog type

The sources agree on the pattern and differ on the numbers. PetMD, written by a veterinarian, says cold should not become a problem for most dogs until below 45 F, that small, thin-coated, very young, old or sick dogs could be in danger below 32 F, and that below 20 F "all pet parents need to be aware." [[1]](https://www.petmd.com/dog/care/how-cold-too-cold-dog) A Cornell veterinarian quoted by NBC Select says at 32 F and below, limit all dogs' time outside, and below 20 F only very short bathroom breaks. [[5]](https://www.nbcnews.com/select/shopping/how-to-keep-dogs-warm-winter-rcna239641) PetSmart says under 40 F can become risky and suggests 10 to 15 minutes, supervised, below freezing. [[6]](https://www.petsmart.com/learning-center/dog-care/how-cold-is-too-cold-for-dogs) ASPCA gives no number: if it is too cold for you, it is probably too cold for your pet. [[4]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/cold-weather-safety-tips) A brisk breeze and wet fur reduce insulation, so every band feels colder.

| Dog | What the sources say, and what it suggests (our reading in italics) |
| --- | --- |
| Thick double coat (PetMD names Siberian Husky, Newfoundland, Samoyed; Cornell names Huskies and Malamutes) | Handle cold best. *Paw care first; do not clip short.* |
| Short or thin coat, lean build | ASPCA suggests a coat or sweater with a high collar for short-haired dogs; thin dogs get cold faster because body fat insulates. *Pack a layer for any walk near or below 32 F.* |
| Small or toy breed | Smaller dogs lose heat faster, and deep snow can reach the chest. *Cover belly and chest.* |
| Puppy, senior or ill dog | Regulate body temperature less well. *Use the cautious end of every number; ask your vet.* |

Cornell says full-body coverage can limit snowball buildup on a small dog's belly and chest, and PetMD says active dogs may make enough body heat to stay comfortable. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/winter-safety-tips) [[1]](https://www.petmd.com/dog/care/how-cold-too-cold-dog) At a cabin, a dog standing on a deck while you unload is not moving.

**Signs to head inside.** Cornell lists shivering, whining or barking, a tucked tail or hunched posture, lifting paws, reluctance to walk and sudden anxiety, and says frostbite most often hits ears, paws and tail tips and can take days to show. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/winter-safety-tips) PetMD says to call a vet for sluggishness, confusion, severe shivering (which may stop as hypothermia progresses) or pale, cool body parts. [[1]](https://www.petmd.com/dog/care/how-cold-too-cold-dog) This is not veterinary advice; ask your vet about a young, old or ill dog's limits.

## A coat or fleece: when it helps

Cornell says a jacket or sweater helps smaller, older or thin-coated dogs. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/winter-safety-tips) PetMD draws the line that matters: sweaters and fleeces help on mildly chilly days but "won't provide enough protection" when conditions get more extreme, where it recommends an insulated, windproof, water-resistant coat. [[1]](https://www.petmd.com/dog/care/how-cold-too-cold-dog) A fleece suits a short walk in cold, clear air; for a night-time bathroom run in wet snow it is a layer, not a shell.

**For a thin-coated dog on dry, cold walks.** If your dog shivers on the first cold lap around the cabin, a light layer is the simplest fix. The page makes no temperature, water or wind claim, so treat it as a dry-weather layer. Skip it for wet snow and for a thick double coat.

On sizing, the page contradicts itself: the size chart says size up for a relaxed fit or down for a snug one, while the fit guide says choose the smaller size. ## Boots, salt and de-icer

AKC says if you would not walk barefoot on a surface, such as a freezing sidewalk, your dog should not either, and that boots can shield paws from deicers and road salts. [[3]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/lifestyle/best-dog-boots/) PetMD says cold, packed snow between the pads, ice and road chemicals can all harm paws, and to choose boots that are waterproof, give traction and are tough. [[1]](https://www.petmd.com/dog/care/how-cold-too-cold-dog)

- **What salt does:** ASPCA lists sodium, potassium, calcium and magnesium chloride as common ice-melt ingredients, says paw contact can irritate skin and swallowing a lot can raise sodium levels and cause tremors and seizures. Dogs get it from licking paws, eating treated slush or eating melt from the bag. [[10]](https://www.aspca.org/news/keeping-pets-safe-during-winter-dangers-ice-melts)
- **"Pet-safe" is not zero risk:** ASPCA says urea and magnesium chloride products are typically safer but may still upset the stomach, and a Cornell veterinarian quoted by NBC Select agrees. [[10]](https://www.aspca.org/news/keeping-pets-safe-during-winter-dangers-ice-melts) [[5]](https://www.nbcnews.com/select/shopping/how-to-keep-dogs-warm-winter-rcna239641)
- **What to do:** wipe paws, legs and belly and limit licking until they are clean. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/winter-safety-tips) If a dog swallows ice melt or antifreeze, call a vet or [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/keeping-pets-safe-during-winter-dangers-ice-melts) Animal Poison Control right away.
- **Ask the host** what goes on the steps and which paths are treated.

**For salted paths and ice.** Ruffwear's Polar Trex boots are sold as a set of two, sized by paw width from 1.50 in (38 mm) to 3.25 in (83 mm) in 0.25 in steps. Measure across the widest part of the paw while it bears weight, measure all paws (fronts can be larger than rears) and, if between sizes, size down; the selector lets you pick front and rear sizes separately. [[11]](https://ruffwear.com/products/polar-trex-winter-dog-boots) The maker describes a waterproof, windproof softshell upper backed with fleece, a rubber outsole with winter fiberglass lugs, reflective trim and protection from "snow melt chemicals." The page gives no temperature rating and no waterproofing test. Check strap tension 15 minutes into a walk, and take the boots off at the door, since the lugs are for outdoor use only. Hand wash and air dry. Skip them if your paths are clear or your dog will not tolerate boots.

AKC's fit advice applies to any boot: let the dog sniff them with treats, start with one, then practice indoors in short sessions. Poor fit shows as paws slipping out, little traction or a boot that does not follow the paw's shape. [[3]](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/lifestyle/best-dog-boots/) Do this at home, not on the cabin porch in the dark.

## Keeping a rental clean and dry

Snow turns into water, grit and salt on the way in. Towel-dry the dog as soon as it is inside, especially the feet and between the toes, and remove snowballs from between the pads. [[4]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/cold-weather-safety-tips) Cornell adds paws, legs and belly. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/winter-safety-tips) ASPCA says to bathe as little as possible in cold spells, since washing too often can dry the skin.

Our routine, not from a source: pick one spot by the door, ideally a mat or tiled entry, and keep every wipe, towel and wet boot there. Dry the dog before it reaches a bed or couch, hang a wet fleece where it will not drip on a rug, and ask the host where wet gear may dry and whether you may run the washer. Our [cabin packing list](/guides/dog-friendly-cabin-packing-list) covers the rest of the kit and our [Christmas week guide](/guides/christmas-week-in-a-cabin-with-a-dog) covers booking terms and holiday hazards.

One small towel covers paws and legs; a big, wet-bellied dog will want a second. Skip it if you already pack a quick-dry towel.

## The car and the cabin

ASPCA and Cornell both say never to leave a dog alone in a car in cold weather, because the inside can get very cold, and a Chewy veterinarian quoted by NBC Select says not to leave a dog in a car with the heat off. [[4]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/cold-weather-safety-tips) [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/winter-safety-tips) Unload the dog first, even if the luggage takes a few trips.

Cornell says ethylene glycol antifreeze can be deadly to pets and to clean up spills around vehicles right away. [[2]](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/winter-safety-tips) [[4]](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/cold-weather-safety-tips) Check the cabin's parking area before the dog does. Our own suggestions: try the coat and boots on a short walk the day you arrive, not at dusk, and take the vest off before a nap so nothing catches. For an older dog, see our [senior dog guide](/guides/vacation-rental-with-a-senior-dog).
