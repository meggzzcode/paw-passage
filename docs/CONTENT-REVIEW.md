# Content review: free adventure pool (schema v2)

Describes only the 14 free adventures in `content/adventures/` and the aggregate method. This document is public; reviews of premium material are kept privately and are not recorded here. Library-wide counts are in `LIBRARY-INVENTORY.md`; the free visitor journey over this pool is in `INTRO-AUDIT.md`.

**Honest statement about the scores.** The 1 to 10 numbers in each adventure file are an independent reviewer's scores, not the authors' own (the authors scored about 0.8 points higher on average and were judged too generous). Publication bar (chosen by the owner on 2026-11-26): average of at least 7.0 and no criterion below 6, except that originality may be 5 for non-signature roles (a signature adventure needs 6). The editorial goal is 7.5 or more for premium adventures. Nothing has been tested with dogs or owners. The scores are editorial judgments, not customer ratings, and must never be shown or described as such. The bar is an authoring gate, not a quality guarantee.

## Method

- Every adventure is schema v2 with controlled lists for every hard-eligibility input. The build rejects any file that breaks a cross-field rule (for example a car adventure must require `car`, and a food adventure needs diet notes).
- Drafts that failed the independent re-score were deleted, not kept. Reviewers re-read steps, twist, variations, safety text and structured fields on one scale where 5 is an ordinary pet-blog suggestion, 7 is good and 8 is genuinely excellent.
- Mechanics were deliberately varied across the pool (bracket and choice, trail and find, blanket scene, photography, checklist walk, choice at forks, observe and notice, tasting contest, a dig, a guided tour) and keepsake themes were kept unique across the free cards; this is an editorial aim, not a measured guarantee.
- A "Read your dog" safety card and a standing line ("General guidance, not veterinary advice.") are shown by the app; adventures carry only specific, non-obvious safety lines.

## Research actually read

Fetched and read in full:

1. Cornell Riney Canine Health Center, "canine enrichment ideas" (riney.vet.cornell.edu). Informed: sniff-led walks where the dog chooses the pace, hidden-food and scent games, learning through positive reinforcement, supervise toys and DIY items.
2. Cornell Riney Canine Health Center, "senior dog enrichment" (vet.cornell.edu). Informed: seniors differ, keep outings short and easy and sniff-focused, scent games and simple tricks indoors, talk to your vet about safe activities.
3. VCA Animal Hospitals, enrichment and foraging toys (vcahospitals.com). Informed: start easy, durable size-appropriate items, supervise, watch for food guarding, check ingredients with your vet.
4. ASPCA, canine DIY enrichment (aspca.org). Informed: household scent games, DIY items, supervise and remove anything a dog tries to swallow.
5. Dogs Trust, enrichment activities for dogs (dogstrust.org.uk). Informed: non-toxic right-sized items, count enrichment food in daily portions, fresh water, check what is safe for your dog.

Could **not** be read, so **not cited**: the AAHA enrichment pages (the fetch returned 403). Nothing was copied from any source or from any dog-adventure book; ideas were paraphrased into original designs. Each adventure lists only the sources that actually informed it.

## Coverage and remaining concerns

- Several adventures sit between 7.0 and 7.4 and could drop under the bar with a harsher reviewer; originality (6 on most of them) and adventure factor are the weakest criteria.
- Coverage gaps, measured in `INTRO-AUDIT.md`: every single-trait profile gets a free result, but only 2 of the 14 free adventures suit children, so a few restrictive combinations (for example puppy plus adult plus senior plus a variable-energy dog plus children plus no car) return no free match and the page says so plainly. The premium library fills these gaps. No adventures were added (scope freeze).
- 12 of the 14 free cards have a cost ceiling of 0 (`LIBRARY-INVENTORY.md`: say "using things you already have", never "nothing at all"). The Taste-Off is a food adventure and is deliberately vet-deferential, with a no-food variation. The site states plainly that nothing is vet-reviewed.
- Several outdoor adventures declare crowd "some" with strangers and other dogs, so they are correctly held back from nervous dogs; the indoor and home-spot set carries those profiles.
- Content owner check (D12): confirm A Trip Down Memory Lane stays puppy-suitable with city or countryside places.
- Content has not been tested with real dogs or owners, and the scores are editorial judgments.
