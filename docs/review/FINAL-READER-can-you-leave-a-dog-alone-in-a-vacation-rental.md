# Independent reader review: can-you-leave-a-dog-alone-in-a-vacation-rental

Method: rendered at 390 and 1280 px in Playwright Chromium; first-screen and full-page screenshots viewed; text and links extracted. No horizontal overflow. 13,471 px tall at 390 (longest of the three), 9,535 px at 1280, about 2,250 words. No prior reviews read.

## First-time search visitor
- First screen: dek "Only if the house rules allow it and your dog copes with it; get the host's answer in writing before you plan a dinner out or a hike." Clear and useful. The first paragraph adds "Four of the 38 managers in our dataset wrote an alone-time rule, and no two match". Strong opening.
- Repeats of the same answer ("silence is not permission; ask in writing"): the dek, lede ("Settle it before you pay"), "The short answer" bullet 1 ("Silence is not a yes"), "Is it allowed?" ("If they are silent, you have an unanswered question, not permission"), the host-message section, and "What we could not confirm" ("Your host's rule... Ask the host"). The "ASPCA's test is whether you are back before your dog gets upset" appears in the short answer and again in "How long is too long" (and the "no standard number" point twice).
- Reading order: the visitor's likely second question ("how long can I leave my dog?") is buried near the end, below crates, cameras, "when should you not", and after three products.

## Mobile traveler
- Heavy: 13.5k px, 10 H2s, 3 product cards, 2 tables, 3 bullet lists, and a long run-on closing ("Before you book, make sure the host's reply is in writing, the host has agreed to any pet camera, your dog has stayed calmly alone before, and you have a first-night plan") that should be a short checklist or cut.
- Table "What the listing says / What to do" is useful and readable at 390 (2 columns); the "What managers say" bullets are fine. The two-column format is the right call.
- "When should you not leave your dog?" has nested bullets including "Heat:" and "Cold:" about parked cars. For a vacation-rental alone-time question this is off-axis padding: "the ASPCA says a car can hold the cold, and pets need a warm place to sleep, off the floor and away from drafts" is generic, and "Do not solve 'dogs not allowed on the trail' by leaving the dog in the car" is the only tied-in line. The "Known separation anxiety" bullet runs about 7 lines on mobile.
- "Can I leave a pet camera on?" sits on a thin basis: the Airbnb policy is about hosts' cameras; the page admits "The page does not address guests' own devices". The answer "Not without asking" is sensible, but it takes about 12 lines to arrive at "ask the host".

## Skeptical reader
- Evidence is honest and labelled ("These sources are general pet guidance, not rental rules", "one writer's summary, not a study", "a convenience sample"). Four managers is a very small sample; the page says so.
- Products: three cards (iCrate, MyPet gate, KONG), about a third of the mobile page.
  - iCrate card and gate card each follow a text intro that gives the reason ("If the rule is 'crated when alone'..."; "If the crate stresses your dog..."). These are conditional and have skip-conditions ("skip this if the host provides a crate", "buy nothing for this" for the no-unattended case). That is the best commercial pattern on these three pages. But the iCrate sentence says the maker's page "gives no weights or folded dimensions, so check the folded size... before you buy". That is useful honesty but weakens the recommendation to a skeptic.
  - KONG card, labelled "SUPERVISED USE ONLY: PRACTICE AT HOME", sits in a guide about leaving a dog alone, while the text says "Do not leave one with your dog alone unless your vet agrees". A skeptical reader sees a product that is explicitly not the answer to the page's question. The card's own note repeats it ("it is not an unattended-time answer"). Low relevance; remove or fold into text.
  - Also an inline Amazon link on each product name in prose ("MidWest iCrate", "KONG Classic") in addition to the card button, so each product has two affiliate links. Not per-card labels, but doubles the commercial surface.
- Disclosure: one top notice with the Amazon Associate sentence; links rel=sponsored. OK. Same "As an Amazon Associate I earn" first-person mismatch.
- Sourcing: inline publisher links (ASPCA about 7 times, Afar twice) and 19-item Sources. The ASPCA link repeated as a bare "ASPCA" so many times is noise.

## Factual and safety points
- Mostly conservative: no unattended in storms, anxious dogs, hot/cold, "no unattended pets" rule. Good.
- The "about four hours" figure from Psychology Today is flagged as one writer's summary. It is the only number a reader will remember; it could be taken as permission by an anxious-dog owner, though it follows "no agreed number".
- Pressure-mounted gate caution "never use a tension-held gate at the top of stairs" is quoted from the maker; good.
- "Cold: ASPCA says a car can hold the cold" is odd wording.

## Voice / padding
- Natural voice, mostly plain. Stock phrases: "Settle it before you pay, not on the afternoon of the dinner reservation" (fine), "A new place changes things". Section "What about noise and neighbors?" is partly a link hub (reactive-dog guide, damage-charge guide). "Can I use a crate, and what about the furniture?" mixes crate, furniture, and the product intros.
- Perceived length: dominated by the longest of the three guides; the index card for this guide says "11 min read" but the article header says "8 min read", an inconsistency a reader can see.

## Verdict: READY WITH MINOR FIXES
1. Cut the KONG card: the page itself says it is not the answer; keep one clause of text at most.
2. Trim length by about 20%: shorten the "Heat/Cold" car bullets and the separation-anxiety bullet; collapse the camera section to 3 sentences plus the 3 bullets.
3. Remove repeated "silence is not permission" and "ASPCA's test/no standard number" statements (short answer vs "How long is too long").
4. Reconcile reading time: header "8 min read" vs index card "11 min read".
5. Drop the duplicate inline Amazon links on product names where a card follows immediately; keep the cards.
6. Turn the long "Before you book, make sure..." sentence into a 4-item list or cut it.
7. Consider moving "How long is too long" higher, since it is the second question a searcher has.
